Already decided you'd take a position? Size it.

The rest of this page is a separate tool for a separate moment — after you've weighed the odds above, working out how many shares a specific risk budget actually buys. Start by setting a portfolio size and how much of it you'd accept losing on this one position, then pick which published level would tell you you were wrong.

This single choice drives everything below it. A level close to today's price means a small loss per share, so the arithmetic allows more shares — and it also means ordinary noise can reach it. A level far away means the opposite. Every option here is a number already published on this name's study page; the percentage is its distance from the last close.

Nothing is stored or sent anywhere — the arithmetic runs in your browser.

What this can't tell you

The arithmetic assumes you can leave at the level you chose. Real exits gap through levels, especially on thin EGX small-caps and around results — a position sized to lose 2% can lose considerably more if the price opens below your level rather than trading down to it. Prices here are the last close in each study, not live quotes, and the study date is shown above; anything that has happened since is not in these numbers.

Sizing arithmetic is also not a view. A tight level produces a large share count on this page whether or not the case is any good — the calculation cannot distinguish a well-founded idea from a poor one, and a bigger number here is not more confidence. The fundamental work that would tell you whether the case holds is in the study itself, and the record of how often our published ranges have actually contained the price is in the public ledger, right and wrong alike.
