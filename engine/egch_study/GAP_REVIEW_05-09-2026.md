# EGCH — valuation gap review, 5 September 2026

**Trigger.** [R-GAP-01]. The answer is **TWO-SIDED** and both branches breach the ten per
cent trigger below the price:

| branch | value | vs spot 14.41 |
|---|---:|---:|
| cash-flow lens, ANNA capital programme **carried through** | **EGP 3.8967** | **−73.0%** |
| cash-flow lens, ANNA capital programme **stopped** | **EGP 7.8807** | **−45.3%** |

Spot is the close on the Egyptian Exchange on 3 September 2026, supplied by the principal
and committed at `engine/prices/SUPPLIED_03-09-2026.json`. The price has not moved since
the previous edition was struck, so this review restates the same disagreement at a
different answer rather than the other way round.

**This edition supersedes the review of 3 September, which audited 2.3109 and 6.2591 at a
gap of −84.0%.** One correction landed between them, in two steps, and it is the largest
single move this study has taken: **+68.6% on the carried-through branch and +25.9% on the
stopped one.** Both remain far below the market, so **the study stays held either way** and
nothing about its publication status changes.

## What moved, and what did not

**The terminal is rebuilt on the sanctioned construction, and the age it rests on is
MEASURED rather than assumed.** The retired identity reinvested a share of profit set by
growth over an assumed 18% return on capital. Read as a capital programme that rebuilds the
whole asset base every 1/g years — 14.3 at a 7% terminal — which is a fact about the pound
rather than about a urea plant. The study had already written that diagnosis down in its own
delivered words, and kept the number because it looked conservative. The terminal now
charges what keeping the plant intact costs at today's prices: the book depreciation charge
escalated over the average age of the base.

**That age is 4.45 years**, from accumulated depreciation of EGP 3,435.300mn over the year's
own charge of EGP 771.213mn — an identity, labelled as derived, read by OCR off the rendered
pixels of note 6 because the filing carries a 48-character text layer across 48 pages, and
footing four ways. Against it, half of the 22.07-year replacement cycle the same note implies
would put the base at 11.04 years. **The base is a quarter worn, not half**, and only EGP
220.5mn of it — 1.3% of the depreciable cost — is fully depreciated and still in production.
What the assumption would have cost is published as the study's largest priced alternative:
**EGP 2.16 a share.**

**And the project's depreciation was charged twice.** The explicit window depreciates the new
complex as it is spent, so the last explicit year already carried the charge on the part in
service; the terminal then charged the whole plant on top — EGP 482mn a year of depreciation
on a plant that appears once in the accounts. Under the retired construction that only
depressed profit. Under the sanctioned one, book depreciation is **also the base of the
replacement charge**, so it was depressing the value twice over, which is why the defect
became worth finding only after the first correction was made. **The model already knew how
to do this**: the programme-stopped branch strips exactly that charge before it grosses the
year, and that branch was never wrong. Corrected, the two branches carry the identical
year-five formula and differ only in the project's own revenue, profit and depreciation —
which is what the difference between them is supposed to be.

**BOTH CORRECTIONS MOVE THE ANSWER TOWARD THE PRICE, AND NEITHER WAS MADE FOR THAT REASON.**
The first was required by a standing rule and was priced before it was built. The second was
found by reading the construction the first one forced open. A correction that happens to
narrow a gap is not evidence about the gap; the test is whether it would have been made if
the price had been the other side, and both of these would.

Every disclosed period is read, and this pass added one that had been read but not fully
mined: **note 6 of the FY2024/25 audited statements**, the fixed-asset register. Its cost
column, accumulated column, land line and disclosure of assets fully depreciated and still in
production are now committed inputs rather than figures used once and discarded. The
extraction foots four ways — the cost movement table, the accumulated-depreciation movement
table, cost less accumulated against the balance sheet, and the year's charge against the
figure this register already carried from an earlier reading.

The nine-month reviewed statements to 31 March 2026 remain the balance-sheet date the bridge
stands on. No period disclosed since has gone unread.

**One thing this pass did NOT settle and it is named rather than left implicit.** Two
registered inputs — gross fixed assets at cost and accumulated depreciation — are sourced to
the 31 March 2026 interim note 6 and carry figures identical to the FY2024/25 audited note.
That is plausible, because the new complex sits in construction in progress rather than in
fixed assets, but it has not been confirmed against the interim's own page: the interim
carries a 37-character text layer and confirming it needs an OCR pass that was started and
not finished in this session. The terminal does not depend on it — the age and the life are
sourced explicitly to the audited note and use that note's own figures — but the labelling
question is real and is registered here for the next pass rather than being written off.

## 3. MACRO COHERENCE

Unchanged, and it is the heading a terminal rebuild most easily breaks. Terminal growth is
7.00%, the central bank's published target, and it is the **same figure** that sits inside the
terminal risk-free rate — so terminal growth is inflation with zero real growth, and the
model says so in real terms rather than leaving a reader to compute it. The maintenance
escalator uses that same inflation. The currency path derives from the same ladder on the
purchasing-power identity.

**The rebuild made one macro relation testable that was not testable before.** Real growth
now costs the capital it consumes, and this study's own driver test measures what a point of
it is worth: **negative**. At a capital intensity of roughly three pounds per pound of
revenue and a terminal cost of capital near 19%, real growth at this company does not pay for
itself. The model takes none, so the term is inert at the central — but it is now a *stated*
zero rather than an assumption hidden inside a reinvestment rate.

## 4. DISCOUNT RATE

Unchanged. Operations are discounted on the glide from 25.8% to a terminal 18.59%; the cash
is not charged for twice — this company is heavily net *debt*, not net cash, so the
construction the two-sided error arises from does not apply here. Country risk enters once,
through the premium inside the equity risk premium, against a risk-free rate normalised by
Egypt's own default spread. Both premium bases are published and the swap basis is named
central.

**The reverse read is the sharpest thing under this heading and it still cuts against the
study, not for it.** At EGP 14.41 the price implies a flat nominal Egyptian-pound discount
rate of **13.0%** on the carried-through branch and **14.3%** on the stopped one. Egypt's own
ten-year sovereign yield is 23.0%. **The price is paying a rate well below what the
government of Egypt pays to borrow** — less than half of it on the primary branch. That is
not a believable number, and under [R-GAP-02] a reverse read landing on an *unbelievable*
figure is the one condition that does not argue the study down. It argues that the
disagreement is real, and it is why this study is held rather than corrected further.

## 7. CLAIMS AGAINST THE RECORD

The contested judgement is **binary and its two answers straddle the interesting range**, so
it is published as two branches and never averaged: is the ANNA capital programme carried
through, or stopped? The gap between them is **EGP 3.98 a share**.

The study's stated finding — that on the disclosed bank-approved cost and the derived
nameplate the programme does not earn the capital sunk into it, so stopping is worth more
than finishing — is recomputed after the rebuild and **still holds**, by a wider margin in
absolute pounds and a narrower one in proportion.

Recomputed in this pass: sustainable return on equity **6.9%** against a cost of equity of
**29.3%**, implying a justified price-to-book of 0.00x where the market pays **1.77x**.

**One claim in the delivered document was removed rather than restated.** The cost-of-capital
section carried a paragraph naming the terminal reinvestment rate as "the largest unsourced
input left in the model" and pricing the alternative it declined. That input no longer exists:
under the sanctioned construction there is no reinvestment rate to contest. The paragraph is
replaced by one that names what actually carries the terminal now — the measured age — and
prices the assumption that would replace it. **The study's own alternatives gate caught the
dead row the moment it scored zero**, which is what that gate is for.

## 8. MULTIPLE CROSS-CHECK

| | carried through (3.90) | stopped (7.88) | at the market (14.41) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| equity value, EGP mn | 7,741 | 15,656 | **28,627** |
| implied enterprise value, EGP mn | 14,236 | 22,150 | **38,659** |
| price to disclosed book (8.158/share) | 0.48x | 0.97x | **1.77x** |
| enterprise value / terminal profit before interest and tax | **2.62x** | **4.11x** | **7.11x** |

**The earnings multiple is where the gap actually is, and the rebuild narrowed it without
closing it.** The carried-through branch moved from 2.25x forward operating profit to 2.62x
against a market at 7.11x. Two and a half times forward operating profit for a nitrogen
producer is still not an ordinary valuation; seven times is.

**What that arithmetic rules out, by arithmetic rather than by argument:**

- **It is not the bridge.** The investment property and the portfolio at fair value through
  other comprehensive income are carried at their disclosed amounts and total EGP 3,538mn.
  Doubling both would move the answer by less than EGP 1.80 a share against a gap of EGP 10.51.
- **It is no longer the terminal charge.** That was the candidate this pass tested, and it was
  worth EGP 1.59 a share of the gap — real, and a sixth of what is outstanding. Closing the
  rest on the terminal alone would need a terminal several times larger again, which no
  maintenance assumption reaches: even charging **nothing at all** for keeping the plant
  intact takes the carried-through branch only to EGP 7.75, still 46% below the price.
- **It is not the discount rate in any believable range**, per heading 4: the price is paying
  less than the sovereign.

**So the residual disagreement is in the operating build, in what the new complex will earn,
or in something the market believes that this model does not — and the honest position is
that this study does not know which.** It says so, and it stays held.
