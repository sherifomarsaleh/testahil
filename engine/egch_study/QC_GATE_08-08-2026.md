## Third pass — the capital-expenditure record, and a driver this study had asserted

A reader asked three questions the study could not answer from its own pages: is the capital
expenditure a one-off bulk, does it repeat, and what is the historical pattern. The third
was answerable directly from the cash-flow statements already in the build path, and had
not been built. It is now, and it changed the published answer.

The investing section carries one line — payments to acquire fixed assets, projects under
construction. It has two clean pre-project years:

| Year | Capex paid (EGP m) | Revenue (EGP m) | Of revenue |
|---|---|---|---|
| FY2021/22 | 80.8 | 4,441 | 1.82% |
| FY2022/23 | 42.5 | 6,612 | 0.64% |
| FY2023/24 | 2,396.6 | 6,532 | 36.69% |
| FY2024/25 | 1,545.1 | 8,603 | 17.96% |
| 9M FY2025/26 | 1,949.1 | 7,315 | 26.65% |

Two drivers were re-anchored on this evidence:

- **Maintenance capital expenditure** was 3.0% of revenue, set as "the mature-plant
  standard" on the assertion that the company's own observed spending was abnormally low.
  The pooled pre-project rate is **1.12%**. The house standard was 2.7 times anything this
  company has ever paid to keep this plant running, and no disclosure supported it. This is
  the same failure the cost-stack rule was adopted after — a house macro proxy standing in
  for a disclosed, dated figure — on a different line. The replacement-rate framing (gross
  fixed assets at the disclosed 3.95% machinery rate, 6.10% of revenue) is now published as
  the alternative and the downside, never averaged in.
- **The project spending path** opened at EGP 3,000m a year, above anything the company has
  spent. It now opens at the observed run rate — the nine-month actual extended to a full
  year, EGP 2,599m — and the remaining years still complete the approved cost inside the
  window, because the terminal year only earns if the plant is finished.

Two further questions the evidence answers: the bulk **is** one-off (the forecast spends
EGP 14,688m against EGP 14,688m still to spend, then stops), and it does **not** repeat
inside any horizon the study can see (the disclosed machinery depreciation rate of 3.95%
implies a ~25-year replacement cycle).

**Effect on the published answer: the carried-through case moves from EGP −1.30 to EGP
−0.79 a share, and the stopped case from EGP 2.20 to EGP 2.49.** The carried-through span
is now EGP −2.87 to +1.47, so its sign is no longer robust — which the study now says in
those words rather than leaving a reader to infer it.

Two new priced alternatives were added with it: the replacement-rate maintenance framing
(EGP −1.57) and the terminal reinvestment rate (EGP −0.22 at a 30% return on capital). The
terminal reinvestment rate is now identified in the caveats as the largest unsourced input
left in the model; the conservative reading is kept and the alternative is priced rather
than adopted.

## What the second pass changed, and the two numbers that moved

Sourcing `compute.py`'s remaining literals to the register surfaced two genuine
inconsistencies, both now resolved in favour of the register:

- **FY2024/25 depreciation** was taken from the note 6 fixed-asset register (EGP 771.2m)
  while the two earlier years were taken from the note 21 cost-of-sales line. The three
  years now use note 21 throughout (EGP 776.5m for FY2024/25), which is the consistent
  basis. FY2024/25 operating profit before depreciation rises by EGP 5.3m, 0.2%.
- **Holding-company debt at 31 March 2026** was typed as EGP 45.905m against the register's
  EGP 45.955m. The register figure is used.

Together with a rounding correction to the other-materials driver, these move the
carried-through answer from EGP −1.303 to EGP −1.298 and the stopped answer from EGP 2.194
to EGP 2.199 — neither changes a published two-decimal figure except the stopped case, which
now reads 2.20. The contested judgement remains EGP 3.50 a share. The relative-multiple lens
moves from 6.91 to 6.92 and the field's upper bound with it.

Also added in this pass: `alternatives.py`, which prices every contested construction by
re-running the model through the same case machinery with one component moved; `run_all.py`,
which fixes the build order that the new dependencies made load-bearing; and builder-assigned
figure and table numbering.

The underlying analysis did not change and did not need to: the filings are the same
documents, verified byte-identical, and the calibration, beta and cost-of-capital gates all
passed the first time.
