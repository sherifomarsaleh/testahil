# MODON — gap review, 4 September 2026

**AUDITED CENTRAL: 3.5423** — the cash-flow lens, AED per share.
**AUDITED GAP: +25.2%** against the price this study is struck at, AED 2.83 (7 August 2026).

This fires the ABOVE-price half of the audit trigger. That half is deliberately not
matched by a publication block — the block is one-sided below the price — so this is the
region where an over-optimistic answer gets no automatic hold and nothing else supplies
one. The review is therefore the only thing standing here, and it is written that way.

## The finding that comes before the eight headings

**The answer did not move because the cash flows moved. It moved because a discount
stopped being applied.**

The previous edition published AED 2.4952, a typed 40/20/20/20 blend of four lenses,
−11.8% against the price. This edition publishes the cash-flow lens itself at AED 3.5423,
+25.2%. Not one forecast line changed between them: not a revenue path, not a margin, not
the backlog, not the cost of capital.

Those weights were not only a view about method. The study's own registered justification
says the three market lenses at 60% were "how float/governance friction is priced", and
that a 30% haircut on top "would double-count it". The arithmetic agrees to within half a
point: the blend cost **29.6%** of the cash-flow lens.

So **the entire swing from −11.8% to +25.2% is that friction ceasing to be priced.** It is
not a new view about the company. Whether a float-and-governance discount belongs — this
is a 22% free float with a dominant sovereign shareholder — is a real question this
edition does not answer, and section 1.5 says so rather than settling it inside a set of
weights.

## 1. LATEST FILINGS

Every disclosed period is read: audited statements FY2023 (Q Holding perimeter), FY2024
and FY2025, the reviewed interim statements to **30 June 2026**, the H1-2026 results
release of 29 July 2026 and the FY2025 results release. The valuation is struck on the
30-June-2026 reviewed balance sheet.

The one filing failure in this study's history is recorded rather than buried: revision 1
struck its development drivers on 31-December-2025 disclosures while the H1-2026 release
had superseded them. That was caught by external audit and corrected in revision 2. No
unread filing sits behind the current gap.

## 2. BASE YEAR

FY2026 is a **half-year stub** and is built as one: `yrfrac` 0.5, with the first half the
disclosed actual and only H2 forecast. Development revenue converts a disclosed backlog at
a rate anchored on the pace actually recognised in the half, rather than on an assumption
about it.

Nothing is annualised. The stub is worth naming because it makes the FY2026 multiple
meaningless as a comparison — see heading 8, where the first FULL year is used instead.

## 3. MACRO COHERENCE

This study commits **no macro record**, so its inflation, currency and rate paths are not
held to the house path by anything. That is a real gap and it is stated rather than
implied: MODON is on the macro ratchet with fifteen other names.

What can be checked from what it does commit: the terminal cost of capital (11.92%) sits
ABOVE the explicit-window rate (11.19%), which is the correct direction for a pegged
market only if the leverage moves — and here it does, because the terminal capital
structure is derived from the model's own FY2030E balance sheet rather than assumed. The
Egypt exposure carries its own stress (17.1% of value, at a 15.27% cost of equity), which
is priced separately rather than blended into one rate.

## 4. DISCOUNT RATE

The cost of equity is 12.56% on a beta of 1.75 — the beta re-derived in revision 3 against
the official FTSE ADX General index, Dimson-corrected for thin trading, replacing a
composite built from this study's own price library. That correction moved the answer
DOWN, and the study reports it rather than absorbing it.

**The reverse read is the sharpest thing under this heading and it cuts against the
premium.** The price is consistent with this study's own model at a cost of equity
**2.69 percentage points higher** than the one adopted. That is not an absurd number — it
is roughly what the Egypt stress case already applies — and a reverse read landing on a
believable figure is evidence against the disagreement, not for it. The +25% is not
supported by the discount rate being obviously wrong.

## 5. TERMINAL

**The terminal is 68.7% of enterprise value**, which is high, and this study says so in the
same breath as noting that the working-capital honesty of revision 2 made that share
larger rather than smaller. A quarter of the answer sitting above the price while
two-thirds of it sits beyond the explicit window is where scepticism belongs, and it is
named here rather than left to a reader to notice.

This terminal has **not** been rebuilt on the sanctioned module: MODON carries the retired
`g/ROIC` reinvestment construction and sits on that ratchet. Its implied replacement cycle
is 40 years at a 2.5% terminal — a fact about the dirham rather than about a building.
Correcting it is a separate pass and its direction cannot be predicted from the ratio
alone; the one name measured so far moved UP.

## 6. BALANCE SHEET

The bridge stands on the **30-June-2026 reviewed** balance sheet, which is the latest
disclosed. It uses the company's own available-cash definition (net DEBT of AED 912mn)
rather than the strict all-cash basis (net CASH of AED 2,762mn) — the more conservative of
the two, and the study states both rather than choosing silently.

MODON commits **no bridge record**, so the minority basis, the cash treatment and the
line-by-line footing are not held to [R-BRIDGE-01] by anything. It is on that ratchet.

## 7. CLAIMS AGAINST THE RECORD

No absolute claim about the company's filed record survives in the delivered documents.
The typed figures the prose check found — the L'imad stake at 84.75%, the release's
adjusted-EBITDA margin at 35.2%, a superseded cost of equity and a superseded central —
are now four-field inputs rather than literals in a builder, so each is checkable against
its source. The release margin is registered rather than reconstructed, because 4,900 over
this study's registered revenue of 13,828.9 gives 35.4% and not the 35.2% the release
prints: the company computes it on a base this study does not carry, and that is recorded
rather than papered over.

## 8. MULTIPLE CROSS-CHECK

The FY2026 stub is excluded as a comparison for the reason given under heading 2. On the
first full forecast year and on the last audited one:

| Basis | At the fair value | At the price |
|---|---|---|
| EV / FY2027E EBITDA | 7.33x | 5.92x |
| EV / FY2028E EBITDA | 6.06x | 4.89x |
| EV / FY2025A EBITDA | 11.58x | — |
| Equity / FY2025A attributable profit | **14.40x** | 11.51x |

The peer set trades on attributable earnings at Aldar 8.10x, Emaar 5.78x and Emaar
Development 4.73x; Aldar's enterprise-to-EBITDA is 5.46x.

**This is the heading that most argues against the premium.** At the fair value MODON
implies 14.40x trailing attributable earnings, ABOVE every peer — and the price already
implies 11.51x, itself above every peer. The forward multiples are gentler because the
backlog converts hard, and 7.33x on FY2027E against Aldar's 5.46x on a trailing basis is
not an unreasonable place to be for a company growing into a disclosed order book. But a
trailing multiple above the whole peer set at the fair value is exactly the shape a defect
makes, and it is not explained away here.

---

## VERDICT

**The answer is not changed, and the reason it is not changed is not that it was
vindicated.** Three of the eight headings cut against a +25% premium:

- the reverse read says the price sits within 2.69 percentage points of cost of equity of
  this model — a believable distance, not an absurd one;
- the terminal carries 68.7% of the value on a construction this house has retired;
- the implied trailing earnings multiple sits above every peer at the fair value.

And the single largest fact is the one before the headings: **the whole move from −11.8%
to +25.2% is a float-and-governance discount ceasing to be applied**, not a change of view
about the business. Removing it from the weights was right — a discount worth a third of
the value does not belong hidden in a blend where a reader cannot disagree with it — but
removing it from the weights is not the same as deciding it should not exist.

**What this review does NOT do is put it back.** Re-applying a ~30% haircut now, in the
same pass that removed it, would land the answer at almost exactly the old blend and would
be fitting the number to where it used to be. The judgement belongs in its own edition,
published as a contested judgement with both framings valued, where the sign test can see
it — which is what [R-ENF-05] exists for and what the weights were preventing.

MODON is **held from publication regardless**: the method has not cleared its Phase 1
acceptance criteria. Nothing here reaches a reader before that judgement is made.
