# MODON — gap review, 4 September 2026

**AUDITED CENTRAL: 3.8583** — the cash-flow lens, AED per share.
**AUDITED GAP: +36.3%** against the latest known price, AED 2.83 (3 September 2026 close,
supplied; the same close the study is struck at).

This fires the ABOVE-price half of the audit trigger. That half is deliberately not
matched by a publication block — the block is one-sided below the price — so this is the
region where an over-optimistic answer gets no automatic hold and nothing else supplies
one. The review is therefore the only thing standing here, and it is written that way.

## The finding that comes before the eight headings

**Two separate things stopped being applied, and neither of them was a view about the
business.**

The first was a typed 40/20/20/20 blend of four lenses, published at AED 2.4952, −11.8%
against the price. Retiring it published the cash-flow lens itself at AED 3.5423, +25.2%.
Not one forecast line changed. The study's own registered justification had said the
three market lenses at 60% were "how float/governance friction is priced" and that a 30%
haircut on top "would double-count it" — and the arithmetic agreed to within half a point:
the blend cost **29.6%** of the cash-flow lens.

The second was the terminal construction, and it is worth stating in full because the
direction is the opposite of what anyone would guess. The retired terminal charged growth
by assuming the entire capital base had to be rebuilt every **50 years** — the reciprocal
of the growth rate, which is a fact about the dirham and not about a building. At this
company's assumed terminal return of 8.5% against a terminal cost of capital of 11.92%,
that charge cost more than the growth was worth, and **this study carried the only
terminal in the book worth less than not investing at all**: 10.7% below its own no-growth
perpetuity. Rebuilding it on what replacing the assets actually costs, and storing growth
as a real rate on the house inflation path rather than as a typed nominal one, took the
central to AED 3.8583.

**The lever that RAISED the value did so by CUTTING the growth rate**, from a typed 2.5%
to a derived 2.0%, and that is the sharpest available evidence that the old construction
was wrong rather than merely conservative. A model in which less growth is worth more is
not being cautious; it is charging for something at a price nobody set.

Whether a float-and-governance discount belongs — this is a 22% free float with a dominant
sovereign shareholder — is a real question this edition still does not answer, and section
1.5 says so rather than settling it inside a set of weights.

## 1. LATEST FILINGS

Every disclosed period is read: audited statements FY2023 (Q Holding perimeter), FY2024
and FY2025, the reviewed interim statements to **30 June 2026**, the H1-2026 results
release of 29 July 2026 and the FY2025 results release. The valuation is struck on the

The one filing failure in this study's history is recorded rather than buried: revision 1

unread filing sits behind the current gap.

## 2. BASE YEAR

FY2026 is a **half-year stub** and is built as one: the first half is the disclosed actual
and only the second half is forecast. Development revenue converts a disclosed backlog at
a rate anchored on the pace actually recognised in the half, rather than on an assumption
about it.

Nothing is annualised. The stub is worth naming because it makes the FY2026 multiple
meaningless as a comparison — see heading 8, where the first FULL year is used instead.

## 3. MACRO COHERENCE

The terminal growth rate is now the house one: a **stated real growth of zero** on the
**2.0%** long-run inflation the UAE path carries, which recomputes to a nominal 2.0%. It
is no longer a number typed into this study. That path is sourced — the IMF's own
published series for the United Arab Emirates prints 2.0% flat from 2027 onward, so no
step in it is interpolated and none of it is a house view.

**What is still open is stated rather than implied.** This study commits no full macro
record, so its risk-free rate, its currency treatment and its cost escalators are not held
to that path by anything; MODON remains on that ratchet with fifteen other names. The one
number that can be compared today does not agree: the study's normalised risk-free rate is
**4.06%** against a **3.98%** the path derives from its own terminal inflation and
real-rate convention — 8 basis points apart, and 8 basis points is worth **+0.79%** on this
answer. It is not taken in this pass, for a reason that is about scope and not about
convenience: the UAE is a pegged market, where whether a terminal risk-free rate should be
derived at all is an open question across every name in that market, and one other UAE
study fails the same test today by 75 basis points. Settling it on one name would be a
per-name exception to a question that is not per-name.

The direction is disclosed too: taking it would move the answer **further from** the price,
not toward it.

## 4. DISCOUNT RATE

The cost of equity is 12.56% on a beta of 1.75 — re-derived in revision 3 against the
official FTSE ADX General index, Dimson-corrected for thin trading, replacing a composite
built from this study's own price library. That correction moved the answer DOWN, and the
study reports it rather than absorbing it.

**The reverse read is the sharpest thing under this heading and it cuts against the
premium.** The price is consistent with this study's own model at a cost of equity of
**16.43%**, which is **3.87 percentage points** above the one adopted. That is a wider gap
than the previous edition carried, and it is still not an absurd number — it is comparable
to what the Egypt stress case applies. A reverse read landing on a believable figure is
evidence against the disagreement, not for it. The +36% is not supported by the discount
rate being obviously wrong, and this heading is the one that most argues the answer is
still too high.

## 5. TERMINAL

**The terminal is 71.3% of enterprise value**, up from 68.7%, and that share rising is the
honest consequence of the rebuild rather than a separate concern: the correction raised
the terminal without touching the explicit window. A share this high is where scepticism
belongs, and it is named here rather than left to a reader to notice.

What the terminal now charges is capital MAINTENANCE at what replacement actually costs.
Terminal operating profit after tax is AED 8,333mn; the AED 810mn of book depreciation is
added back because that profit is already struck after it, and against it the model charges
AED 1,329mn — the same depreciation escalated over half the **50-year** life the company
discloses for its longest-lived assets. Inflation on the AED 37.8bn of working capital a
developer carries takes a further AED 757mn, and real growth is charged nothing because
none is assumed. That leaves AED 7,057mn, **84.7%** of terminal profit, which is a payout a
going concern can actually sustain.

**Three choices here are conservative and are named as such.** The life taken is the
LONGEST the company discloses, which on this basis charges the MOST maintenance — reading
it at 25 years instead would raise the answer by about 3%. The working-capital charge is
real and large; a terminal that omitted it, as several constructions do, would have
flattered this one by about eight points. And real growth is zero, so the model pays
nothing for growth and receives none.

The terminal now sits **3.8% above** the no-growth perpetuity at book depreciation rather
than 10.7% below it. That marker is a diagnostic and not a bound — it assumes a maintenance
charge the company does not actually face — but a terminal below it was the signature of
the defect, and it is gone.

## 6. BALANCE SHEET

The bridge stands on the **30-June-2026 reviewed** balance sheet, which is the latest
disclosed. It uses the company's own available-cash definition (net DEBT of AED 912mn)
rather than the strict all-cash basis (net CASH of AED 2,762mn) — the more conservative of
the two, and the study states both rather than choosing silently.

MODON commits **no bridge record**, so the minority basis, the cash treatment and the
line-by-line footing are not held to that standard by anything. It is on that ratchet, and
that is unchanged by this pass.

## 7. CLAIMS AGAINST THE RECORD

No absolute claim about the company's filed record survives in the delivered documents.
Every percentage and multiple in both documents reconciles to a model output — 369 figures,
none unmatched. Two claims the previous edition made are now WITHDRAWN because the rebuild
falsified them, and they are named rather than quietly dropped: the figure caption asserting

growing into a disclosed order book. But a trailing multiple at nearly twice the best peer
is exactly the shape a defect makes, and it is not explained away here.

## VERDICT

**The answer is not changed, and the reason it is not changed is not that it was
vindicated.** Three of the eight headings cut against a +36% premium, and two of them cut
harder than they did at +25%:

- the reverse read now says the price sits 3.87 percentage points of cost of equity away
  from this model — a wider disagreement, and still a believable number rather than an
  absurd one;
- the terminal carries 71.3% of the value, more than before;
- the implied trailing earnings multiple sits at nearly twice the best peer.

What the rebuild removed was a defect, and the evidence for that is arithmetic rather than
judgement: a construction in which cutting the growth rate raises the value is charging for
growth at a price nobody set, and the correction is one this house is applying to every
name rather than to this one. What the rebuild did NOT do is answer the question the
headings keep returning to, which is whether a 22% free float under a dominant sovereign
shareholder should carry a discount at all.

**What this review does NOT do is put that discount back.** Re-applying a haircut now, in
the same programme that removed it, would land the answer near the old blend and would be
fitting the number to where it used to be. The judgement belongs in its own edition,
published as a contested judgement with both framings valued, where the sign test can see
it — which is what the output records exist for and what the weights were preventing.

MODON is **held from publication regardless**: the method has not cleared its Phase 1
acceptance criteria. Nothing here reaches a reader before that judgement is made.
