**Why the proxy erred, and in which direction.** β = corr × (σ_stock / σ_market). The official
index ran 11.5% annualised volatility over the common 255 weeks against the composite's 14.9% — a
factor of 1.30 — at a similar correlation (0.298 vs 0.343). A better-diversified benchmark has a
smaller denominator, so the true regressor RAISES beta. Beta moves 60% of the weighted central,
not 40%, because the book lens runs through Ke as well (`pb_just = (ROE_sust − g)/(Ke − g)`).

**Window selection.** The standing hierarchy takes the longest window up to 5 years that clears the
usability gate. 5-year β 1.278 (SE 0.258, R² 0.089, n 255) PASSES and is adopted. The shorter windows
read HIGHER — 3-year 1.800 (R² 0.258), 2-year 1.581 (R² 0.306) — so the adopted figure is the
friendliest of the three and the residual risk on this input runs against value. Stated in the study.

**A disclosed band that did not bracket the answer.** Revision 2 sensitised beta 0.8–1.2. The true
value, 1.278, sits OUTSIDE that band. Beta strips are now centred on the adopted estimate in steps of
one standard error (0.76 → 1.79), so the published range is a property of the measurement.

**A proxy-based inference that was real, robust and wrong.** Before the official series arrived, a
turnover-weighted composite (built to approximate cap weighting) read β 0.842 and implied a central of
3.78. A paired block bootstrap confirmed the difference from equal-weighting excluded zero at every
block size {2,3,4}. It was statistically robust and pointed the opposite way from the truth. Recorded
in the bibliography's negative-results table and adopted as a standing rule.
