# THREE INTERLOCKING MECHANISMS MAKE THIS BOOK STRUCTURALLY CONSERVATIVE

**Measured 18 September 2026**, on the principal's challenge — repeated — that the published
numbers are "way way too conservative". **They are.** Not as a matter of temperament: as three
construction choices, each defensible in isolation, none disclosed as a direction, and they
compound because each one justifies the next.

None of this is implemented. All three are candidate levers for the promotion sequence.

---

## MECHANISM 1 — A "NO FORECAST" CONVENTION THAT IS A REAL-TERMS PRICE FORECAST

Four studies — **ADNOCDRILL, AMOC, EGCH, GBCO** — hold a traded price flat in NOMINAL dollars,
stating in the cell itself that *"no forecast of a traded commodity price is defensible."*

**Flat in nominal dollars is not the absence of a forecast; it is a forecast of a permanent
real decline at US inflation.** −11.6% over five years at the house's own 2.5%, and falling for
ever in a terminal that inherits the year-five level. The view-free choice is flat in REAL
terms.

**It is asymmetric**, because in every one of these models the costs escalate at FULL domestic
inflation while revenue rides the currency path:

| | revenue grows at | cost grows at | wedge |
|---|---|---|---|
| AMOC | (1+i_EG)/1.025 | (1+i_EG) | **−2.5pp a year** |
| EGCH | currency path only ($530 flat) | (1+i_EG) | **−2.5pp a year** |

**Priced by two independent auditors: AMOC 11.40 → 13.44 (+17.8%); EGCH 4.04 → 6.82 (+68.7%).**
AMOC's corrected value lands within 0.5% of its traded price.

## MECHANISM 3 — THE TERMINAL CAPITAL BASIS, WHICH IS WHAT MAKES MECHANISM 2 LOOK JUSTIFIED

**This is the largest, and ARCC commits both halves of it itself.**

| ARCC, same company, same year, both in `study_numbers.json` | |
|---|---|
| terminal ROIC on **REPLACEMENT** capital (IC = EGP 51,191mn) | **11.26%** |
| terminal ROIC on **BOOK** capital, FY2025 | **60.60%** |
| ratio | **5.38x** |
| terminal cost of capital | 18.34% |

- On replacement capital: **11.26% < 18.34% → growth DESTROYS value** → real growth set to zero
  → terminal minimised.
- On book capital: **60.60% ≫ 18.34% → growth creates value, enormously.**

**The choice of denominator decides the SIGN of the growth term**, which decides mechanism 2,
which decides the terminal, which is **53.5% of this study's enterprise value.**

### And the ratio is not a valid measurement in the first place

**The numerator and the denominator are not on the same basis.** NOPAT is earned on assets
bought at HISTORICAL cost; the capital base is struck at REPLACEMENT cost. Dividing one by the
other is not a return on anything — it is a return a company would earn *if it had to rebuild
today at today's prices while selling at yesterday's economics.*

This house has already written the general lesson down, in its own words:

> **"A RATIO BETWEEN TWO QUANTITIES DEFINED DIFFERENTLY IS NOT EVIDENCE ABOUT EITHER. It
> computes, it sorts the book, it looks like a measurement, and the first time somebody does
> the arithmetic it was standing in for, it points the other way."**

That lesson was adopted about a different ratio. **The terminal ROIC is the same error, and it
is load-bearing.**

**Priced — and the house already measured it**: holding growth, the discount rate and every
other driver exactly as published and moving only that return, ARCC goes **53.22 at 11.3% →
68.52 at 20% → 75.09 at 30% → 79.27 at the company's own FY2024 book figure. +49%.**

### The bases are not even consistent across the book

| | terminal capital basis | terminal ROIC |
|---|---|---|
| ARCC | replacement cost | 11.26% |
| SCEM | replacement cost | 19.95% (book ROIC computes to **nan**) |
| AMOC | historical gross book | 30.28% |
| EGCH | book D&A escalated | — |

**Three different bases across four studies, none named on the page, and the choice sets
whether growth adds or destroys value.**

## HOW THEY COMPOUND

1. **Mechanism 3** inflates the capital base → terminal ROIC falls below the cost of capital.
2. → *"growth destroys value"* → **Mechanism 2** sets real growth to zero, and the study is
   right to, *given the ROIC it computed.*
3. **Mechanism 1** meanwhile erodes the revenue base both are applied to, by 2.5 points a year
   in real terms.

**Each step is locally correct. The chain is not.** And no gate can see it, because every gate
checks relationships between figures and all three of these move the figures together.

---

## THE CORROBORATION IS INDEPENDENT AND IT ALL POINTS ONE WAY

- **Driver census, five walk-forwards:** revenue comes in **45% above** forecast, cost 40%
  above — margin right, **scale too low** — and it compounds, −0.169 at one year to **−0.629 at
  five**. Intercept near zero, large slope: **a rate error.** Fitted, nominal revenue growth is
  understated by **~12.9 points a year.**
- **Two outside auditors, two names, one mechanism**, both pointing up (+17.8%, +68.7%).
- **The cross-section:** 12 of 16 studies below the price; median −9.1%.
- **AMOC's own back-test**, quoted by its auditor: *"under-forecast every single time, by 64%
  on average."*

## WHAT I AM NOT DOING, AND IT IS NOT TIMIDITY

Correcting all three at once would move the book violently upward — mechanism 3 alone is +49%
on ARCC — and the pooled lean ex the two studies known to be broken is only **−1.6% mean,
−5.3% median**. **A house that corrects its pessimism into optimism has fixed nothing**, and
the promotion guard is symmetric for exactly this reason: one lever at a time, in a fixed
order, halted where the pooled bias would cross zero.

**The order I will propose**, because it runs from the most structural to the most
parameter-like:

1. **Mechanism 3** — put every terminal on ONE capital basis with the numerator and denominator
   matched, and name it. This is a correctness fix, not a lever: the current ratio is not a
   measurement of anything.
2. **Mechanism 1** — state the convention as a rule: a traded price is held flat in REAL terms,
   and any real path is written down as the real number it is.
3. **Mechanism 2** — only then revisit real terminal growth, because after (1) it may no longer
   need revisiting: with a coherent ROIC above the cost of capital, growth stops destroying
   value and zero stops being the defensible default.

**The general lesson: conservatism does not arrive as a judgement anybody made. It arrives as a
default nobody had to argue for, a convention adopted to avoid taking a view, and a ratio whose
two halves were measured differently — and every one of those looks like rigour on the page.**
