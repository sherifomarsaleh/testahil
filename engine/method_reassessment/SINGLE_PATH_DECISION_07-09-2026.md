# One path: every name is BUILT to the standard, never repaired to it

**Decision taken 07-09-2026, on the principal's proposal — "why don't we issue a new
fundamental study and disregard the one we had before".** Measured before it was taken, and
the measurement is why it stands.

## The question

Twenty-four study directories exist, carrying **351 ratchet entries** between them, a median
of fourteen each. Sixty-six published names have no study at all. The plan until today was
two-track: repair the twenty-four, then build the rest. The proposal is one track — build
all ninety fresh under the finished framework and discard what the old studies concluded.

## What decided it: the debt is mostly SHAPE, not error

Classifying every ratchet entry by **what actually closes it**:

| what closes it | share |
|---|---|
| a record the study does not commit — a terminal record, a cost-of-capital schedule, driver lines, a bridge | **68%** |
| rebuilding the delivered document or workbook | 15% |
| genuine new research — a figure or a search that does not exist yet | 6% |
| unclassified (exemplar pointers, figure axes) | 10% |

Two-thirds of the debt is a record the **sanctioned modules now emit by construction**.
A study built through `terminal_value.build()`, `cost_of_capital.py` and
`assert_ground_up()` commits those records because it cannot do otherwise. On the repair
path somebody writes each one in by hand, twenty-four times.

**And the repair path has no advantage on the expensive half.** Only six of the twenty-four
directories hold their filings on disk, so the statements are re-fetched either way. The
research is common to both routes; the reconciliation with an old model is not.

## Tested on the exemplar rather than on the average

The 68% is a book-wide figure and a book-wide figure can hide the case in hand. ADNOCLS
carries sixteen entries and they split:

* **nine close by construction** — the cost-of-capital weights, the driver lines, the
  asset-base record, the anchor ordering (a build that reads the income statement out of the
  filing it already takes the balance sheet from), the retired "weighted central" wording in
  a document rebuilt under the current lens rule, and the rebuild ledger the rebuild itself
  creates;
* **two are real modelling work** — the equity bridge arriving at 1.5263 a share against a
  published 5.6054, and the relative lens that will not reproduce from its own operands. One
  unit confusion, thousands against millions, and a fresh build does not fix it for free;
* **one is a single figure from a filing** — the reported earnings per share;
* **one does not close by rebuilding at all**, and knowing that in advance is the point of
  testing on a real name: the terminal needs a disclosed vessel life, and
  `TERMINAL_EVIDENCE_05-09-2026.md` records three independent reasons why the accounts do
  not yield one. It stays on the ratchet with its reason.

So the saving is real and it is not total. What the single path removes is the paperwork;
what it leaves is the defects, which is the right thing to be spending the time on.

## What is NOT discarded, and this is the half that could go wrong

Discarding a study means discarding **its model and its conclusion**. It does not mean
discarding evidence, and three things must survive:

1. **`engine/fv_vintages.json`** — every fair value the site has published, dated, with the
   spot it was struck against. [R-VCAL-01] scores criterion 3 on it, criterion 3 is one of
   the two things currently holding publication, and that rule states in its own text that
   the archive cannot be reconstructed afterwards. Losing it pushes the method proof further
   away, not closer.
2. **Sweep registers, beta records, filings on disk** — seventeen of twenty-four carry a
   Step 2A sweep and twenty-three a beta record. Those are inputs. A fresh build needs the
   same ones and re-running a four-ring sweep that was done properly is waste.
3. **The ledger, the graded cohorts and the band records** — the price lens's own track
   record, untouched by any of this. The two clocks stay apart.

## The move is still a move

A fresh study that lands somewhere different from the published number has moved the answer,
and calling it new rather than rebuilt does not make the route invisible — `fv_vintages`
shows it either way. [R-REBUILD-01] exists because six individually-correct corrections took
one study from 55% below the price to 71% below it **through +45% on the way**, and the
running total was looked at once, at the end. A single-path build carries the same
obligation: the levers in the order applied, each naming the rule it serves, and an audit
point declared in advance.

## The document does not change — only what sits under it

**Per instruction, 07-09-2026: the rebuilt study carries the same structure, elements and
formatting as the fundamental section of the studies as issued.** This is a rebuild of the
constructions, not a redesign of the deliverable, and the shape is not a matter of taste
here — it is machine-readable in `research_protocol.MODEL_STUDY` and enforced by
`check_document_structure.py` and `check_workbook_structure.py`:

* **sixteen Word sections in order**, with the fundamental valuation as §1 and its nine
  sub-sections unchanged — 1.1 the cash-flow model with the full FCFF waterfall and the
  enterprise-to-equity bridge, 1.2 book value and sustainable return, 1.3 relative multiples,
  1.4 normalised earnings power, 1.5 synthesis, 1.6 drivers with margins as outputs, 1.7 the
  crux, 1.8 macro and country with every contested construction priced, 1.9 sensitivity;
* **sixteen Excel sheets, same names, same order**;
* the standalone bibliography and the QC gate beside them.

What changes is underneath: the terminal comes from the sanctioned module, the cost of
capital from `cost_of_capital.py`, the driver lines from a committed record rather than a
summary of one, the bridge reaching the answer the study publishes. A reader opening the
rebuilt document should find the same document in the same order — with figures that now
reproduce.

One consequence worth stating: **§1.5 already conforms to the current rule in the skeleton
and not in the exemplar's own delivered text.** The skeleton says the class primary IS the
central and forbids a weighted blend; ADNOCLS as issued still prints the retired four-lens
weighting. The rebuild follows the skeleton, which is what "the same structure as the
studies issued" means once the skeleton and one delivered document disagree.

## Sequencing: the exemplar first, and it is not a preference

CLAUDE.md tells every study to open the model report beside the one being written and match
its sections, sheets, content and depth. That instruction is how this house's standards
propagate, and it transmits **every property the template happens to have** rather than the
rules somebody remembered to write down. ADNOCLS sits on sixteen ratchets today. Build
eighty-nine fresh studies against it as it stands and every one inherits a bridge that lands
73% away from its own published answer and a unit confusion between thousands and millions —
looking exactly like the house standard, because it is the house standard.

So: ADNOCLS first, then the rest.

## What it does to the schedule

There are no longer twenty-four to repair and sixty-six to build. There are **ninety to
build, one way**, of which twenty-four arrive with a sweep, a beta record and — for six of
them — their filings already fetched.

The rate is still unmeasured and this document does not invent one. Building the exemplar
end to end under the full gate set produces the first honest number, and that number is what
turns ninety into a date.

## What would overturn this decision

If building the exemplar fresh takes materially longer than repairing it would have — the
measurement is the rebuild ledger's own lever count and the session it takes — then the 68%
was a bad proxy for the work and the two-track plan was right. Stated here in advance so the
comparison is not made after the fact by whoever prefers the answer they already gave.
