## What is wrong

The terminal carries the reinvestment identity, whose implied replacement cycle is 1/g —
**fifty years at a 2.0% terminal** — against a base this company's own note turns over in
about thirty. That is a fact about the dirham's peg and not about a polyolefins complex.
The charge it levies is 16.7% of terminal profit.

Per [R-TERM-01 CLAUSE TWO] the defect runs the OPTIMISTIC way in a pegged market, so a
rebuild would be *expected* to lower this value. [CLAUSE TWO CORRECTED] is equally explicit
that the sign is measured per name and never read off the ratio, so nothing here claims to
know it in advance.

## And the accounts close the route, with a demonstration rather than a policy sentence

**[L-328] condition (i), the residual value.** Note 3:

> *"Depreciation is charged so as to write off the cost **less estimated residual values** of
> property, plant and equipment ... over their estimated useful lives, using the
> straight-line method"*

**[L-328] condition (ii), and this is the clearest case in the book because the company
quantified BOTH ends of it.** Under "Change in estimate – useful life of property, plant and
equipment":

> *"during the year ended 31 December 2025, the Group has reassessed the estimated useful
> life of some of its Plant, Machinery and related Buildings assets ... resulting in a
> decrease in depreciation charge and increase in profit before tax for the year of **USD
> 90,335 thousand** ... the estimated useful lives of such plants and related buildings have
> increased by **up to an additional 10 years**"*

and, for the year after:

> *"the change in useful lives is expected to decrease depreciation expense ... by
> approximately **USD 179,198 thousand in 2026**"*

**applied prospectively from 1 July 2025** — so the FY2025 charge is a blend of six months on
the old lives and six months on the new, and the 2026 effect is almost exactly twice the
2025 one, which is what a half-year of it should be.

**THE SAME ACCUMULATED BALANCE THEREFORE SUPPORTS THREE DIFFERENT "AGES" SPANNING FIFTY PER
CENT, AND NOTHING ABOUT THE ASSETS MOVED:**

| the age the identity returns | denominator | years |
|---|---|---:|
| on the pre-change basis | the 2025 charge plus the 90,335 the change removed | **14.98** |
| on the charge actually reported | the blended 2025 charge of 449,384 | **17.99** |
| on the go-forward basis | approximately 360,521, the pre-change level less the disclosed 2026 effect | **22.42** |

The last row is approximate and is labelled so — it holds additions and disposals still,
which the disclosure itself does ("assuming no further additions, disposals or changes in
estimates"). The first two are exact.

**A ratio is a measurement only when its numerator and denominator are struck on the same
basis.** Here the numerator is nine years of accumulated charges on the old lives and the
denominator is half a year of them, so the ratio is not a fact about this plant at all — it
is a fact about when the technical steering committee finished its lifetime-extension review.

## Why the disclosed-life route does not rescue it either

The note now publishes lives, and they are ranges: buildings 15–40, plant and machinery
8–35, motor vehicles 4–10, furniture and equipment 3–4. A midpoint of a range is a choice,
not a disclosure, and the identity that would weight them is the one just broken. Plant and
machinery is 91% of the depreciable cost and its range is **8 to 35 years** — a spread wide
enough that the choice, not the evidence, would decide the terminal.

So no life is adopted and no age is assumed. Stop and inform, SIGCM clause 8.

## What would open it

- **The residual values**, in aggregate or by class.
- **The FY2026 charge as reported**, which lands the go-forward denominator on the same
  footing as an accumulated balance that will by then include a full year on the new lives —
  one more reporting cycle makes this name straightforwardly measurable, which is worth
  writing down because it is a date rather than a hope.
- A **class-level split of the 90,335**, which the note gets close to and does not give.

## The general form: three for three, and they are not alike

Three names re-tested today, three stops, on the same clause: an ocean fleet, a fuel-retail
network and a polyolefins complex. **Residual-value depreciation is the ordinary IFRS
presentation, so the measured-age route is the exception rather than the rule** — which
[L-328] said and which is now measured on seven names rather than five.

**And there is a jurisdictional pattern worth testing rather than asserting.** The one name
the route has unlocked in this pass, EGCH, discloses depreciation as RATES applied to cost
with no residual mentioned — the Egyptian presentation. The three that stopped are all UAE
filers using the IFRS residual-value form.
