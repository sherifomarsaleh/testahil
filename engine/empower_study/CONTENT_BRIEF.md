## The company and the story
Emirates Central Cooling Systems Corporation PJSC ("Empower"), DFM: EMPOWER,
AED. The world's largest district-cooling utility by connected capacity: 1,707k
refrigeration tons (RT) connected / 2,018k RT contracted at 30-Jun-2026, ~80%
of Dubai's district-cooling market, 161k+ customer accounts. Listed 16-Nov-2022
at the tail of the Dubai privatisation programme; DEWA (Dubai Electricity &
Water Authority) took its stake from 56% to 80% in Feb-2026 by buying Dubai
Holding's 24% at AED 2.16/share — a related-party control price 44% above the
07-Aug-2026 close of 1.50. Free float ~20%.

Business model: two revenue legs on one asset base. (1) CAPACITY (demand)
charges — contracted, paid on connected RT regardless of usage; the fixed-cost
recovery and effectively the profit pool. (2) CONSUMPTION charges — metered
chilled-water usage (RTh) whose dominant cost, electricity & water purchased
from DEWA (AED 1,467.7m in FY2025), is ~76% of consumption revenue — i.e. the
consumption leg is largely a regulated pass-through. Plus a small
pre-insulated-pipes line (Logstor, 17.2m) and the DXB CoolCo airport cooling
concession (70%-owned, 35-year, acquired 2023 — inside the connected base; its
finance-lease interest income, 58.3m, is presented inside gross profit).
Tariffs are regulated (Dubai Executive Council Resolution 6/2021; RSB approval).

Financial character: EBITDA margin 48-50% every audited year FY2021-FY2025;
revenue 2,463.9 → 3,419.3m (FY21→FY25); net profit 1,003.9m FY2025; net debt
2,991m (1.8x EBITDA) at Jun-2026 after the 2025 refinance of both AED 2.75bn
RCFs at a reduced margin; dividend committed at AED 875m/yr for 2025 AND 2026
(DPS 0.0875, 5.8% yield at 1.50) — and FCFE runs almost exactly at the payout,
so the dividend is covered but not slack.

## What happened to the price (why now)
2026 is the conflict year: Iranian strikes on UAE infrastructure Feb-Apr 2026,
ceasefire from 8-Apr (formalised Jun, fraying since Jul). DFM fell 21.9% peak-
to-trough; CBUAE cut 2026 GDP growth guidance 5.6% → 1.7%. Empower's H1-2026:
revenue +4.5% but Q2 standalone revenue FELL 2.7% y/y — consumption dropped
42m RTh, equivalent full-load hours −9.0% y/y, tied by the interim notes partly
to conflict-hit hospitality occupancy. The stock closed at 1.50 on 07-Aug,
−23% from its 52-week high of 1.96, with the last leg (−6%) after the 05-Aug
H1 release. RSI(14) ~29.

## The two structural judgements (both surfaced, never blended)
1. THE CRUX — consumption per connected RT: does the −6% FY2026 shock recover?
   Base case: recovers to the FY2025 level through FY2027 (hospitality-linked,
   truce holds). Full alternative model: it NEVER recovers. The finding the
   reader should take away: because consumption is ~76% DEWA pass-through, the
   persistence case only moves the DCF from 2.664 to 2.594 (−2.6%) — the
   market's readthrough from the consumption miss to the equity is larger than
   the cash-flow arithmetic supports. The capacity charge, not the meter,
   carries the value.
2. THE DUAL-FRAMED TAX QUESTION: FY2025 effective tax is exactly 9.0% (UAE CT).
   Whether the OECD Pillar-Two 15% domestic minimum top-up tax reaches Empower
   through DEWA-level consolidation (DEWA group revenue far exceeds EUR 750m)
   is contested and unresolved. The ENTIRE valuation is published BOTH ways:
   DCF 2.664 (9%) vs 2.483 (15%); central 2.250 vs 2.139. Side by side in the
   summary table, the body, the workbook and Expert 3's range. Never averaged.

## Caveats / what would change our mind (§7)
- Consumption failing to recover in FY2027 prints (watch EFLH in each deck).
- DMTT confirmed → shift weight to the 15% column (published).
- War re-escalation onto UAE soil → bear case machinery.
- Tariff cut at an RSB review would break the flat-tariff assumption (the
  single most valuable regulated-utility assumption in the model).
- The 4.4y AED sovereign tenor vs 5y+ cash flows; index (DFMGI) data sparsity
  in the beta window; the H1-2026 mix percentages come from the IR deck, not
  the audited notes (flagged).
- Related-party dominance: 80% owner is also the sole input supplier (DEWA) —
  governance/tariff-bargaining concentration risk.
