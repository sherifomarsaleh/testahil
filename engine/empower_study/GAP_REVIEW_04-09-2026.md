## The finding that comes before the headings: this study was invisible

**Until this edition, no gate could see this answer at all.** EMPOWER published four
branches under keys only its own builder knew, so every instrument that looks for a central
found none and recorded the study as UNREADABLE — which is how it escaped the one check
that examines the ANSWER rather than the process that produced it. An unreadable answer is
not a clean answer, and the fix is a two-line change: the branches are now published in the
shared form, and the moment they were, this review became owed.

That is worth stating because nothing about the study changed. The +17% was there
yesterday.

## 1. LATEST FILINGS

Every disclosed period is read and cited to the filing: audited statements FY2023, FY2024
and FY2025, the reviewed interims to 30 June 2026, and the H1-2026 earnings deck. The
bridge stands on the 30-June-2026 reviewed balance sheet and the cash-flow clock starts
there, so FY2026 contributes its second half only.

**This edition added something the study did not have: the FY2025 audited statements
themselves**, fetched from the company's own investor-relations page. They carry no usable
text layer — 85 bytes across 85 pages — and were read by OCR off the rendered pixels, with
the landscape property note rotated first. The extraction foots four ways against figures
the notes print independently. What that made possible is set out in
`TERMINAL_EVIDENCE_04-09-2026.md` and is not spent in this edition.

## 2. BASE YEAR

FY2026 is built from the reviewed first half plus a forecast second half on the company's
own disclosed physical drivers — connected tonnage, equivalent full-load hours, and the
consumption and capacity tariffs — rather than from a growth rate applied to last year.
The model's FY2026 revenue is checked against the same-seasonality projection of the H1
print and sits within 4% of it, which the study asserts rather than asserts.

## 3. MACRO COHERENCE

**This heading finds something and this edition does not fix it.** The terminal growth
rates are typed nominals — 2.5% for the FY2031-40 window and 1.5% beyond it — and the
study carries no macro record, so nothing holds them to the house path.

The arithmetic is in `TERMINAL_EVIDENCE_04-09-2026.md` and its conclusion is unusual and
worth stating here: **storing them properly would move no number on this name.** This
company's tariff is regulated and NOT indexed, so nominal revenue growth is volume growth,
and 2.5% on 2.0% house inflation is +0.49% real while 1.5% is −0.49% real. Both reproduce
exactly. What storage would change is what a reader can see — that stage two assumes the
business shrinks in real terms for ever, which the study's own phrase "about zero real
tariff growth" describes without naming.

## 4. DISCOUNT RATE

The cost of capital is built rather than assumed, on a beta regressed against the exchange's
published index, with both premium bases carried and converging within 30 basis points. The
study prices four separate weighting constructions — target net-debt, gross-debt, negative
carry, and a DFM-beta comparison — and publishes each with a per-share value rather than
choosing one silently. Country risk enters once.

**Nothing found.**

## 5. TERMINAL

**The terminal carries 78.0% of enterprise value**, and it is built on the construction this
house has retired: two stages, each charging reinvestment as growth over the return on
capital, which implies rebuilding the whole capital base every fifty years in stage one and
every sixty-seven in stage two, against a base the company's own notes turn over in **28.1
years**.

**The evidence to correct it is now gathered and the correction is not made in this
edition.** The reason is recorded in full rather than implied: the model change was built
and run — worth about +1.5% on the terminal and +0.5% on the answer — and the workbook
stopped it. This study publishes four parallel live models on one sheet, each rebuilding
the terminal in live formulas on a fixed row map, and the sanctioned construction needs six
more rows per block; inserting them moves addresses a dozen formulas on four other sheets
name directly. Doing that carelessly would have been worse than not doing it.

**Found, priced, and outstanding: worth about +0.5%, so it does not close this gap.**

## 8. MULTIPLE CROSS-CHECK

**This heading cuts against the answer, and it does so through a lens the study already
publishes.** The relative lens reads AED 1.44 on enterprise value against the closest listed

So the cash-flow lens is the only one above the price, and the published central is a

retiring them here would take the answer to the cash-flow lens alone at AED 2.11 — further
from the price, not closer. That change belongs in its own pass, on a study whose answer is
already two-sided.

**Found: the answer rests on one lens of four, and the other three sit below the market.**

## VERDICT

**The answer is not changed, and this review's most useful output is that the study is now
visible to the instruments at all.**

Three headings found something:

- the study was **unreadable** to the answer-side check, and the branches are now published
  in the shared form — which is what made this review owed;
- the **terminal** carries the retired construction on 78% of enterprise value, and the
  evidence to rebuild it is now gathered, priced at about +0.5%, and set down with the

- the **multiple cross-check** puts three of the four lenses below the traded price, and the
  published central is a typed blend of all four — an architecture this house retired.

None of the three closes a +17% gap, and two of them would widen it. **That is itself the
finding:** where a study sits above the price and its own known defects would push it higher
still, the disagreement is not in the places this review can see.

**The number is not moved toward the price.** What this edition changes is that the answer
can now be seen, measured and argued with.
