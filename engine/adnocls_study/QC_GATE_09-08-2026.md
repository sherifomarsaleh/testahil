## The four driver expectations that were wrong

The protocol says that when a driver test fails, the first hypothesis is that the expectation
is wrong rather than the model. All four were decomposed before anything was changed, and in
all four the model was right and the expectation was not.

**The sovereign default spread.** Asserted to raise fair value, actually lowered it by 0.52%.
The spread enters twice. In the explicit period it is subtracted from the observed government
yield to normalise the risk-free rate, which lowers the cost of equity — the asserted effect.
But the terminal cost of debt is built as the terminal risk-free rate plus the company's credit
spread, and that spread is measured as the observed cost of debt less the normalised risk-free
rate. Widening the sovereign spread lowers the normalised rate, so the measured credit spread
mechanically widens, so the terminal cost of debt rises. Eighty-four per cent of the value is
terminal, so that dominates.

**The gross-up from charter-equivalent revenue to reported revenue.** Asserted to lower fair
value through receivables, actually raised it by 0.03%. Working capital is receivables at 108
days on revenue, plus inventory at 14 days on operating cost, less payables at 132 days on
operating cost. The gross-up leaves earnings untouched, so operating cost moves one-for-one
with revenue, and payables less inventory is 118 days against receivables at 108. Extra
gross-up revenue is a net source of working capital, not a use.

**The statutory corporate tax rate.** Asserted to lower fair value, actually raised it by
0.55%. The assertion is true of the three business-unit tax rates, which tax operating profit,
but false of the statutory rate, which in this model appears only in the after-tax cost of
debt and the after-tax interest in the funding roll. A higher statutory rate is a larger
interest tax shield, so the weighted cost of capital falls 0.33% and the valuation rises. Both
rates are now asserted separately.

**The depreciation rate on property, plant and equipment.** Asserted to raise fair value
through the add-back, actually lowered it by 5.94%. In the explicit period free cash flow
barely moves — depreciation is deducted and added straight back and only the tax shield
survives, worth little at an effective rate near 4%, so 2026 free cash flow rises 1.09%. But
the terminal block capitalises an after-tax profit measure rather than a cash measure, so a
higher non-cash charge lowers it permanently: terminal value falls 6.27% and fair value with
it. This is a real property of the construction and is worth knowing.

## The beta, rebuilt on the exchange's published index

The first edition of this study regressed the share against an equal-weight composite of its
exchange's names, built from the study's own price library, because no published index series
was available. One is now available and the regression has been rebuilt on it. The published
index is what the method asks for, and the answer moves a long way.

| | Regressed against | Beta | Cost of equity | Cost of capital | Cash-flow lens | Weighted central |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Adopted** | FTSE ADX General Index | **1.085** | 9.34% | 9.01% | **AED 5.69** | **AED 6.60** |
| Published alongside | Equal-weight composite of the same exchange | 0.705 | 7.49% | 7.31% | AED 8.40 | AED 7.68 |

The regression passes the usability test on all three conditions: 161 weekly observations,
R-squared 0.158 against a 0.05 floor, standard error 0.199 against a point estimate of 1.085.
The 90% confidence interval is 0.758 to 1.412, and the lead-lag sum beta — the correction for
co-movement a share books late because it does not trade every session — is 1.164, above the
contemporaneous estimate rather than below it.

**The gap between the two constructions is a property of index construction, not of the
company.** The published index is weighted by size, so it is dominated by the same large-
capitalisation group the subject belongs to; an equal-weight composite gives the exchange's
smallest names the same say as its largest. Two further disclosures work against the adopted
figure rather than for it, and are stated for that reason: the subject is itself a constituent
of the index it is regressed against, and on a capitalisation-weighted index that cannot be
removed — the equal-weight proxy run both ways puts the resulting self-covariance at about
0.20 of beta; and the index series ends 24-Jul-2026, before the last stock session used
elsewhere in the study, so two weekly observations fall outside the regression.

The bear and bull cases no longer use round betas chosen by hand. Each now takes the
regression's own 90% confidence bound — 1.412 and 0.758 — so the published range is the range
the estimate itself supports.

Two bounds elsewhere in the study were built on the *alternative* cost of equity rather than
on a confidence bound, and inverted silently when that alternative stopped being the demanding
one: the book lens's low bound and Expert 2's low bound both came to sit **above** their own
base cases. Both now take the same two confidence bounds as the cash-flow lens — the book lens
reads 3.49 – 5.11 – 7.64 and Expert 2 reads 3.41 – 4.66 – 6.55. Two assertions were added so
this class of defect cannot recur silently: every lens range must bracket its own base, and
every expert range must bracket its own base. The football-field and expert-range figures also
draw each bar over the full extent of its three values, so a base can never again be plotted
outside its own bar.

## What the study concludes

Central fair value **AED 6.60** a share against a market price of **AED 6.16** — about 7%
above it, which is inside the noise of the estimate rather than a gap worth acting on. The
cash-flow lens alone gives **AED 5.69**, *below* the market. On the alternative index
construction the same model gives AED 8.40 and a central of 7.68; both are published at full
size and neither is averaged into the other.

The finding worth stating plainly is that the first edition's central disagreement has
resolved itself. That edition set the share's measured beta of 0.705 against an asset-risk
prior that a fleet owner carries the risk of the market it trades in whoever its customer is,
and published both because they disagreed. Measured against the exchange's own published
index, the regression now returns 1.085 — the prior is confirmed by the data rather than
contradicted by it. What remains genuinely contested is where tanker rates settle after 2026,
which is §1.7, and that is unchanged.
