That is why it was audited first and why the audit is written in full.

## 1. LATEST FILINGS

Every disclosed period is read and consumed. The most recent is the CONDENSED
CONSOLIDATED INTERIM FINANCIAL INFORMATION FOR THE THREE MONTHS ENDED 31 MARCH 2026, and
the valuation date IS that balance-sheet date rather than the date of the latest traded
price — so no roll-forward stands between the bridge and a filing, and the first
quarter's free cash flow sits inside net debt rather than being discounted a second time.

Consumed: the audited statements for FY2022 through FY2025, the FY2025 annual report, the
FY2025 results release, the FY2025 and Q1-2026 management discussion, the investor
presentations of FY2025 and April 2026, and the FY2025 and Q1-2026 earnings calls.

**ONE THING IS OUTSTANDING AND IT IS NAMED RATHER THAN GLOSSED.** This review is written
on 4 September 2026 and the half-year to 30 June 2026 would normally have been filed by
mid-August. This study holds no such document. Under [R-BRIDGE-01] the bridge stands on
the LATEST DISCLOSED sheet, so if that half-year exists the bridge is one period stale —
and on a company that acquired a fleet during 2025 and is still spending USD 1.3bn a year
of capital, one period is not nothing. IT IS REGISTERED AS AN OPEN ITEM rather than
assumed absent: the sweep must attempt the company's own investor-relations channel and
log the outcome either way, which is the primary-source rule and is the first thing the
next pass on this name does.

## 2. BASE YEAR

The base year foots to the filed periods. FY2025 revenue, EBITDA, depreciation and profit
are the audited figures. The forecast is built vessel by vessel — each chartered vessel at
its own rate for exactly the days its own contract runs, everything else at the implied
spot rate for its class — rather than from a group growth rate, and the day rates are the
company's own disclosed quarterly figures.

Nothing is annualised into the answer. FY2026 is discounted over the 75% of the year
still unearned at the 31 March valuation date.

## 3. MACRO COHERENCE

Inflation, currency and price are ONE path, and after this pass none of them is this
study's own. The running-cost escalator is the house AE inflation ladder year by year —
2.5 per cent in 2026 and 2.0 thereafter, sourced to the published target — and the
terminal risk-free rate is DERIVED from that ladder rather than typed. Before this pass
the study carried a terminal rate of 3.25 per cent on a 1.25 per cent real assumption of
its own, against a house-derived 3.98; conforming LOWERED the answer, which is the
direction that shows a rule is not being fitted to a price.

There is no currency path, and that is a construction rather than an omission: the dirham
is hard-pegged at 3.6725, so this economy imports United States monetary policy and the
house path returns a FLAT currency path by construction of the peg. A model deriving a
depreciation here would be manufacturing movement the peg forbids.

Charter rates carry a STATED exemption from the ladder. They are US-dollar day rates set
in a global freight market, and Egyptian-style domestic inflation has no route to them —
under a peg there is not even a currency path for it to arrive through.

## 4. DISCOUNT RATE

The operations are discounted at a rate weighted on the GROSS capital structure — equity,
drawn debt AND the perpetual capital securities — and every one of those three is also
deducted in the bridge. Those are the two halves of one treatment: a claim deducted from
enterprise value must be allowed to price that value, or the model subtracts a cheap
tranche of capital for nothing. The cash is not added at face anywhere; it sits inside the
net-debt figure deducted once.

The schedule is 8.55 per cent, essentially flat to a terminal 8.52, and the flatness is
the peg rather than a choice. Country risk enters once. Both premium bases would be
published and only one exists: the country-risk file carries NO sovereign
credit-default-swap entry for the United Arab Emirates, so the rating basis is the only
one there is, and the spread netted out of the risk-free rate and the premium added back
are the same basis.

**ONE DEFECT FOUND AND CORRECTED HERE.** The adopted cost of debt was the AVERAGE of three
constructions, and only one of the three is weighted by what is actually drawn — so the
adopted figure reproduced from no set of facility lines at all. It is now the
balance-weighted rate, which reproduces from the contractual anchor exactly, and the other
two are published as what they are.

**AND THE DISAGREEMENT WITH THE MARKET IS A RATE.** The reverse read solves what the price
must believe on this study's own cash flows: at AED 6.85 the price is paying for a
flat 7.88 per cent cost of capital against a schedule equivalent to a flat 8.31.
43 basis points is the whole of it. That is a small number to carry an 18 per cent gap,
and it is small because the terminal is 72.7 per cent of enterprise value — at that
weighting a modest change in the price of time moves the answer a long way.

## 5. TERMINAL

**THIS IS THE LARGEST KNOWN DEFECT IN THIS STUDY AND IT IS NOT FIXED IN THIS PASS.** The
terminal is built on the reinvestment identity, which charges the growth rate times
invested capital every year for ever and therefore implies a replacement cycle of one over
that growth rate — FIFTY YEARS at a 2 per cent terminal, for a fleet of ships whose own
accounting-policies note states 25 years for tankers, dry-bulk and containers, 20 to 25
for offshore vessels and 25 to 40 for gas carriers. An inflation rate is silently doing
duty as an asset life.

THE DIRECTION OF THAT DEFECT REVERSES AT LOW INFLATION, and this is where it was first
seen: on the Egyptian names a 7 per cent terminal makes the implied cycle 14.3 years and
the construction OVER-charges a 25-year plant. Here a pegged 2 per cent makes it fifty and
it UNDER-charges a 25-year ship by half. Correcting it would LOWER this value further, not
raise it.

IT WAS REBUILT ON THE SANCTIONED CONSTRUCTION IN THIS PASS AND THE BUILD REFUSED, for a
reason worth stating rather than working around. Maintenance at the disclosed 25-year
vessel life comes to less than this model's own book depreciation, so terminal free cash
flow would exceed terminal profit and the implied payout lands at 117 per cent — a going
concern distributing more than it earns for ever. The two figures disagree because they
are not measuring the same thing: this study's own register already records that the
realised depreciation rate runs above what the disclosed lives imply BECAUSE DRY-DOCKING
COMPONENTS ARE WRITTEN OFF OVER TWO TO FIVE YEARS. Dry-docking is maintenance, capitalised
and amortised fast, so a terminal charging only hull replacement while adding back all
book depreciation would be adding back the amortisation of a maintenance cost it never
charged.

The honest charge covers the hull cycle AND the recurring dry-docking, and this disclosure
splits neither the vessel line by type nor the charge by component. A life this desk chose
is not a disclosed life, so none is chosen: what is needed is the component split, and it
is registered as an open item rather than invented.

## 6. BALANCE SHEET

The bridge stands on the 31 March 2026 statement of financial position, subject to the
half-year question under heading 1. Net debt carries the committed acquisition and the
deferred purchase consideration, because both are funded commitments rather than options.
The perpetual capital securities are deducted at carrying value, with the present value of
their perpetual coupon published beside it as the alternative — which of the two a reader
prefers is a judgement and the study does not hide it inside one number.

The minority is deducted from EQUITY value and on a value-share basis, with one refinement
this company needs: the Navig8 slice is subject to a disclosed purchase arrangement at a
stated price, so its claim is that price rather than a share of anything, and the
remainder is worth its share of the value the model capitalises, floored at book. The
adopted deduction is therefore ABOVE book, and it is above book because the model
capitalises 100 per cent of subsidiary cash flow.

## 7. CLAIMS AGAINST THE RECORD

The shape a reader should see, printed rather than left to be inferred: the cash-flow lens
sits BELOW every other read in this study. Replacement of the blend does not change that
— it exposes it. Sum-of-the-parts on peer multiples reads 10.04, the group relative
multiple 8.64, and the disclosed book floor 3.66.

The study's own largest judgement is worth more than the gap. Moving the beta regressor
from the published index to an equal-weight composite of the same exchange's names takes
the answer from 5.61 to 7.90, which is +41 per cent — and the beta rule, not a
preference, decides it: the regressor is the published index of the exchange the stock is
listed on, and a constituent composite is a coverage artefact rather than a market. A
reader who prefers the other regressor lands close to the market.

The second largest is the charter-rate path. Adopting the company's own guidance instead
of reversion toward mid-cycle takes the answer to 6.97. GUIDANCE IS SCORED AND NEVER
CONSUMED, and the evidence for reversion is not this desk's opinion: an independent
one-year time charter fixed in early 2026 priced a very large crude carrier well below the
spot rate of the moment, and a crude tanker order book near a quarter of the trading fleet
is the supply reason why. A forward market that will not pay spot for a year of time is
saying it does not expect spot to hold.

The sign test on all five contested judgements reads p=1.00 — two resolved upward and
three down. THERE IS NO LEAN TO CORRECT, which is worth stating on a study sitting 18 per
cent below the market.

## 8. MULTIPLE CROSS-CHECK

At the fair value the shares imply 7.49x this study's own FY2026 forecast EBITDA; at the
traded price, 6.92x. The listed peer set prices long-term contracted gas shipping at
12.26x and spot-exposed tanker names at 8.53x, and this company's own disclosed
spot-exposed share is 31 per cent.

**THAT IS THE MOST UNCOMFORTABLE NUMBER IN THIS REVIEW AND IT IS PRINTED RATHER THAN
BURIED.** The cash-flow lens values a fleet that is roughly seven-tenths contracted at a
multiple below what the market pays for a pure spot tanker operator. Either the peers are
mispriced, or the rate path is too conservative, or the terminal construction under
heading 5 is doing more damage than its 50-year cycle suggests. The third is the one this
house can test, and it is the open item.

## VERDICT

The answer is NOT moved to meet the price, and the two corrections this pass made to the
discount rate moved it in opposite directions — the terminal rate down, the cost of debt
up — with the net effect against the value.

The gap is -18.2 per cent and it is READABLE rather than merely large: 43 basis points
of it is a disagreement about the price of time, measured directly, and the study's own
beta judgement spans more than the whole gap on its own.

WHAT WOULD CHANGE OUR MIND, IN ADVANCE: a half-year to 30 June 2026 showing charter rates
holding at first-quarter levels rather than reverting, which would move the base toward
the sustained path and toward the market; or the vessel-component disclosure that lets the
terminal be rebuilt on a life the filings support, which on present evidence moves the
answer the other way.

THE STUDY IS HELD, NOT PUBLISHED [R-GAP-02]: at -18.2 per cent it is past the 10 per cent
publication limit and no market-dissent document is filed. Holding is the right outcome
for a study whose own multiple cross-check is this uncomfortable and whose largest
structural defect is named and open.
