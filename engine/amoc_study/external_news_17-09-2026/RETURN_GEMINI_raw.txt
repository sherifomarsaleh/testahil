Alexandria Mineral Oils Company (EGX: AMOC): External Intelligence Dossier Post-30 June 2026
Alexandria Mineral Oils Company (EGX: AMOC; ISIN: EGS380P1C010)—registered on the Egyptian Exchange under its official Arabic corporate title, شركة الإسكندرية للزيوت المعدنية—entered the second half of calendar year 2026 with an expanding industrial integration profile and an energy transformation initiative1. Following the close of its reviewed six-month financial period on 30 June 2026, the company’s operating environment has been shaped by three external developments: the execution of a 50 million USD off-balance-sheet utility partnership to eliminate power purchase volatility, state-directed inter-refinery feedstock optimization across the Alexandria petrochemical cluster, and escalating margin pressures driven by foreign exchange depreciation alongside impending fuel pricing adjustments4.
The most significant operational event not captured in historical filings is the 30 July 2026 Heads of Terms agreement executed between AMOC and Intro Utilities4. Structured as a Build-Own-Operate-Transfer (BOOT) concession, the project establishes a Combined Heat and Power (CHP) generation plant intended to insulate AMOC's energy-intensive base oil and wax extraction units from national grid tariff escalations while cutting site fuel consumption by 15%4. Concurrently, inter-refinery coordination disclosures from the Ministry of Petroleum and Mineral Resources in August 2026 confirm that AMOC expanded its intermediate feedstock processing arrangements with El Nasr Petroleum Company and the Middle East Oil Refinery (MIDOR), lifting secondary conversion utilization within the Alexandria refining corridor6.
On governance and capital allocation, the regulatory record on the Egyptian Exchange between 1 July 2026 and 16 September 2026 reflects formal board approvals of reviewed interim accounts under joint audit by Crowe Global (Dr. A. M. Hegazy & Co.) and the Central Auditing Organization (CAO), the issuance of assembly convocations for late September 2026, and preparations for disbursing the second installment of its transitional dividend in mid-October 20269. Every external lead collected across regulatory portals, official state petroleum gazettes, and domestic industrial records has been verified against primary documentation.

Infrastructure and Plant Operations
The Intro Utilities Combined Heat and Power Concession
On 30 July 2026, AMOC entered into a definitive commercial agreement that fundamentally alters the utility and cost architecture of its Alexandria refining complex5. At the company’s administrative headquarters, AMOC Chairman and Managing Director Chemist Maged Mostafa El-Kordy and Intro Utilities Chief Executive Officer Eng. Hany Helaly signed a Heads of Terms / Memorandum of Understanding (مذكرة الشروط الأساسية) to initiate detailed technical and engineering execution for a dedicated Combined Heat and Power (CHP) industrial plant4.
The signing ceremony was officially conducted under the auspices of the Egyptian General Petroleum Corporation (EGPC), represented by Eng. Mohamed Talaat, Director General of Energy Efficiency, and Eng. Yasser Negaty, Assistant Director General4. Senior operational leads in attendance from AMOC included Chemist Naji Abdel-Dayem (General Manager of Energy Efficiency), Eng. Moataz Mohamed Salem (Project Director), and Eng. Neema Hamdallah (General Project Coordinator)5.
The commercial mechanism relies on an off-balance-sheet utility-as-a-service framework5. AMOC avoids direct upfront capital outlays while Intro Utilities designs, constructs, finances, and operates the plant across a multi-year concession before transferring the asset to AMOC4. By securing dedicated on-site power and high-pressure steam, the complex circumvents production interruptions stemming from external grid fluctuations while moderating the financial impact of domestic electricity and industrial gas tariff increases4.
Inter-Refinery Feedstock Integration Across Alexandria and Suez
Beyond utility infrastructure, external announcements from the Ministry of Petroleum and Mineral Resources indicate an acceleration of state-coordinated operational integration involving AMOC6. On 23 August 2026, during the operational review of El Nasr Petroleum Company (NPC) presided over by Minister of Petroleum and Mineral Resources Eng. Karim Badawi, NPC leadership confirmed ongoing direct feedstock exchanges between its Suez installations and the Alexandria refining cluster, explicitly citing bilateral integration with AMOC and Alexandria Special Petroleum Products Company (ASPC)6.
This integration focuses on optimizing the throughput of atmospheric residue and vacuum gas oil (VGO)6. Unfinished distillates and heavy fuel oil components from state-owned atmospheric distillation columns are channeled into AMOC's specialized lube oil, wax processing, and vacuum distillation units6. This integration complements AMOC's multi-year operational arrangement with the neighboring Middle East Oil Refinery (MIDOR)8. Under this bilateral agreement, AMOC supplies intermediate vacuum distillates to MIDOR’s hydrocracker in exchange for finished Euro-V compliant gas oil/diesel (approximately 350,000 tonnes annually at international parity formulas), ensuring steady capacity utilization while maximizing value-added light and middle distillates8.

Macroeconomic Adjustments and Q3 2026 Margin Volatility
Between 14 and 16 September 2026, Egyptian government statements and energy sector dispatches highlighted mounting structural pressure on the petroleum subsidy framework7. International crude oil benchmarks escalated sharply, with Brent spot prices advancing into the 100 to 107 USD per barrel range7. This created a wide divergence from the Egyptian government's fiscal year budget assumption of 75 USD per barrel7.
Simultaneously, the Egyptian pound experienced renewed depreciation in local commercial banking channels, crossing 52.00 EGP per USD on 15 September 20267. Because AMOC purchases intermediate crude inputs, vacuum bottoms, and chemical additives indexed to USD-denominated Mediterranean international cargo benchmarks, currency depreciation expands nominal cost of goods sold, even as it inflates nominal revenues from export sales of paraffin wax and specialty products8. Official communications from the Cabinet in mid-September indicated that the Automatic Pricing Committee convened evaluations to determine the scale of domestic price increases required to offset the rising cost of imported crude and refined products7.
