## PART 4 — THE ONE REJECTION, WITH RECEIPTS

**Finding 8's remedy.** The premise is accepted in full: the masthead says 6 August, the price is
3 September, and a study may not carry two dates. The **conclusion** — that the honest
comparison is 11.40 against the 6-August close of 9.10, making the study *+25% above* the market
— is rejected, and the receipt is this house's own standing rule:

> **NO STUDY IS DELIVERED AGAINST A STALE PRICE.** *(adopted 3 September 2026, on the measurement
> that six of nineteen studies then sat more than ten per cent behind the market — AMOC by 48%,
> at exactly this 9.10.)*

Pricing 11.40 against 9.10 would be delivering against a four-week-old quote, which is the
defect the rule exists to stop. **The fix is to re-date the masthead to the pricing date, not to
re-price the study to the masthead.** The gap is −15.5%, it is real, and it is why this name is
held.

*The critique had no way of knowing the rule. Its instinct — that a study cannot carry two dates
— is right, and it is the only finding in 34 where the remedy points the wrong way.*

## PART 6 — WHAT THIS NAME SAYS ABOUT THE BOOK

**1. The largest finding is the one I had already measured this morning, on four other names.**
Finding 1 is the flat-nominal-price convention: holding crude flat in nominal dollars is not the
absence of a forecast, it is a forecast that the price falls in real terms at US inflation, every
year, for ever. AMOC derives it rather than typing it, which makes it harder to see and does not
make it right. **An outside auditor with no access to that work found it independently and priced
it to four decimal places of my own figure.**

**2. The study has been using a promotion guard as a reason not to be right.** Its own forecast-
anchor record says so in terms:

> *"The reason it is not simply re-anchored is [the] one-lever-at-a-time guard — the move is
> priced at +55% and would carry this study from 12.3% below the price to 35.9% above it in a
> single pass."*

That guard governs levers **promoted from the valuation calibration** — candidate improvements
seeking evidence. It does not govern **corrections to defects**, and the rebuild rule says so
explicitly: *a study wrong in six ways moves a long way when all six are fixed, and a cutoff
would be a free parameter; what is forbidden is the move being invisible.* **Four undisputed
corrections stack to EGP 14.82 here. The guard has been holding them, and the guard was never
about them.**

**3. Every gate passed, and the answer was wrong anyway — in the direction the gates cannot
see.** 6,069 formula cells, zero disagreements; the bridge foots to the unit; country risk
counted exactly once; the cash charged exactly once; the minority at share-of-value rather than
book. All of it correct, all of it improvement, and none of it able to notice that the income
statement runs two inflation paths, that the terminal implies a 14.3-year refinery, or that the
page prints the sign of its own committed number backwards.

**4. And one finding is about our instruments rather than this study.** SA-3: the gate that
enforces workbook recalculation carries a two-name allowlist, AMOC's recalculator is a third
name, and AMOC has been carried as *failing* a check that in fact **passes**. The gate is not
wrong about 22 studies. It is wrong about the one it condemned — which is [L-355] arriving for
the fourth time in two days, and this time inside a gate rather than inside a study.
