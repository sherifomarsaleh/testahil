# AMOC — valuation-gap review, 1 September 2026  [R-GAP-01]

**Trigger.** The first pass of this edition published a central fair value of **EGP 5.53**
against a market price of **EGP 9.10** — **39.2% below**, four times the 10% threshold. This is
the worked precedent for the rule: the review below is what the rule asks for, and it is written
up as it was actually performed rather than reconstructed.

**Outcome.** Five defects and one false claim, every one of them the model being wrong rather
than the company being cheap. Corrected, the study prints **EGP 8.64 against 9.10, −5.0%**, which
no longer trips the threshold. The gap was the only thing in the room saying something was wrong;
every other gate in the repository passed the study.

---

## LATEST FILINGS — was every disclosed period actually read?

**NO, AND THIS WAS THE LARGEST DEFECT.** The reviewed consolidated statements for the six months
to 30 June 2026 had been downloaded from the company's own investor-relations archive on the
first day of this run — one of 104 documents — and were never opened. The study went on
describing that half as *"a press release rather than a filing"*, rejected its gross-profit line
on a coherence test, and solved gross profit from the profit line instead.

What the filing says: net sales **26,223,032,454**, gross profit **3,258,947,633** — a
**12.43%** gross margin — and majority profit **1,881,950,266**, which is **more in six months
than the whole preceding financial year earned**. Every subtotal foots.

Most recent filing now read: reviewed consolidated financial statements, six months to
30 June 2026, limited review report attached. Also read for the first time in this pass: that
filing's cash-flow statement (depreciation 86,495,738; capital expenditure 139,109,418) and its
statement of financial position at 30 June 2026.

## BASE YEAR — does it foot to filed periods?

**It does now; it did not before.** The base year is the twelve months to 30 June 2026, built as
the audited transition half plus the reviewed half. Both halves are FILED. The product table
sums to **1,502,324.605 t** and **EGP 46,958,758,266**, which is the disclosed twelve-month
revenue **exactly** — so the solved price index the previous pass needed disappears entirely.
The cost stack is the two filed note 15-A stacks added, and foots to twelve months of disclosed
cost of sales.

Base-year gross margin **8.997% → 9.653%**. Nothing is annualised and nothing is solved.

**What the rejected coherence test got wrong:** it estimated the half's other income by
**doubling the March quarter's** 225,556,509, putting EGP 451mn where the filing shows
196,526,615. Other income is the most volatile line in this income statement and the least
suited to being doubled. Registered as **L-053**.

## MACRO COHERENCE — are the paths mutually consistent?

**THEY WERE NOT.** Three paths ran side by side for five years with no mechanism offered for the
gaps between them:

| leg | previous edition |
|---|---|
| pound-denominated costs | Egyptian inflation 14.5% → 9.5% |
| product realisation | 9.0% → 5.2% |
| the currency | 7.7% → 3.6% |

A country cannot run 14.5% domestic inflation against a currency sliding 4% a year without an
enormous real appreciation. On a company whose feedstock is **82% of revenue** and is priced off
the same barrel as its output, that incoherence alone manufactured the margin decline the
forecast then reported as a finding: gross margin 8.62% → 7.09%, EBIT **falling** 2,239 → 1,370
while revenue rose 27%.

**This is L-048** — *"a forecast scenario must be internally consistent, or it invents its own
bias"* — the lesson this name's own walk-forward had produced hours earlier, sitting unapplied in
the study being rebuilt. Both paths are now DERIVED from the one registered inflation series by
relative purchasing-power parity, with crude held flat in dollars. **Two free parameters removed,
not added.** EBIT now runs 2,809 → 3,070.

## DISCOUNT RATE — the right rate, and is the cash charged once?

**IT WAS CHARGED TWICE.** The operating cash flows were discounted at a cost of capital weighted
on NET debt. AMOC holds net cash, so the debt weight is **−26.2%**, the equity weight levers to
1.26, and the rate applied to the refinery came out at **31.19% — 374 basis points ABOVE the
27.45% cost of equity.** The bridge then added the same cash back at face.

Gross borrowings are **0.14%** of the capital structure at market value, so the unlevered rate is
the cost of equity to three decimals. The operations are now discounted at **27.45%** and the
cash added at face — one charge, not two. Registered as **L-054**.

## TERMINAL — coherent with the inflation inside its own discount rate?

**NO.** The terminal risk-free is built as the central bank's 7% target plus a 5.5% real rate =
**12.5%**, so the terminal discount rate embeds 7% inflation. Terminal growth was set at **5%
nominal** — a business shrinking about 2% a year in real terms **for ever**, on a plant serving a
population growing 1.6% a year. That assumption was never stated as one.

Terminal growth is now **7.0%: inflation only, zero real growth**, which is still the
conservative end. Terminal value is **54.2% of enterprise value** and that is disclosed
prominently rather than buried — it is high, and it is the main reason the far years carry a
wide range.

Cash rose over the half **after** paying 861,257,265 of dividends.

## CLAIMS AGAINST THE RECORD — does every "best ever" survive the filings?

**ONE DID NOT, AND IT WAS THE HEADLINE.** The study said the market price required *"a PERMANENT
gross margin near 12.2% — above the best single quarter this company has ever filed"*.

- The **12.2%** was typed into the headline, into section 1.14 and into Figure 2 as a hardcoded
  constant, and was never recomputed as the model moved. **Solved, it is 9.37%** — *below* the
  9.653% the company has just filed for twelve months.
- *"Above the best single quarter ever filed"* was false on the record. AMOC filed **13.84%** for
  the whole year to 30 June 2022 and **13.92%** in the quarter to 30 June 2026.

The figure is now solved by the model and read into all three places from one source.

## MULTIPLE CROSS-CHECK — what does the fair value imply?

Trailing twelve-month majority profit is **EGP 2,507,890,727**, so EPS is **1.94**.

| | at the old fair value 5.53 | at the new fair value 8.64 | at the market price 9.10 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Trailing price/earnings | **2.85×** | 4.45× | 4.69× |
| Ex-net-cash price/earnings | **1.65×** | 3.25× | 3.49× |

**A net-cash, dividend-paying refiner on 1.65× ex-cash earnings is not a valuation, it is a
symptom.** That cross-check alone should have stopped the first pass, and it is in this rule's
required list for that reason.

## What did NOT change

The walk-forward and its finding that the method does not beat "no change"; the conforming beta
(0.9080 against EGX30); the retirement of the skill verdict; and the **flat volume path** — the
filed twelve-month tonnage of 1,502,325 t is a better base than the annualised half it replaced,
and since the half to June 2026 annualises to 1,388,482 t, flat remains the *optimistic* case.

## What this review cost, and what it is worth

Every correction above is of the *the model was wrong* kind. None is of the *the company turned
out better than we thought* kind, and the distinction matters: a review that reaches its number
by finding reasons to be more generous has not audited anything. The test is whether each change
would still be right if the price moved tomorrow — all six would.
