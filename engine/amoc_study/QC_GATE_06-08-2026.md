## What failed on the first pass

The gate is only worth running if it can fail. Four things did.

**1. A column-offset bug in the revenue build (caught by gate (q)).** On the Product Legs sheet the
base year occupies column B and the forecast years C–G, but the forecast loops also started at B —
so each formula overwrote the base year and referenced itself. The recalculation gate reported 171
unresolvable formulas with circular references. Fixed by separating the column lists, and the class
of defect was closed structurally: every cross-sheet row reference is now written as a `@TOKEN@` and
resolved in one pass after all sheets are laid out, so a row number can no longer be hardcoded
before the sheet it points at exists.

**2. A genuine circularity in the cash-flow roll-forward (caught by gate (q)).** Net finance income
pointed at *closing* net cash, while closing net cash depends on finance income. The model computes
it on the *opening* balance; the workbook did not. Fixed, with an explicit opening-cash row added so
the reader can see which balance the yield is applied to.

**3. `Total assets` was a dead input (caught by gate (r)).** Property, plant and equipment was
pasted rather than derived from disclosed total assets, so the single largest disclosed balance-sheet
line drove nothing. This was fixed in the *model*, not the expectation: the calendar-2025 asset base
is now the residual against disclosed total assets as a live formula, the earlier years scale off it,
cash became the closing plug and equity a roll-back through disclosed profit and dividends. That
removed five dead inputs at once and eliminated every pasted number from the Balance Sheet sheet.

**4. EBITDA above gross profit — an arithmetic impossibility (caught by reading the rendered PDF).**
The first build closed the historical income statements *backwards* from reported profit. For
FY2022/23 that implied EBITDA of EGP 1,775mn against a **disclosed** gross profit of EGP 1,297mn,
i.e. a negative operating cost. The residual was not a cost at all: it was **other and non-operating
income** — EGP 760mn in FY2022/23, 442mn, 253mn, and effectively nothing by calendar 2025, the exact
profile of exchange gains on dollar export receivables washing out as the pound stabilised. The
historical years were rebuilt from the gross margin *down*, with that residual isolated on its own
line and an assertion added that EBITDA can never exceed gross profit in any period. The forecast now
carries **no** other income at all, which lowered the central fair value from EGP 9.69 to 9.46 and
turned a modelling error into the study's most useful single finding: it explains why revenue
compounded hard for three years while reported profit barely moved.

## Two driver expectations that were wrong, not the model

Per the standing rule that a failing driver test is first a wrong expectation:

- **`Total liabilities` did not move the cash-flow lens.** Correct — net working capital comes from
  days drivers and the asset base from total assets, so total liabilities drives *equity*, and
  therefore book value per share and the book lens. Re-asserted against those headlines; it moves
  book value per share −4.0% and the weighted central −0.4%.
- **`Dividend per share` did not move the forward model.** Correct — closing equity is disclosed, so
  the dividend drives the roll-*back*, not the roll-forward. A larger dividend means more was paid
  out of each historical year, so opening equity three years ago must have been **higher**. Asserted
  in that direction, +16.9% on FY2022/23 equity.

Two directions that *look* wrong and are not were asserted deliberately, because a model that got
them backwards would still look plausible: a **higher cost of debt raises** the cost of capital here,
and a **higher yield on cash lowers** it. Net debt is negative, so the debt weight is negative and
the cost of net debt has a negative denominator — two sign flips, one result. The unlevering identity
is asserted to recombine exactly to the cost of equity, which is what proves the weighting is a
decomposition rather than an adjustment.
