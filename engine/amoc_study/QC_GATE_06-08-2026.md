## What the second pass caught

The gate is only worth running if it can fail. Reading this render caught six things, three of
them substantive.

**1. The study claimed an 8.4% gap was corroboration.** §1.4 said "two independent routes agreeing
within 8.4% is the corroboration the old residual pretended to be". An 8.4% gap on the revenue line
is not agreement and calling it so repeats, in a new place, exactly the overclaim the rebuild was
meant to remove. Rewritten in three paragraphs that (i) state the line build is 8.4% too HIGH,
(ii) identify where the gap comes from — the disclosed table is the year to June 2024 at 1.433mn
tonnes against 1.362mn in calendar 2025, so volumes FELL over the bridge while the roll-forward
assumed they grew — and (iii) say plainly that the gap is closed by scaling, not by explanation,
that the filed halves therefore govern the LEVEL while the product table governs only the MIX, and
that an input the model will scale by 8.4% does not support three-significant-figure claims about
any single line.

**2. The retired residual check was still running in the company overview.** §1.4 announced that the
residual fuel price was gone and explained why a residual cannot corroborate its own construction —
while the "What it actually sells" caption, twelve pages earlier, still offered the implied fuel
realisation as "the check that tells us the split is real rather than fitted to a target". Two
sections of the same document contradicting each other on the same point. The caption is rewritten
to withdraw the claim explicitly and to state that the two legs shown there are an aggregation of
the three lines built in §1.4, not a separate estimate.

**3. "Both checks come in BELOW the adopted rate" was false.** §1.10 said it. Check (a), the
historical compound NOPAT rate, is **+34.8%** — far above the adopted 5%. The sentence was true of
the model as it stood before the margin rebuild and became false when the rebuild moved that rate
from +2.6% to +34.8%; it survived because the assertion guarding it tested only the adopted rate
against its ceiling and never against the checks. The same stale reading had propagated to the
crossover paragraph, which asserted that all three candidate rates sit below Egyptian nominal
growth — when the compute layer had already established that +34.8% is above it and DOES bind, at
about 36 years. Both paragraphs rewritten to report each candidate individually, the guard now
reports per candidate, and the waterfall-consistent reinvestment definition (19.1%, implying 7.4%
growth, ABOVE the adopted rate) is stated alongside the capex-only one rather than only in the
compute log.

**4. The enterprise-value bridge presented the rejected order.** The arithmetic had already been
corrected to deduct the minority from the OPERATING enterprise value before the cash is added —
EGP 300mn on EV 10,002, not 3% of the post-cash 12,440 — but the printed table still listed net
debt first, still labelled a row "equity value before minority interests", and still carried the
caption defending the old order. Table reordered to match the arithmetic; caption replaced with a
statement of what was wrong, why the old defence was algebraically inverted, and what the
correction is worth (EGP 0.06 a share). The workbook's EV Bridge sheet was reordered the same way
and given two live MEMO rows that compute what the rejected order would have produced, so the size
of the correction is on the face of the sheet rather than in a note.

**5. The price-record paragraph confused two thresholds and mis-stated the series start.** It said
the series "runs from 2022" — 2022 is where the calibration exercise starts scoring, while the
series runs from 2011-01-02, 3,754 clean sessions over 15.6 years — and it compared the largest
observed move to 0.290 while calling that "an exchange daily price limit". 0.290 is the engine's
artefact screen, set deliberately ABOVE the limit; the exchange's ±20% limit is 0.1823 in logs.
Both corrected, with the observation that the largest observed move (0.1813) sits inside the price
limit by four ten-thousandths, which is the actual evidence that no unadjusted corporate action is
hiding in the series. `step0.py` now emits the series bounds, raw and clean row counts, the maximum
move and the threshold, so none of these is retyped.

**6. A driver was dead against the probe set, and the probe set was wrong.** Making the expert panel
live introduced Expert 1's own justified multiple as a driver. The dead-input sweep flagged it,
because the sweep tested only whether an input moves the headline — and the expert panel is a
published cross-check, not the headline. First hypothesis applied: the expectation was wrong, not
the model. The probe set was extended to cover the panel, and three assertions added, including one
that is itself a finding — bumping Expert 1's multiple moves Expert 1 but leaves the panel MEDIAN
unmoved, because Expert 1 already sits above the median and the median is Expert 3, which is the
cash-flow lens by identity. The workbook now proves in one bump that its own panel median is not an
independent check.

in the bridge, the discounting of the normalised-earnings lens, the circularity in the relative
lens's anchor, and the 2026E rebasing question each have a priced set of branches recorded in

is implemented in the numbers above.
