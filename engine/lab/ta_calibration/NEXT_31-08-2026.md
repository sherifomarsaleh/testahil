# What next — the dead-bar finding, and how to land 93 libraries safely

31-Aug-2026.

## Done since the last pass: the density screen, and it found something

The protocol carries a standing rule — *screen a market's trading-day density
against that exchange's REAL calendar before trusting any fit built on it* — and
nothing in the repository executed it. `density_screen.py` now does, across all 93
libraries. The reference calendar is measured, not assumed: for each market and year
the denominator is the union of dates across every library in that market.

**The first cut of the screen was wrong and is worth recording.** It combined two
readings with an OR and flagged 37 of 93 names — almost all of them for having
listed mid-year. ADNOCDIST 2017, SALIK 2022 and LULU 2024 all read 0.05–0.25 density
with *every bar trading*. A screen that fires on every IPO is one everybody learns to
ignore. Split properly:

- **density** (sessions present / market sessions) — usually benign, and scored only
  on a name's *interior* years, never its first or last, which are partial by
  construction.
- **liveness** (share of present bars with High > Low) — **this is the reading that
  matters.** A bar with no range is a non-trading day wearing a session's clothes,
  and unlike a missing bar it enters every window, every pivot and every volatility
  estimate as a real observation of zero movement.

That took it from 37 names to **11**, and the 11 are real:

| name | dead years | worst liveness | share of interior history |
|---|---|---|---|
| EG/PHAR | 2012–2018 (7) | 0.536 | 50% |
| EG/RAYA | 2012–2020 (7) | 0.284 | 50% |
| EG/DSCW | 2013–2018 (5) | **0.000** | 38% |
| AE/MODON | 2018–2020 (3) | 0.273 | 38% |
| AE/IHC | 2012–2016 (3) | 0.034 | 21% |
| EG/ABUK | 2014–2016 (3) | 0.506 | 21% |
| EG/EFID, GBCO, BTFH, EGAL, LCSW | 1–2 each | 0.42–0.68 | 7–20% |

DSCW is the extreme: **1 session in 2016, 4 in 2015, 6 in 2014**, every one of them
a flat bar. It is a covered name with a live page.

Those windows feed the EG and AE market fits and the published band record — so
PHAR's and RAYA's public "over N resolved three-month forecasts" rests roughly half
on windows struck while the stock was barely trading.

**DSCW's zero is the important row, and it is luck, not design.** Its dead years are
excluded only because `MIN_HISTORY=260` happens to push its first origin past them.
That is exactly the pattern the protocol already recorded once: *"Break filtering was
never applied — it only appeared to be, because min_history=260 happens to push most
markets' first origin past their break anyway."* The same accident, in a second
place, protecting a name whose dead years sit early and protecting nobody whose dead
years sit late. MODON's dead years run to 2020 against a library starting 2017, and
it is contaminated.

This is a production defect, found by a screen that should already have existed. It
is a diagnostic, not yet a gate — turning any cut into a gate needs the promotion
rule and its own evidence.

## How to send the 93 libraries

`intake.py` validates a batch and **writes nothing**. Run it on an upload directory
before any file goes near `raw_ohlc/`. It resolves each file, runs Step 0.0,
diagnoses price vs total-return, and counts coverage against the libraries on disk.

**Three things make the difference between a clean landing and a silent mess:**

1. **Keep the vendor filenames.** `ADX_FAB_1D.csv`, `DFM_DLY_EMAAR_1D.csv`. That
   prefix is the one piece of provenance the library layout destroys — `raw_ohlc/`
   groups by *market*, and AE spans both ADX and DFM. The protocol says the exchange
   is read from the code prefix in `data.js` and never inferred from the folder;
   intake checks the filename against `data.js` and refuses on a disagreement.

2. **Send one market at a time, complete.** A market fit pools its whole panel, so a
   panel holding 6 total-return names and 22 price names is worse than either basis
   chosen consistently. Intake reports `partial — DO NOT CONVERT` until a market is
   whole. Order by size: **AE 28, EG 37, SA 13, then IN/KR/QA/US 3 each, XAU 2,
   XPT 1.**

3. **Tadawul symbols are numeric.** `data.js` carries `TADAWUL:2050` for SAVOLA and
   `TADAWUL:4142` for RIYADHCABLE, so a file named `TADAWUL_2050_1D.csv` resolves by
   code, not by name. Intake tries both and prints the resolution — this is the
   failure that once put ALRAJHI under raw file RAJHI.

On the seven files already held, intake returns: 5 total-return, 1 price (IHC, which
pays no dividend), 1 unreconciled (LULU — 45 distinct divergence levels across 444
rows, i.e. a dividend staircase, flagged rather than guessed because its final
ex-date falls inside the tail window).

## Sequence

1. **Now, no new data needed** — decide whether the dead-bar screen becomes a Step
   0.0 gate, and fix the RSI wording in `technicals.py` ("stretched" is followed by
   an up-rate 7.6pp *above* base).
2. **As each market lands** — intake, then convert that market whole, with
   `q_annual` = 0 in the same commit. Never the library without the carry change.
3. **Once all 93 are converted** — refit, and re-run this calibration on the new
   basis to see what moved.
4. **Then** build the per-name record: tape everywhere it is earned, trend where
   earned, levels never.

The technical read keeps the price series throughout — total-return levels are prices
at which nothing ever traded.

## CORRECTION (same day): the dead bars are not a defect, and must not be gated

I called the 106 contaminated windows "a production defect, found by a screen
that should already have existed." The screen was worth writing and the 106
windows are real, but the diagnosis was wrong and the proposed remedy would have
destroyed real market history.

**The flat bars carry volume.** Measured across the affected names:

| name | flat bars | with volume | no volume |
|---|---|---|---|
| EG/RAYA | 1272 | **1272** | 0 |
| EG/PHAR | 579 | **579** | 0 |
| EG/ABUK | 313 | 312 | 1 |
| AE/IHC | 317 | 301 | 16 |
| AE/MODON | 182 | **182** | 0 |
| EG/DSCW | 84 | 82 | 2 |

A flat bar with volume is not corruption. It is a real session in which a thinly
traded stock printed at one price all day — a genuine observation of zero
movement, and a fact about the stock rather than about the vendor. Step 0.0 drops
an interior bar only when it is flat **and** carries no volume, which is exactly
right: it removes non-trading placeholders and keeps real sessions.

**So this is a liquidity-regime finding, not a data-quality one.** PHAR, RAYA and
MODON were far less liquid in those years than they are now, and a cone fitted
across both eras mixes two different stocks. The protocol's instrument for that
is the structural break, not Step 0.0 — but `profile.breaks` is **per market**,
and an illiquid era in PHAR is a PHAR fact, not an EG fact. There is no per-name
break mechanism, and adding one is a research change that must clear the
promotion rule, not a gate to be switched on.

**Recommendation: do not gate it.** A Step 0.0 liveness gate would delete
thousands of real sessions on a misdiagnosis, and it would fail 11 names on day
one — the permanently-red check [R-ENF-02] warns about. Keep `density_screen.py`
as the standing diagnostic it is, and note in the same breath that under
[R-CAL-02] those windows belong in the published record anyway: every one is a
forecast that was really made and really resolved.

The general lesson is the one this repository keeps re-learning in new places: a
screen that fires is not the same as a defect that exists, and the first question
after a gate goes red is what the flagged rows actually are.
