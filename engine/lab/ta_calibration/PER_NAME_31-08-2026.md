# Per-name calibration — EMAAR, EMAARDEV, ENBD, FAB, IHC

Run 31-Aug-2026 on request. Research only; nothing adopted, published or spliced.
Re-run `per_name.py` rather than quoting these numbers.

Each name is scored twice, on two series that are **not the same data**:

- **production** — the persistent library at `engine/raw_ohlc/AE/{TICKER}.csv`, which
  is what the site actually publishes from.
- **upload** — the TradingView daily exports supplied 31-Aug-2026, scored through
  Step 0.0 without being written anywhere.

## The uploads are total-return series and must not be spliced

Under the roll-forward rule — *merge, never overwrite; verify overlapping dates match
to the 4th decimal, no back-adjustment* — four of the five **fail**:

| name | overlap rows | median rel. diff | rows matching to 4dp | last 90 days |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| EMAAR | 3873 | 29.4% | 2.6% | 0.000% |
| EMAARDEV | 2188 | 24.8% | 4.6% | 0.000% |
| ENBD | 3743 | 27.1% | 3.3% | 0.000% |
| FAB | 2255 | 20.1% | 4.9% | 0.000% |
| **IHC** | 2078 | **0.000%** | **100.0%** | 0.000% |

A 20–29% historical divergence that decays to exactly zero at the right-hand edge is
the signature of **dividend back-adjustment**: these are total-return series, not
price series. IHC agrees to the fourth decimal on all 2,078 overlapping rows because
it pays no dividend — for IHC the two are the same series and the upload is simply
shorter.

Splicing the other four would silently convert those names from a price series to a
total-return series, moving every cone, every level and every fitted parameter that
reads them. **Not done. It needs a deliberate decision, not a merge.**

## Two library defects the test surfaced

- **FAB carries 1,723 sessions of the wrong company.** FAB exists from the 2017
  NBAD/FGB merger; the library starts 02-Jan-2011 at 5.50, so everything before
  2018 is legacy NBAD filed under FAB. The upload starts 10-May-2017, correctly.
- **IHC carries 796 pre-2018 sessions of a near-untraded shell**, 27.8% of them flat
  bars (High == Low) at a median price of 1.49 against 372 today. Step 0.0 strips
  only a *leading* placeholder block, so most of these survive into the panel.

Both change this name's answer, below. Both are worth fixing regardless of what is
decided about the technical lens.

## Result 1 — per-name level records are not readable, and that is the finding

Break-through at the published level vs the distance-matched two-sided null,
conditional on both being reached. Production library:

| name | 1M n | 1M delta | 3M n | 3M delta | 3M verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| EMAAR | 28 | −0.018 | 64 | +0.039 | not robust |
| EMAARDEV | 26 | +0.039 | 45 | +0.011 | not robust |
| ENBD | 24 | +0.063 | 51 | +0.010 | not robust |
| FAB | 24 | +0.188 (robust +) | 63 | +0.159 | **robust +** |
| IHC | 34 | +0.059 | 52 | +0.010 | not robust |

24–64 paired observations per name per horizon. Two of ten cells clear the house bar,
and splitting by side leaves most cells below the 22-window floor `band_record.py`
derives for exactly this reason. **The level claim can be calibrated for the book and
for a market; it cannot be calibrated per name at this origin density.** The pooled
book-wide result (+3–4pp, 15 of 16 cells robust) is unaffected — it is the per-name
cut that fails, and it fails on power, not on sign.

**And the one robust per-name result does not survive the data.** FAB's +0.159 falls
to **+0.056, not robust**, on the upload — because the production figure rests on
1,723 sessions of NBAD. A per-name record would have published a confident number
about a company that did not exist for the first third of its own history.

## Result 2 — the trend clause is where the per-name signal actually lives

Forward up-rate by trend clause, against each name's own base rate. Both series:

| name | series | base (3M) | above the whole stack | below the whole stack |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| IHC | production | 0.573 | **0.830** (n=47) | **0.306** (n=36) |
| IHC | upload | 0.560 | **0.800** (n=35) | **0.235** (n=17) |
| EMAAR | production | 0.547 | 0.524 (n=63) | 0.471 (n=34) |
| EMAAR | upload | 0.599 | 0.606 (n=71) | 0.444 (n=27) |
| FAB | production | 0.563 | 0.517 (n=58) | 0.488 (n=43) |
| FAB | upload | 0.637 | 0.652 (n=23) | 0.600 (n=20) |
| EMAARDEV | production | 0.662 | 0.636 (n=33) | 0.750 (n=8) |
| **ENBD** | production | 0.621 | 0.556 (n=63) | **0.783** (n=23) |
| **ENBD** | upload | 0.683 | 0.638 (n=69) | **0.818** (n=22) |

Two things stand out, and both reproduce across two independently-sourced series:

- **IHC's trend clause separates by ~50 percentage points** and is the strongest
  per-name effect in this set. Read with the caution it deserves: IHC ran 1.33 → 372
  over the window, so this is close to one enormous secular trend observed once.
- **ENBD's points the wrong way.** Trading *below* the whole moving-average stack was
  followed by an up-move 78% (production) and 82% (upload) of the time, against a base
  of 62%/68% — i.e. the clause a reader takes as bearish has been this name's most
  bullish state. n is 22–23, too thin for a verdict, but it is a **watch flag** in the
  [R-DRIFT-02] sense: recorded, graded live, revisited at every refit, not acted on.

## Result 3 — the tape word holds up per name

Spearman(ATR% at origin, realized forward vol), production / upload:

| | EMAAR | EMAARDEV | ENBD | FAB | IHC |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1M | +0.40 / +0.37 | +0.18 / +0.23 | +0.40 / +0.50 | +0.40 / +0.33 | +0.81 / +0.73 |
| 3M | +0.24 / +0.25 | −0.08 / −0.01 | +0.42 / +0.46 | +0.41 / +0.21 | +0.81 / +0.73 |

Positive on nine of ten name-horizons and stable across the two data sources —
consistent with the pooled +0.63/+0.66. EMAARDEV at 3M is the one flat cell.
The tape word remains the most defensible statement the technical read makes.

## What follows

1. The per-name *level* record should not be built at this density. Either raise the
   origin density and re-derive a readability floor from the new counts, or publish
   the level record at market level and keep the per-name record to the trend and
   tape clauses, which are readable.
2. FAB's and IHC's libraries need a decision independent of this work.
3. The uploads need a decision: adopt total-return series book-wide, or keep price
   series. Not a merge either way.
