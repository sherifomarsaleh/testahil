## PART 2 — WHAT I INDEPENDENTLY TESTED, AND WHAT IT SETTLES

Four tests were run before any verdict was written. They decide fourteen of the forty-five rows.

### TEST A — Are the four calibration multipliers typed or derived? (decides #2, #3, #4)

The critique's premise is that `Assumptions!B142–B145` carry "four calibration multipliers
absent from the 212-row register", that they are "the four cells doing the most work in the
forecast", and that the remedy is to "register them, or delete them".

**Read from `compute.py` lines 1163–1190, they are DERIVED, not typed:**

    _cal_loc  = fy26_loc_implied / (model's own uncalibrated FY2026 local revenue)
    _cal_exp  = fy26_exp_implied / (model's own uncalibrated FY2026 export revenue)
    _cal_cost = fy26_cashcost_implied / BU[1]['cc']
    _svc_share_cal = fy26_svc_implied / (fy26_loc_implied + fy26_exp_implied)

Every numerator comes from the **reviewed H1-2026 statements**, scaled to a full year on
FY2025's own half-year split per channel. The code states why both legs are scaled together:

> *"Scaling the price legs onto a reviewed actual while leaving the cost stack on its own
> assumed path would manufacture a margin out of the calibration."*

**Verdict split:**
- **PREMISE TRUE.** The four quantities do not appear in the register. That is a real
  disclosure defect against this study's own claim that "every input carries a value, a
  source, a date and a research layer".
- **CONCLUSION WRONG.** They are not *inputs*. Deleting them — the critique's own remedy, and
  the basis of its −21.2% and +19.5% prices — would **un-calibrate the model from a reviewed
  filing**, which is precisely what this house's anchor rule forbids: a near-term reviewed
  actual outranks a stale full-year rate.
- **THE REAL FIX** is therefore neither of the critique's two options: register them as
  **derived quantities**, with the reviewed statements as their source, the scaling identity as
  their construction, and the date of the half they come from.

never typed**.
