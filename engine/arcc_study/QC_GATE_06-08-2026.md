## What revision 3 changes, and why

The forecast assumed local realised price growing **3.0%** in FY2026 against **11.5%** cost
inflation, and about 4% a year after that — a cumulative real squeeze near 28%, and the
source of the whole margin collapse to 24.5%. The statements that revision 2 had just read
contradict it:

| | FY2024 | FY2025 |
|---|---|---|
| Revenue growth | +44.5% | +42.6% |
| Total cash-cost growth | +41.8% | **+12.5%** |
| Gross margin | 23.9% | **40.6%** |

Q1-2026, reviewed: revenue **+17.3%** at a **42.9%** gross margin against 40.6% for FY2025 —
still WIDENING. In every period the accounts cover, price outran cost.

Two further defects compounded it. The assumption implied a producer unable to raise price
even at the central bank's own 7% medium-term target. And the same model had utilisation
RISING from 69.7% to 78.2% across the same window — volume gained and real price lost at
once, which is two conservatisms stacked rather than one judgement made.

**The correction.** The local price path is recalibrated to **8.0% / 9.0% / 8.0% / 7.0% /
6.5%**. It remains BELOW the cost path in every single year, and it produces FY2026 group
revenue growth of 10.7% against the 17.3% the first quarter actually ran, so it stays
conservative against the live evidence rather than against nothing.

**What was deliberately NOT changed.** The cost path stays at headline inflation
(11.5% → 7.0%) even though this company's own realised cost inflation ran below the national
rate, because crediting that here would double-count the alternative-fuel saving already
carried as a driver of its own — anchored to EGP 240mn of capacity under construction and a
EUR 25mn facility drawn, not to a trend.

## The assertion that failed, and what it turned out to mean

Revision 3 broke `growth destroys value at the terminal return on capital`. The first
hypothesis was that the EXPECTATION was wrong, not the model, and the decomposition confirmed
it.

Reinvestment is `g / ROIC`, and ROIC is itself `N(1+g) / IC` on a fixed replacement-cost base.
The reinvestment charge therefore collapses to a constant `g·IC` and the terminal block
reduces to

```
TV(g) = [ N(1+g) − g·IC ] / (W − g)        dTV/dg  ∝  N(1+W) − IC·W
```

The derivative **contains no g**. The direction of the growth lever is a constant of the
model, and the hurdle is `N/IC vs W/(1+W)` — NOT `ROIC vs W`. The two differ by exactly
`(1+g)/(1+W)`, because ROIC is measured on terminal-YEAR profit against a valuation-DATE
capital base.

| | Rev 2 | Rev 3 |
|---|---|---|
| Terminal NOPAT / IC | 8.22% | **13.81%** |
| Hurdle `W/(1+W)` | 13.61% | 13.61% |
| Verdict | growth DESTROYS value | growth ADDS value, by 21bp |
| Value across g = 3% → 7% | material | **EGP 68.56 → 68.92, +0.5%** |

Nothing was changed to force the old direction. The assertion now tests the model against its
own algebra — `analytic_adds_value == model_adds_value` — which is a real check in either
direction, and the driver test carries the same correction with the mechanism written into
the comment above it. The practical reading is that on a replacement-cost denominator this
plant roughly breaks even on new tonnes, so **no part of this valuation is bought with an
assumption about perpetual growth**.

## The cost of debt, restated plainly

7.54% is not a chosen rate. It is the balance-weighted blend of three disclosed facility
rates, and the euro weight is the reason it lands where it does:

| Facility | Currency | EGP mn | Weight | Contractual rate |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| CIB credit facilities | EGP | 99.92 | 8.8% | corridor + 0.60% = 20.60% |
| NBE / KfW | EUR | 145.74 | 12.8% | 6M Euribor + 3.00% = 5.10% |
| EBRD | EUR | 888.27 | 78.2% | 3M Euribor + 4.35% = 6.45% |
| Leases | EGP | 1.18 | 0.1% | 20.60% |
| **Blended** | | **1,135.11** | **100%** | **7.54%** |

The obvious objection — that a euro rate flatters an EGP-reporting borrower carrying the
devaluation risk — is answered as a computed value, not a footnote: the pound-equivalent
alternative under uncovered interest parity is **13.00%**, and it is worth −0.3% on the
cash-flow lens.

The 150bp integrity bound against the trailing effective rate is **NOT met** — the
contractual 7.54% sits above the FY2024 20.85%, FY2025 5.03% and Q1-2026 3.73% checks in
different directions — and the reason is stated rather than smoothed: the book re-based
mid-year from pound facilities to euro term debt, and interest on assets still under
construction is capitalised.

## The two judgements that drive the answer, stated plainly

**The terminal denominator.** Return on invested capital is
60.6% on the audited book and 14.5% on
replacement cost. The terminal block uses replacement cost. That leaves the plant roughly
breaking even on new tonnes (13.81% against a 13.61% hurdle), so four points of terminal
growth are worth 0.5% of value. On the book basis growth would be close to free and the
valuation materially higher. A reader who prefers the book basis should say why a plant
carried at a tenth of replacement cost is the right denominator.

**The margin path.** Local prices grow 44.9% across five years while pound costs grow 54.4%
— a real erosion of about 7%, against the 28% revision 2 assumed. The EBITDA margin falls
from the audited 39.3% to 34.3% by FY2030, still well above the 23.1% of FY2024. The first
quarter of 2026 ran a 42.9% gross margin against 40.6% for FY2025, so margins were still
expanding when the forecast assumes they turn — this remains the assumption to attack, and
the sensitivity is published: two points of margin is worth about EGP 5.70 a share.
