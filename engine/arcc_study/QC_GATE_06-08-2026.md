## New checks the audited accounts made possible

1. **The balance sheet closes to zero.** Total assets less total liabilities less equity and
   minorities = 0.000. Revision 1 could not run this check and disclosed a EGP 1,247mn gap
   between two competing liability prints instead.
2. **The reinvestment record is buildable.** Capital expenditure is disclosed for all three
   years, so the terminal reconciliation now carries capex, capex/EBITDA, net reinvestment,
   the reinvestment rate, book return on capital and implied growth per year, with each
   year characterised. FY2023 spent less than it depreciated; FY2024 and FY2025 carried the
   alternative-fuel and silo programmes.
3. **The kiln constraint.** Volume is built off cement capacity, so the kiln could bind
   first. At the audited clinker factor the FY2030 volume needs 3.284Mt of clinker against
   4.2Mt of kiln capacity — 78.2%, with 0.916Mt of headroom. The forecast fits the plant.
4. **The cost-of-debt integrity triple, from disclosure.** Currency composition
   (91.1% euro, facility by facility); independently computed effective
   rates of 20.85% (FY2024), 5.03% (FY2025) and
   3.73% (Q1-2026 annualised); and the bound. **The 150bp bound
   is NOT met** — the contractual 7.54% sits above all three — and the
   reason is stated rather than smoothed: the book re-based mid-year from pound facilities
   to euro term debt, and interest on assets still under construction is capitalised. The
   pound-equivalent alternative of 13.00% is published as a value.
5. **The share count is confirmed rather than triangulated.** 378,739,700 issued less
   3,872,255 treasury = 374,867,445, and the declared FY2025 dividend divides to the same
   count to 0.0000%.

## The two judgements that drive the answer, stated plainly

**The terminal denominator.** Return on invested capital is
60.6% on the audited book and 8.6% on
replacement cost. The terminal block uses replacement cost, which makes growth
value-destroying and lowers the valuation. The audited book carries a 2010-vintage plant at
about USD 12 per annual tonne against a USD 130 replacement cost. A reader who prefers the
book basis reaches a materially higher number and should say why a plant carried at a tenth
of replacement cost is the right denominator.

**The margin path.** Local prices are assumed to grow 21.0% across five years while pound
costs grow 54.4% — a real squeeze of about 28%. The EBITDA margin falls from the audited
39.3% to 24.5% by FY2030, roughly its FY2024 level. The first
quarter of 2026 ran a 42.9% gross margin against 40.6% for FY2025, i.e. margins were still
EXPANDING when the forecast assumes they turn. This is the assumption to attack, and the
margin sensitivity is published: two points of margin is worth about EGP 5.04 a share.
