## What changed in this pass

The projected income statement, balance sheet and cash flow, and the full
discounted-cash-flow waterfall, were added at the reader's request and are in
Appendix A and section 1.1. Building them surfaced three things worth recording:

1. **The 2025 wedge.** The audited balance sheet says working capital rose EGP
   20,084.7mn; the audited cash-flow statement implies EGP 3,146.2mn. The gap
   is 46.8% of revenue and cannot be split from three published totals. It is
   measured, registered as a gap, and the forecast is published both ways.

2. **The document published two different valuations.** The lens tables and the
   bridge came from the bottom-up units-and-prices model; the headline range,
   the sensitivity grid, the implied rate and the whole workbook came from a
   separate ten-year capacity-ratio model — EGP 9.17 to 38.74 in the headline
   against EGP 2.85 to 38.75 in the summary table two pages later. Everything
   now comes from the one bottom-up model, and the recalculation asserts that
   the workbook, the document and the projected statements agree to the cent.
   The independent recalculation of the DELIVERED workbook is what caught it.

3. **Two checks were auditing something other than what shipped.** The column
   audit read cell widths the renderer ignores, and the figure check inspected
   figures nothing rebuilt. Both are fixed at the class rather than the
   instance: the audit now reads the grid the renderer reads and refuses
   adjacent tables, and the gate rebuilds every figure before checking it and
   fails on any figure it did not rebuild.
