## What blocks delivery

**(F1) The document, the workbook and Figure 1 date the new price to the old day, and the study contradicts itself about it.**
Three delivered surfaces say "the closing price on **23 August 2026** was EGP 14.40" — the
Figure 1 caption, the Valuation summary row "Market price, 23 Aug 2026 | 14.40", and the
Figure 1 legend "close 14.40 (23 Aug 2026)". The workbook's Summary sheet says the same.
EGP 14.40 is the **3 September** close; the 23 August close was **15.20**, and §2 of the same
document says so four pages later: *"The shares closed at EGP 15.20 on 23 August 2026."* The
date is hard-typed in five builder sites — `build_xlsx_phdc.py:136`, `docx_phdc.py:366`,
`docx_phdc.py:824`, `docx_phdc.py:842`, `figures.py:89` — while `inputs.py:153` records the
spot correctly as the 3 September close. [R-GAP-01 AMENDED] requires the published spot to
carry **its** date beside it.

**(F2) The delivered document publishes the ERP basis this edition retired.**
The Headline states *"The company's cost of capital, built from the ground up, is **26.10%**
… that was corrected on 30 August 2026 and the rate is **unchanged here**"* — both clauses
false. 26.0967% is the rating-basis rate; the model discounts at 25.1085%. The superseded
rate reappears in §1.2 ("a cost of equity of **31.3%**"), §1.4 ("the cost of equity of
**31.26%**"), Expert 3's working ("holding the cost of capital at **26.10%**… Named
sensitivity: at a 25.11% cost of capital…" — the adopted rate demoted to a sensitivity),
C.5 ("All three accept … the **26.10%** cost of capital"), C.6's divergence table (26.10%
twice) and B.2's risk register ("cost of capital above **26.10%**", pointing at a
sensitivity grid whose columns run 21.11%–29.11%). §1.8 publishes both columns correctly and
its caption says only *"Both bases are published because both are defensible"* — **it never
says which one is adopted**. The bibliography's judgements table states the discount rate is

## The direction of this edition, which is what the answer row is for

The central sits **+23.9% ABOVE** the traded price, and **both** of this edition's corrections
raised it: the ERP basis was worth **+2.2%** (17.46 → 17.85) and the lower market
capitalisation's effect on the equity weight **+1.8%**, a net edition move of **+4.06%** with
no business driver touched. That is a pattern worth being sceptical of, and three separate
instruments were pointed at it:

1. **The sign test says there is no lean, and the split is real.** Five material judgements,
   two resolved upward and three downward, p = 1.00. The three downward ones are large — the
   fifteen-year window cost 9.36 a share against the five-year alternative, the terminal
   growth 3.85, the fading delivery path 14.74. A study that wanted a high number had three
   cheap ways to get one and took none of them. **The record is stale, though**: it is stamped
   at the previous edition and every adopted value in it is the previous central.
2. **The gap review is honest about the direction and says so in its own voice**: *"both of
   them raised it — a direction this review is obliged to be more sceptical about, not less"*,
   and it records why the ERP switch was made in the direction that hurts: *"The identical
   switch on EGCH earlier today moved that study toward the market. Correcting the one that
   helps and deferring the one that hurts is exactly the lean [R-ENF-05]'s sign test exists to
   measure."* That reasoning is sound and it is the strongest thing in this delivery.
3. **The delivered document tells a reader none of it, and tells them the opposite.** No
   delivered surface mentions the re-strike or the ERP conformance. READ FIRST says the
   discount rate is unchanged; the Headline says the rate "is unchanged here" and names the
   **retired** 26.10% as the company's cost of capital; the bibliography's judgements table
   says the discount rate is "unchanged in this edition". The gap review — the one artefact
   that states the direction — is internal and a reader never sees it. And the only framing of
   the disagreement the document publishes is the summary table's **"vs the central −19%"**;
   the **+23.9%** the rule measures appears nowhere in any delivered file. Under the
   dual-framing rule both belong in the same table.

So: the *decision* was made in the right direction and audited properly; the *disclosure* of
it was not made at all. On the specific question of whether a reader can see the direction of
this study's own corrections — **no**, and what they can see is a denial that any correction
happened.

## What the filings disclose that the model does not consume

Asked explicitly, against the primary-documents table and the fetch log, rather than inferred
from the work already done.

- **Cost of revenue and units delivered are both disclosed, and cost per delivered unit is
  therefore constructible — and is not constructed.** The study computes it once (FY2024,
  EGP 8.9186mn) and then discards it, back-solving cost from a fixed margin for all fifteen
  years. It even measures the real drift the filings imply (`cost_drift_measured_not_carried
  = +9.65%`) and carries zero. That is defensible as a forecasting choice under
  [R-ANCHOR-01] and indefensible as a *construction*: the cost side is not built.
- **Regional new sales, units sold and price per unit are disclosed for three regions and
  five years** (`bottom_up.regions`, reconciled to the group total in code) and drive the
  order-book roll — but revenue is driven by units *delivered* through a single blended
  revenue-per-delivered-unit, so the regional price detail the company publishes never
  reaches the revenue line.
- **The 4 February 2026 securitisation discloses four tranches with sizes, tenors and
  national-scale ratings.** The study uses none of it because no coupon is published, and
  records that as a gap — correctly. But the tranche *sizes and tenors* are a disclosed
  maturity profile that the debt stack does not consume.
- **The half-year 2026 filing.** The study states it was not published as at its information
  date. I re-checked the fetch log (89 attempts, latest 30-Aug-2026) and the gap review
  re-checked the claim on 3 September; it still holds. **This is the single most likely thing
  to overturn this edition**, and it is named as such in §7.
- **89 successful primary-document fetches sit in `engine/phdc_walkforward/fetch_attempts.json`
  with sha256 per file**, covering the company's disclosure back to 1Q2015. The study
  consumes three fiscal years of it. The rest is available to the walk-forward and unused by
  the valuation.
