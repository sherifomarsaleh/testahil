### The driver assertions that failed on the rebuilt model, and what each decomposition showed

Six assertions failed on the rebuilt workbook. Every one was decomposed before anything was
changed, and in every case the expectation was wrong and the model was right — which is the
outcome the standing rule anticipates and the reason the rule exists.

**1. FY2026 revenue no longer moves for any unit driver.** Asserted UP for the day-rate
escalator, for rig counts and for the unconventional path. All three returned exactly zero. The
cause is the segment reconciliation adopted in this revision: each segment's unit build is
reconciled to the company's own FY2026 segment guidance, so the correction factor absorbs
whatever the FY2026 build would otherwise have done. **The unit rates set the growth path; the
guidance sets the FY2026 level.** Every such assertion is now written against FY2030, and a
matching assertion that FY2026 must NOT move is written beside it. The size of what the guidance
is carrying is published in §1.6 rather than left implicit.

**2. A better unconventional EBITDA margin LOWERS group EBITDA.** Asserted up. A higher assumed
margin means the unconventional programme carried less of FY2025's reported direct cost, which
leaves more of that reported cost in the conventional stack — and the conventional stack is
escalated on a growing rig fleet while the unconventional book runs off. The model was right.

**3. Acquired minority interests do not move the valuation.** Asserted down. They reduce equity
attributable to owners on the forecast balance sheet, which they do and is now asserted, but the
bridge deducts the put liability over those same interests and not the minority as well — so a
zero here is the correction working, not a dead input. Both directions are now asserted.

**4. Borrowings assumed with the acquisitions do not move the discounted-cash-flow lens.**
Asserted down. Free cash flow to the firm is struck before financing and the bridge deducts the
borrowings actually reported at 30 June 2026. They move forecast cash, which is now what is
asserted.

**5. A richer FY2025 onshore rate slightly LOWERS FY2030 revenue.** Asserted up. With FY2026
pinned, all that survives is a mix shift inside the reconciled onshore segment, away from the
faster-growing regional book. 200,000 on the FY2025 base moves FY2030 by 3,151 — the magnitude
says the same thing the sign does.

**6. Adding rigs to the 2026 year end alone LOWERS FY2030.** Asserted up. Moving one year of an
absolute fleet schedule while leaving the rest makes the FY2026 build larger against a fixed
guided level, so the correction factor shrinks and later years come out lower. The schedule is
meant to move together; a matching case that bumps the 2030 year end is now asserted, and it
raises FY2030 as expected.

A seventh failure was not an expectation problem and is recorded separately, because it changed
the model: raising the working-capital ratio was asserted to lower the valuation and RAISED it,
because it flipped the sign of terminal net debt and switched the terminal capitalisation rate.
That is the discontinuity that caused the terminal-rate finding to be reversed — see REVISION 2
above.

### The two driver assertions that failed on the FIRST edition (retained for the record)

Both were decomposed before anything was touched, on the standing rule that the first
hypothesis is that the expectation is wrong, not the model.

**1. Depreciation rate +1 percentage point.** Asserted to RAISE the valuation, because
`FCFF = EBIT×(1−t) + D&A − capex − ΔWC`, so extra depreciation leaves EBIT and returns as a
non-cash add-back, leaving only the tax shield. It failed. Decomposition:

| | base | +1pp | change |
|---|---|---|---|
| PV of the explicit five years | 6,539,164 | 6,555,938 | **+16,774** |
| PV of the terminal value | 19,457,778 | 19,107,510 | **−350,268** |
| Enterprise value | 25,996,942 | 25,663,448 | −333,494 |

The reasoning was right about the explicit window — the gain is +16.8m, exactly 9% of the extra
depreciation, which is the tax rate — and wrong about the headline. The terminal value is
capitalised off terminal-year NOPAT, which is struck *after* depreciation, so a permanently
heavier charge is a permanently less profitable business, and at 75% of enterprise value the
terminal block swamps five years of tax shield. **The model was correct.** Both effects are now
asserted separately.

**2. Lens weight +10 points on the relative lens.** Asserted to LOWER the weighted central,
since the relative lens sits below it. It failed, and the decomposition was arithmetic: the
central was a bare sum of value × weight, so adding 0.10 to a weight added 0.10 × AED 4.06 =
AED 0.406 — precisely the move observed — instead of re-weighting. **That one was a real defect
in the workbook, not a wrong expectation**, and it was fixed at source: the central now divides
by the sum of the weights. At the delivered weights, which sum to one, the fix changes no
delivered number.

## Not done, and why

**Not published.** The user asked explicitly for the study and the workbook only, and said
publication would be requested separately. Nothing was written to the live site: no ticker page,
no ledger row, no entry in the calibration registry, and `ADNOCDRILL` has **not** been added to
the UAE calibration panel — `fitted_configs.json` and `market_profiles.py` are untouched, and
`step0.py` asserts the name is absent from the panel as its own precondition for treating this
as new coverage.

**The price file is deliberately NOT in the shared library.** Repository convention places a
covered stock's prices at `engine/raw_ohlc/{MARKET}/{TICKER}.csv`, but any push touching that
path fires the continuous-calibration workflow, which refits the whole UAE market and commits
the result — a publication action. The price file therefore lives with the study as
`ADNOCDRILL_Stock_Price_History.csv`, exactly as the reference study carries its own, and every
script reads it from there. `technicals_run.py` stages a copy into the library only for the
duration of the technical read and removes it again. Moving it into the library permanently is
the first step of publishing, not of building.
