# ELEC — valuation-gap review, 10 September 2026

AUDITED CENTRAL: 0.3229

The central reads **EGP 0.3229** against a latest known close of **EGP 2.08**, a gap of
**−84.5%**. This is the widest disagreement in the book and it has been for weeks.

**THE CONCLUSION OF THIS REVIEW IS THAT THE GAP IS NOT A VALUATION JUDGEMENT.** It is an
artefact of a company whose consolidated accounts cannot be obtained, and the study's own
sourcing section has said so since 5 September. What is new today is that the mechanism
has been traced from the missing accounts through to the number.

## What changed today, and why it moved the answer DOWN

The study was wired to the house Egyptian macro path, which it had never read.

**The terminal risk-free rate was right by accident.** It typed 10.5% and justified it as
"the CBE's Q4-2028 inflation target of 5% plus a ~5.5pp emerging-market real-rate
convention". Both components are wrong against the house path, which holds 7.0% inflation
and, from today, a 3.5% structural real rate — **and they cancel exactly: 5 + 5.5 = 7 +
3.5 = 10.5.** A number correct for two offsetting wrong reasons survives every check that
looks at the number. It is now read from the path and cannot drift.

**The terminal growth was a real decline.** It typed 5.0% as a "standing convention for
established Egyptian names", naming no inflation rate at all. Against the house path's 7.0%
that is a real contraction of about 1.9% a year in perpetuity. It is now derived at zero
stated real growth, giving 7.0%.

A study-local assert refused the correction — it tested `g_term == 0.05`, pinning the
ANSWER rather than the procedure, so it enforced the very convention that was the defect.
It has been RE-POINTED, not widened: it now requires that the rate reproduce from the house
path's own function at a stated real rate, which is what the rule actually demands.

**Raising terminal growth from 5.0% to 7.0% LOWERED the value, from 0.3357 to 0.3229**, and
the reason is the whole finding:

> terminal ROIC 9.2% vs terminal WACC 15.00% — spread NEGATIVE: growth subtracts value

## Why the return on capital is 9.2%, and why that is the answer

ELEC's disclosed operating margins are not weak. FY2025 revenue 10,819 with EBIT 2,796 — a
25.8% operating margin, on 30.0% in FY2023 and 24.7% in FY2024. A business earning 26% at
the operating line does not normally earn 9.2% on capital.

It earns 9.2% here because of one input:

| | ELEC, this study | SWDY, the listed peer |
|---|---|---|
| Net working capital, % of revenue | **112%**, gliding to 88% | **19.7%** |

At 112% of revenue, working capital is **12,117** against a total balance sheet of
**16,460** — **74% of every asset the company owns**, and more than fifteen months of sales
tied up in inventory and receivables. It is 5.7 times the intensity of the largest listed
Egyptian cable maker.

That single figure does two things, both of which push the answer down and neither of which
is visible as an assumption about value: it inflates invested capital so the return on it
falls below the cost of capital, which makes growth subtract value rather than add it; and
it absorbs cash through the forecast, so free cash flow is thin regardless of the margin.

**The input is sourced as "FY25 intensity ~113% of revenue (triangulated balance sheet)".**
Triangulated, because it could not be read.

## The sourcing, restated because it is the finding rather than a caveat

From the study's own escalation record, re-run on 5 September against the correct host by
enumerating the filing index rather than counting it: of 61 linked statement files, **every
statement from mid-2021 onward is STANDALONE**; the most recent carrying the CONSOLIDATED
marker is **30 September 2020**; and **every consolidated statement without exception sits
on the dead legacy host**, 42 of the 61 links, while the 19 reachable files are all
standalone. The index ends at 30 September 2025 — no FY2025 annual, no Q1-2026, no H1-2026.

Every income and balance-sheet figure this study uses is consolidated. **The issuer has
served no consolidated statement in five years and none of the ones it lists can be
fetched.**

So the working-capital intensity that decides the answer is a reconstruction from accounts
nobody can open. That is not a defect in the arithmetic and it is not a view about the
company. It is the absence of the evidence a valuation needs.

## Headings 1-8

### 1. LATEST FILINGS
The most recent obtainable consolidated statement predates the current capital structure by
five years. Standalone statements to 30 September 2025 are reachable and are NOT a
substitute: this is a group with subsidiaries and the study values the group.

### 2. BASE YEAR
FY2025 is triangulated rather than filed. Revenue, EBIT and net profit are reconstructed;
the balance sheet is carried as a single total-assets line (16,460) with no committed
breakdown. **This is the heading that fails, and everything below inherits it.**

### 3. MACRO COHERENCE
Fixed today and now genuinely coherent. The study carries no macro view of its own:
terminal risk-free and terminal growth both derive from the one dated house path and are
struck against the same 7.0% inflation. Previously it held two different inflation rates
four rows apart and the error cancelled.

### 4. DISCOUNT RATE
Country risk enters once. Beta 0.9640 against the exchange index. Terminal WACC 15.00%,
inside the 14.2–15.1% band the region's leading house prices Egyptian terminals at, so the
discount rate is NOT what makes this answer an outlier.

### 5. TERMINAL
Terminal risk-free 10.50% = 7.0% house inflation + 3.5% structural real rate. Terminal
growth 7.0% at zero stated real growth. The spread over growth is 8.0 points, in line with
the rest of the book. **The terminal is not the problem either.**

### 6. BALANCE SHEET
The problem, and it cannot be closed from what is obtainable. One committed line — total
assets — against which a working-capital intensity of 112% of revenue is asserted, being
74% of that line. No inventory, receivable, payable or debt breakdown is held.

### 7. CLAIMS AGAINST THE RECORD
The study's "triangulated" label is honest and was already there. What was NOT stated is
that the triangulated figure is the single largest driver of the answer. It is stated now.

### 8. MULTIPLE CROSS-CHECK
At 0.3229 the study implies about 1.4x FY2025 reconstructed attributable earnings; the
market at 2.08 pays about 8.9x. The book lens reads 0.845 and the normalised-earnings lens
0.699 — **both more than double the cash-flow central**, and both far less dependent on the
working-capital line. That the two lenses least exposed to the unverifiable input sit
highest is itself evidence about where the answer comes from.

## Conclusion

The −84.5% is not this house disagreeing with the market about Elsewedy Electric's smaller
listed peer. It is a cash-flow model resting on a working-capital figure reconstructed from
accounts that have not been published in consolidated form since 2020.

**What would settle it:** any consolidated balance sheet from 2021 onward, from any
obtainable source. A single one would replace the triangulation and either confirm the
intensity or remove the entire gap.

**What should NOT happen:** the working-capital assumption should not be moved toward a
level that produces a friendlier answer. It is unverified in both directions, and 19.7%
would be as unsupported as 112%. The honest position is that this study's cash-flow lens
cannot be relied upon at the precision the other studies carry, and that its book and
normalised lenses — which do not lean on the missing line — read 0.845 and 0.699.
