# ELEC — the blend averages three floors, and one of them stands in for "worthless"

**Dated 6 September 2026.** Verified by hand against the study's own committed numbers and
its own builder. Every figure below reproduces exactly.

---

## First, what the study actually says, because it says it honestly

`compute.py:382-388`:

```
eq_dcf_unfloored = ev - net_debt - nci
# LIMITED LIABILITY: the equity of a listed company cannot be worth less than zero.
# The unfloored intrinsic (negative here — the EV does not cover the net debt) is
# DISCLOSED in the bridge; the lens carries a nominal floor as pure option value.
eq_dcf = max(eq_dcf_unfloored, 0.0)
dcf_ps  = max(eq_dcf / SH, 0.01)
```

| | EGP mn |

| enterprise value | 3,813.4 |
| net debt | 9,805.0 |
| **unfloored equity** | **−5,991.6** |
| **unfloored per share** | **−1.8082** |

**The cash-flow lens says this equity is worth nothing** — the debt exceeds the enterprise
value by six billion pounds. Limited liability floors it at zero and the lens carries **0.01**
as nominal option value. The study discloses all of this in its bridge. Nothing here is
hidden and nothing here is careless.

## The defect is what happens next

`weights` = `{dcf 0.40, relative 0.20, normalized 0.20, book 0.20}`, and the published central
reproduces from them with a residual of **exactly zero in all three scenarios**:

| scenario | blend | published | residual |
|---|---|---|---|
| bear | 0.1825753729383991 | 0.1825753729383991 | 0.0e+00 |
| base | 0.3357186228503667 | 0.3357186228503667 | 0.0e+00 |
| bull | 0.9488142905842855 | 0.9488142905842855 | 0.0e+00 |

Now look at what is being averaged, base case, against a latest price of EGP 2.08:

| lens | base | vs price | what it is |
|---|---|---|---|
| `dcf` | 0.0100 | −99.5% | **a floor.** `max(eq/SH, 0.01)`, standing in for a negative intrinsic |
| `relative` | 0.0500 | −97.6% | **a floor.** `max(..., 0.05)`, and it is 0.05 in bear, base AND bull |
| `normalized` | 0.6994 | −66.4% | a valuation |
| `book` | 0.9092 | −56.3% | **a floor.** [R-LENS-03] names book a disclosed floor |
| **blend** | **0.3357** | **−83.9%** | |

**THREE OF THE FOUR INPUTS ARE FLOORS, AND TWO ARE LITERAL `max()` CLAMPS THE STUDY ITSELF
LABELS AS FLOORS.** The relative lens returning 0.0500 in every scenario is the tell: a lens
that gives the same answer in the bear case and the bull case is not computing a value, it is
reporting a bound.

**A weighted mean of bounds is neither a bound nor an estimate.** 0.3357 is not what the cash
flows say (nothing), not what the book says (0.91), and not a probability-weighted mixture of
anything — it describes no state of the world. Averaging a floor is a category error, because
a floor is a *constraint on* a value rather than a *reading of* one.

## This corrects the framing this study was picked out under

ELEC was selected as one of four names carrying the book's entire negative mean, on the
hypothesis that the tail is where house pessimism lives. **On this name that hypothesis is
wrong, and in the interesting direction:** the cash-flow lens alone reads **−1.81 per share**,
so retiring the blend and taking the class primary — which is what [R-LENS-03] requires —
makes the published answer *far more negative*, not less.

ELEC is not an over-pessimistic valuation. It is a company whose enterprise value does not
cover its debt, and whose published −83.9% is the arithmetic of averaging that fact against
three-fifths of a book value. **The disagreement with the market is real and it is about
leverage, not about method.**

Whether the market at 2.08 is paying for an option this model floors at 0.01, or whether the
model's net debt or enterprise value is wrong, is the question the eight headings should ask
here — and neither answer is reached by adjusting a weight.

## CORRECTION, later the same day — "about leverage, not method" was wrong

The section above concluded: *"ELEC is not an over-pessimistic valuation. It is a company whose
enterprise value does not cover its debt... The disagreement with the market is about leverage,
not about method."* **The reverse read, computed since, contradicts that.**

`diagnostics_elec.py` re-runs this study's own `compute.py` with one driver moved at a time —
202 sandbox runs behind the record — and solves the conversion EBITDA per tonne, the unit rate
the whole forecast is built on, margin being an output of it:

| | terminal EBITDA margin |
|---|---|
| implied by the traded EGP 2.08, on the published four-lens central | **28.34%** |
| implied on the cash-flow lens alone (what [R-LENS-03] makes the central for this class) | 27.17% |
| **this study forecasts** | **12.30%** |
| **this company's own reconstructed filed record** | **25.33% – 30.68%** |

**THE MARKET IS PAYING FOR A MARGIN SQUARELY INSIDE THE RANGE THIS COMPANY HAS ACTUALLY
PRINTED, AND THIS STUDY FORECASTS LESS THAN HALF OF THE LOWEST FILED YEAR.**

So the leverage reading was downstream of the thing that matters rather than the thing itself.
The enterprise value fails to cover the debt **because** the terminal margin is set at 12.30%;
put the filed range in and the arithmetic that produced −1.8082 a share does not survive. That
is [R-ANCHOR-01] territory — a rate forecast far below the filed record — and it was invisible
from the floors, because a floor tells you the answer hit a bound and never why.

**Two quantities are recorded as NOT REACHABLE, with their reasons**, which is the half a
weaker record would have forced: terminal growth cannot reach the price at any value, because
the terminal return on capital of 9.17% sits below the terminal cost of capital of 15.00%, so
growth destroys value and the gradient inverts by construction; and working-capital intensity
cannot either, since reaching the price needs 5% of revenue against an FY2024 level near 76%.

**What this does NOT overturn:** the blend still averages three floors, book still carries 20%
where the rule forbids any weight, and the relative lens still returns 0.0500 in bear, base and
bull. Those are separate defects and they stand.

## The rule-extension candidate, stated but NOT adopted

[R-LENS-03] says **book value is a disclosed floor, published as such and never weighted**.
ELEC weights **two further floors** that the rule does not name, both of them explicit
`max()` clamps.

The candidate extension is: **no floor of any kind enters a weighted answer — not book, not a
limited-liability clamp, not a lens pinned to its own minimum.** A lens returning an identical
figure across bear, base and bull is reporting a bound and is detectable as one.

That is a rule amendment, which belongs in both governing documents in one commit with an
identifier and a negative control. It is recorded here as a candidate with its arithmetic, not
adopted in passing.

**Nothing is corrected in this pass.** Retiring this blend moves the answer — a long way, and
downward — which makes it a re-issue with a rebuild ledger [R-REBUILD-01].
