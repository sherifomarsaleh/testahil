Capital Restructuring and Equity Rights Expansion
On August 9, 2026, the Board of Directors convened and unanimously approved a capital restructuring plan designed to reduce short-term balance sheet debt and finance operational working capital4. The formal disclosure, transmitted to the Egyptian Exchange on August 16, 2026, authorized a capital increase structured as an equity rights offering to existing shareholders4.
This capital expansion addresses severe liquidity and financing pressures4. High domestic interest rates elevated the group's net debt service obligations, while extended collection cycles in domestic infrastructure projects tied up working capital18. By raising EGP 650 million in nominal equity from its existing shareholder base, management seeks to stabilize operating liquidity without incurring additional floating-rate bank credit facilities4.
Prior to the rights issue announcement, Electro Cable Egypt held its Ordinary General Assembly on Tuesday, May 5, 202628. Assembly voting records confirm that shareholders approved the board's recommendation to roll over the full distributable profit share for the 2025 financial year into retained earnings, explicitly bypassing a cash dividend distribution5. This decision preserved internal cash balances to support ongoing industrial production requirements5.

Industrial Feedstock Dynamics and Metallurgical Trade Protections
Direct raw materials account for over 70% of finished cable manufacturing costs, making cathode copper and aluminium rod spot prices the primary external determinants of gross margins40. The regulatory trade environment governing nonferrous scrap and ingot flows remains critical to preserving local production spreads14.

Global Nonferrous Markets (LME Spot)
  ├── Cathode Copper & High-Grade Ingot

Domestic Trade & Scrap Policies (Ministry of Trade & Industry)
  └── Scrap Export Tariff Enforcement (رسم صادر)
        ├── Halts Outflow of Copper & Aluminium Scrap
        └── Preserves Low-Cost Feedstock for Local Secondary Smelting

Factory Processing & Localization (Mostorod & Abu Rawash Complexes)
  ├── 80% to 90% Domestic Value Addition on Telecom Lines
  └── Final Conversion into High-, Medium-, and Low-Voltage Cabling
The company operates primary drawing, annealing, and insulating complexes in Mostorod and Abu Rawash, capable of processing cathode copper and secondary aluminium scrap into bare overhead conductors and insulated distribution cables40. Primary domestic aluminium ingot is procured from state-owned Egypt Aluminium (EGAL) under commercial contracts linked to London Metal Exchange (LME) spot rates40. In parallel, import substitution initiatives across secondary telecommunication fittings lifted local component integration to between 80% and 90%, mitigating direct foreign-currency conversion costs for polymers and PVC insulation12.
The Ministry of Trade and Industry maintains protective export duties on metal scrap (رسم صادر على خام وخردة النحاس والألمنيوم)14. This regulatory framework levies restrictive tariffs on unworked copper and aluminium scrap exports, preventing scrap metal from leaving Egypt14. Local cable producers, including Electro Cable Egypt, utilize this supply of secondary scrap to produce commercial-grade wiring and low-voltage conductors below international LME scrap import equivalents, protecting operational spreads14. Customs and foreign trade circulars issued through July and August 2026 maintained these metal scrap duties while adjusting independent duties on nitrogenous chemical fertilizers42.
