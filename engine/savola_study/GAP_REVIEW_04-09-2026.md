# SAVOLA — gap review, 4 September 2026

**AUDITED CENTRAL: 26.6424** — SAR per share.
**AUDITED GAP: −12.1%** against the latest known price, SAR 30.30 (Tadawul close of
3 September 2026, supplied and committed; the study is re-struck on it).

This fires the BELOW-price half of the audit trigger, which is also the half that HOLDS a
study from publication. SAVOLA does not publish while this stands, and this review is
written to find our defect first rather than to explain the market's.

## The finding that comes before the eight headings

**The disagreement is new, and almost none of it came from the model.** The prior edition
published 27.2426 against a settled 18-August close of 25.40 — inside the band, +7.3%, and
owing no review at all. The stock has since risen 19.3% to 30.30 while three rebuild levers
moved the central by half a per cent in total:

| Lever | Rule | Before | After | Move |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| terminal growth stored as a real rate on the house Saudi path | macro coherence | 27.2426 | 27.4379 | +0.7% |
| terminal built through the sanctioned module on a derived asset life | terminal construction | 27.4379 | 27.2811 | −0.6% |
| re-struck on the latest known price | this rule | 27.2811 | 27.1153 | −0.6% |

**The terminal construction changed completely and the terminal value moved 0.9%.** That is
worth stating plainly because it is the opposite of the recent pattern: the two terminal
levers point opposite ways and very nearly cancel. Storing growth on the house inflation
path RAISES the rate from 2.50% to 2.71%; charging maintenance at what replacement actually
costs LOWERS the terminal by slightly more. On this name the retired construction happened
to land near the right answer for the wrong reasons, and nothing but doing the arithmetic
would have shown that.

So the honest summary is: **the model did not become more pessimistic; the price moved.**
That does not make the model right, and the eight headings below are where it is tested.

## 1. LATEST FILINGS

Every disclosed period is read and cited to the filing: audited statements FY2023, FY2024
and FY2025, the reviewed Q1-2026 and Q2-2026 interims, the H1-2026 earnings release, the
FY2025 annual report and both investor presentations. The balance-sheet legs used for the
cost-of-capital weights and the book lens are the reviewed 30-June-2026 figures; the
enterprise-to-equity bridge stays dated 31-December-2025 and says so.

No filing published since the prior edition has gone unread, and no unread filing sits
behind the gap. What DOES sit behind the gap is sixteen sessions of price movement, which
no filing explains and this review does not pretend to.

## 2. BASE YEAR

FY2026 is a full year with the first half actual: revenue of SAR 27,060mn against a
disclosed H1 of 13,588mn, so the implied second half is 13,472mn against 13,001mn a year
earlier. Nothing is annualised and nothing is solved out of a profit line.

The EBITDA path runs 2,391 → 2,801mn on margins of 8.8% falling to 8.7% — a forecast that
opens at and holds the level the company has recently earned, rather than assuming an
expansion the filings do not show.

## 3. MACRO COHERENCE

**This heading changed in this edition and it is the reason it did.** The prior edition
typed a nominal terminal growth of 2.50%, built on its own reading of Saudi consumer prices
at 1.8%. It is now stored as a **stated real growth of 0.7%** on the house terminal
inflation of **2.0%**, and the nominal recomputes to 2.714%. The real assumption is exactly
the one the prior edition argued in prose; what changed is that a reader can now tell which
half is which, and that a study may not carry an inflation number of its own.

The house path is sourced rather than assumed. What remains outstanding is stated: SAVOLA
still commits no full macro record, so its cost escalators and its currency treatment are
not held to that path by anything, and it stays on that ratchet.

## 4. DISCOUNT RATE

The cost of equity is 10.41% on a beta of 1.087 regressed against TASI, the exchange's own
published index, with an R² of 0.159 over 254 weekly observations. The explicit-window cost
of capital is 8.06% and the terminal 8.42%; the terminal is HIGHER, which is the right
direction here because the terminal capital structure carries less debt.

**The re-strike moved this heading, and against the answer.** A market capitalisation 19.3%
higher raises the equity weight in a market-value-weighted cost of capital, so the blended
rate rose and the valuation fell. That is the mechanism working as intended — a company the
market has re-rated upward is, on this construction, discounted slightly harder — and it is
recorded rather than smoothed.

The beta is wide: a 90% interval of 0.73 to 1.44, partly the 2024-25 restructuring. That
width is published in the study rather than hidden, and it is a larger source of honest
uncertainty than anything this review found.

## 5. TERMINAL

**The terminal is 79.2% of enterprise value.** That is high, it rose from the prior
edition's 78.8%, and it is the number a reader should be most sceptical of.

What that block now charges for is capital maintenance at what replacement costs. Terminal
operating profit after tax is SAR 1,138mn; SAR 760mn of owned and intangible depreciation is
added back, and SAR 909mn charged against it — that same depreciation escalated over half
the asset base's life. Inflation on the working capital AND the lease book takes a further
SAR 205mn; the lease book belongs there because the explicit years charge its growth as cash
out, and a terminal that omitted it would hand this group a rent-free store estate for ever.
Real growth costs SAR 80mn. That leaves SAR 823mn, **70.4% of terminal profit**.

**The asset life is the one real judgement and it is DERIVED, not chosen.** The accounting
policy gives ranges for five classes — buildings 5 to 50 years, leasehold improvements 3 to
33, plant and equipment 3 to 40, furniture 1 to 10, vehicles 2 to 15 — and no weighting, so
no single figure can be read off it. The depreciation note's own two columns supply one by
identity: gross cost of the depreciable base, SAR 10,981mn excluding land and construction
in progress, over the year's own charge of SAR 609mn, giving **18.03 years**. The prior
year's columns give 17.05 on the same identity, and the route is clean because 2025 carries
no business-combination additions to contaminate the charge.

**Two classes come out above their own disclosed range** — vehicles at 31.9 years against a
stated 2 to 15, furniture at 13.9 against 1 to 10 — which says those bases are largely
written down rather than that the note is wrong, and it is recorded rather than smoothed.

**The note can be read a second way and that reading is published.** Accumulated
depreciation over the same charge says the base has already taken 11.27 years of
depreciation, while the escalation formula assumes an average age of half the life, 9.02
years. Reading it the heavier way gives SAR 22.82 a share against the cash-flow lens's
24.65 — so the width of this judgement is about 7% of the cash-flow lens, published beside
the base and never averaged into it.

**Had the retired construction been kept, this terminal would have been 0.9% higher.** The
correction is real and its effect here is small; that is a fact about this name rather than
about the correction.

## 6. BALANCE SHEET

The bridge stands on the audited 31-December-2025 balance sheet and says so, while the
cost-of-capital weights and the book lens use the reviewed 30-June-2026 figures. Those are
two dates in one study and the reason is stated in the study rather than left implicit: the
bridge legs are audited and the weights need to be current.

**This is a real weakness and it is named here rather than defended.** A reviewed 30-June
balance sheet exists and the bridge does not stand on it. The house standard is that a
bridge stands on the LATEST disclosed sheet, and SAVOLA commits no bridge record, so it is
on that ratchet with most of the book. Moving it is a separate pass, and its direction is
not known in advance.

## 7. CLAIMS AGAINST THE RECORD

Every percentage and multiple in both delivered documents reconciles to a model output —
383 figures, none unmatched, where the prior edition carried four that did not. Four of
those were exposed rather than created by this pass: a range written "5.22-5.83%" had been
read by the checking instrument as a NEGATIVE 5.83%, which then matched some other committed
number by coincidence and read as clean. The instrument was fixed, not the sentence.

Two claims the prior edition made are WITHDRAWN because the rebuild falsified them, and they
are named rather than dropped: the terminal-return variant held at 10.5%, which the
sanctioned terminal does not use at all and which is replaced by the asset-life variant
above; and the sentence describing the terminal as continuing "the economics the forecast
itself produces", which was true of a construction this study no longer carries.

## 8. MULTIPLE CROSS-CHECK

On one basis throughout — enterprise value at the fair value is the model's own, at the
price it is the market capitalisation plus the same bridge:

| Basis | At the fair value | At the price |
|---|---|---|
| EV / FY2027E EBITDA | 5.11x | 5.58x |
| EV / FY2028E EBITDA | 4.89x | 5.35x |
| EV / FY2025A EBITDA | 5.40x | 5.90x |
| Price / FY2026E earnings | 11.03x | 12.33x |
| Price / FY2027E earnings | 10.49x | 11.72x |

The peer set trades on trailing earnings at Almarai 19.77x, BinDawood 20.20x, NADEC 13.00x
and Wilmar 12.54x.

**This is the heading that cuts AGAINST our answer, and it does so clearly.** At the fair
value SAVOLA implies 11.03x forward earnings against a peer set spanning 12.5x to 20.2x —
BELOW every peer, including the two food producers closest to its own mix. At the traded
price it implies 12.33x, which is inside the peer range at its low end. On multiples the
market's price looks ordinary and ours looks cheap, and a fair value below every comparable
is the shape a defect makes as surely as one above every comparable is.

What can be said on the other side is stated rather than assumed: SAVOLA's earnings are more
concentrated in a grocery retail business under margin pressure than Almarai's or NADEC's,
and its lease-heavy store estate carries a capital charge the multiple does not see. That is
an argument for a discount, not a measurement of one.

## VERDICT

**The answer is not changed, and the honest reason is that this review did not find the
defect — it found the price moved.** Three of the eight headings cut against the answer:

- the multiple cross-check puts our fair value below every peer on forward earnings;
- the terminal carries 79.2% of enterprise value, and reading the depreciation note the
  heavier of its two ways takes the cash-flow lens down another 7%;
- the bridge stands on a 31-December sheet while a reviewed 30-June sheet exists.

None of the three is a candidate for a 10% gap on its own, and two of them point the same
way the gap does — that is, correcting them would widen the disagreement rather than close
it. **That is itself the finding.** Where a study sits below the price and its own
weaknesses would push it lower, the pessimism is not in the places this review can see, and
saying so is more useful than manufacturing a candidate.

**What this review does NOT do is move the number toward the price.** A fair value adjusted
to meet a quote is the reverse-engineered rate this house prohibits outright, arriving
through the front door instead of the side one.

SAVOLA is **HELD** from publication: the central sits more than 10% below the latest known
price. It is held for that reason and, independently, because the method has not cleared its
Phase 1 acceptance criteria. Nothing here reaches a reader before both are resolved.
