## PART 2 — WHAT I VERIFIED, AND IT IS THE LARGEST SINGLE FINDING IN ANY OF THE SEVEN AUDITS

### Finding 01 — the domestic price escalator. **CONFIRMED TO THE DECIMAL. +182%.**

Read directly from the delivered workbook:

| `Assumptions` row | | |
|---|---|---|
| **C14:G14** Domestic price per pack growth | 5.0% · 8.0% · 7.5% · 6.5% · 5.5% | compounds to **1.3697** |
| **C29:G29** Escalator: domestic consumer prices | 16% · 12% · 9% · 7.5% · 7.0% | compounds to **1.6289** |
| | | **cumulative real price change −15.9%** |

**The price path sits below the inflation path in every one of the five years.** And the basis
note, in *both* delivered artefacts, reads:

> *"Administered prices track inflation, no real gain."*

They do not track inflation. Meanwhile **68.4% of the cost stack escalates through an exchange
rate derived from that same CPI path**, with labour and energy above it. One event counted once
on costs and ignored once on price.

**Priced by the auditor: Frame A 33.00 → 93.16, Frame B 49.68 → 111.85. +182%, closing 64% of
this study's entire gap to the market.** FY2030E gross margin goes 36.5% → 43.5%, against a
**44.0% audited actual** — i.e. the corrected path lands on what the company actually earns.

**This is the third independent confirmation today of one mechanism.** AMOC's auditor priced
the same escalator wedge at +17.8%; EGCH's at +68.7%; PHAR's at +182%. Three names, three
auditors, one defect, all pointing up.
