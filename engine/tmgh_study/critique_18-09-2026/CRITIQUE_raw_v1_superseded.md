# Forensic Audit — Talaat Moustafa Group Holding valuation study

**Subject documents** · `01_valuation_study.docx` (2 September 2026) · `02_valuation_model.xlsx` (2026-09-02) · `03_bibliography.docx` (1 September 2026)

**Auditor's position** · adversarial toward every claim; a failure is recorded only where it can be demonstrated, and a suspicion that cannot be demonstrated is logged UNVERIFIABLE.

## Section A — Audit Scope

Audited in full: every paragraph and all 33 tables of the study, all 137 coded rows of the source register, and all 691 populated cells of all 16 workbook sheets, with every formula evaluated by hand and the ten-year waterfall, both cost-of-capital stacks, the terminal, all four cases and all twelve bridge figures independently recomputed before the report's answers were read.

**Primary sources reached.** TMG's own reviewed interim consolidated financial statements for the six months to 30 June 2026 (subject and period verified from inside the document — cover, review report and the statement of financial position — never from the filename), the FY2024 and FY2025 consolidated statements, and the earnings releases from FY2018 to 1H2026, all from the company's own investor-relations archive. Also the Central Bank of Egypt's Monetary Policy Report Q1-2026, the CBE's August 2026 MPC outcome, CAPMAS inflation prints, World Bank nominal GDP, the Egyptian Income Tax Law, the NYU Stern country-premium dataset in both its January and July 2026 vintages, and EGX's own September 2026 index review (via a wire republisher).

**Not reached.** The Egyptian Exchange's own site (egx.com.eg) is comprehensively unavailable from this environment — sixteen URLs, five user agents, two HTTP versions and a persistent cookie jar all returned HTTP 503 or an F5/Shape bot challenge — so no EGX bulletin could be read directly and price rows rest on secondary market data, marked as such. Egypt's 10-year sovereign yield at 6 August 2026 could not be established from any reachable source. The beta itself cannot be tested: no return series, no index series and no regression output exists in any artefact.

**Scope note.** This audit does not rebuild the valuation and proposes no alternative value. It asks only whether each input is true, each calculation correct, each construction the one claimed, and each judgement defensible and internally consistent.

### The three most valuation-critical findings

**1 — The delivery schedule the study is built around not having (row 1).** The study's crux, its headline, its sensitivity grid and its "what would change our mind" all rest on one sentence: *"Everything in this study turns on one number that the company does not publish: how long its order book takes to convert into handovers."* TMG publishes it, in essentially every release since FY2018, with unit counts: **"some 37,000 residential and non-residential units to be delivered within the coming four to five years"** (FY2024); **"some 40.7 thousand… within the coming five years"** (1H2025). The study adopts **fourteen years**. Its own crux grid starts at eight, worth 58.54 — so the company's published horizon lies off the bottom of the grid entirely, and in this model **faster conversion is worth less**. The protocol's rule is that management guidance is *scored, never consumed*; the study does neither, because it denies the disclosure exists. This does not merely move the headline — it inverts the direction of the study's disagreement with the market.

**2 — The order book is not converted; it is compounded, by a mechanism stated nowhere (rows 5–10).** The declared driver is conversion of a fixed, disclosed EGP 491bn book over fourteen years. What the model actually contains is a **first-order partial adjustment at a speed of 0.25**, disclosed in no table, no cell and no sentence: `rev_t = rev_(t−1) + 0.25 × [ (opening book_t + sales_t)/14 − rev_(t−1) ]`, which reproduces every year 2027–2035 to 2.9e−11, with the identical 25% form governing work in progress. Its consequence is structural: the target compounds while the adjustment closes only a quarter of the gap, so **realised conversion never reaches 1/14 = 7.143%** — it runs 6.24% → 4.55% and, extended sixty years, settles at **5.13%**. *A cell labelled "14 years" cannot produce a fourteen-year conversion in this model at any horizon.* Around that mechanism, new contracted sales of EGP 300bn a year are injected at face and escalated 15%, so the book compounds to **5,265,316** and the advances line supplies **152,135 against total free cash flow of 137,742** in 2035. Three disclosures the study holds but does not read destroy the premise: **43% of 1H2026 contracted sales are SouthMed, explicitly excluded from backlog under an asset-light model earning a c.8% fee**; the FY2023 "non-core land transaction" is in fact **EGP 47.8bn of third-party commission volume**; and the register's own SouthMed cumulative sales of 500,000 exceed the entire group backlog of 491,000.

**3 — The discount-rate glide is sourced to a publication that does not exist (rows 10–15).** The study's stated largest single change from prior editions is that each year carries its own rate, and the sentence that justifies the shape reads: *"the shape follows the central bank's published easing calendar rather than a second assumption of this study's own."* The CBE publishes an MPC **meeting** calendar and nothing else — no path, no projection, no schedule — and its own Q1-2026 report states it has *"opted to pause the easing cycle."* The terminal rate compounds the problem: it is built on the **Q4 2026 waypoint of 7%** rather than the CBE's announced **Q4 2028 end-state of 5%**, on a "standard real return" of 5.50% with no source anywhere, and on a 7.00% equity premium that silently retires 9.71 points of Egypt country risk without a word of derivation. By the study's own headline arithmetic, a pound in year nineteen is worth **3.77×** what a flat rate gives — so this path *is* the study's answer.

### Direction of the contested judgements

**Sixteen of twenty-three** contested judgements worth more than roughly 5% of value are resolved **upward**; six downward. The two that can be isolated arithmetically — the minority basis (**36.34**) and the investment-property double count (**9.67**) — are together worth **EGP 46.01 a share, 39% of the adopted headline**.

Upward: the minority basis; the terminal equity risk premium; the discount-rate shape; the cost of debt; the development margin held flat; new contracted sales at face; the sales escalator; hospitality growth; other-recurring growth; investment property; the work-in-progress cover; the 2024 comparatives; the land-uplift grid; the headline floor; the fourteen-year conversion period against the company's published four-to-five; and the 25% adjustment speed, which holds realised conversion near 5.1% while the headline is parameterised at 14 years.
Downward: sales declared not extrapolated; revaluation gains stripped; the faster case published alongside; a continuing value at 35% of a Gordon; zero real terminal growth; the terminal risk-free rate anchored to the higher of the CBE's two targets; and two small bridge omissions.

This is a flag, not a failure — a company can genuinely deserve a consistent read. But this study's own stated protocol is to publish both framings wherever two exist, and on thirteen of these sixteen it does not.

The answer is **two-sided** and both branches were audited. The slower branch sits 21–26% above the traded price of 97.80; the faster branch sits 33–35% below. The findings move the slower branch down and leave the faster branch roughly where it is — **narrowing** rather than widening the disagreement with the market, which is the opposite of what the study's own reverse-read framing concludes (row 39).

### Does the headline range survive the corrections?

**It does not. On the study's own model and the company's own disclosures, the range collapses.**

That verdict needs the distinction stated precisely, because a great deal here is done well and a gate is not worth less for saying so. **The arithmetic is sound.** Every number in the delivered document reconciles — the ten-year waterfall to the pound, the cost of capital from its printed inputs, all twelve bridge figures, the balance sheet, the segment margins, the price cone at both horizons, every country-premium input against the source dataset. Country risk is charged exactly once, on both bases, and the bases are never mixed. The borrowing-rate denominator correctly excludes advances, supplier balances and cheque obligations. The lenses are not blended, book value is published as a floor and never weighted in, the relative multiple is non-circular, and the price simulation is genuinely independent of the valuation. The opening forecast year is anchored on the reviewed half with the company's own seasonality. The sourcing discipline on company *figures* is unusually good. Most studies fail several of these; this one fails none.

What collapses is the range, for three compounding reasons.

**The conversion period.** The headline 118.50 and the published ceiling 123.03 rest on fourteen years. The company publishes four to five, continuously, in documents the register lists. Fourteen years is not a conservative reading of an undisclosed number; it is roughly three times a disclosed one, and in this model the shorter horizon is worth *less* — off the bottom of the study's own grid, whose lowest rung (eight years) is already 58.54 against a traded price of 97.80.

**The driver beneath it.** Even at fourteen years the model is not converting a book. An undisclosed 25% partial adjustment means it *cannot* converge on any stated period — realised conversion settles near 5.1%, an implied horizon close to twenty years — while new sales compound the book it is meant to be drawing down, 43% of them fee volume that never becomes a deliverable obligation, and the resulting working-capital inflow is harvested as half of terminal-year free cash flow.

**The rate that discounts it.** The glide that makes the back years worth four times what a flat rate would give is attributed to a central-bank publication that does not exist, terminates on a waypoint target rather than the announced end-state, and retires nine points of sovereign equity risk without derivation.

Three of the study's own artefacts already contradict its headline before any of that is applied: its **discount-rate grid** values the primary case at 108.05 rather than 118.50; its **crux grid** values the stated faster case at 84.50 rather than 63.70; and its **workbook bridge** computes 113.24 rather than 118.50. Its own **projected accounts** use a minority basis worth 36.34 a share less than the one the headline adopts. Its own **register** disproves the sales assumption that drives half of terminal free cash flow. Its own **capacity disclosures** — 3,500 keys at 89% implied occupancy — will not support the hospitality line it prints.

A range whose floor the same table prints as 43.89, whose central its own sensitivity grid puts ten points lower, whose conversion period the company contradicts by a factor of three, and whose largest cash-flow line rests on sales the company says never enter the book, is not the range 63.70–123.03.
