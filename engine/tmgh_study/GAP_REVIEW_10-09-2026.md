## 4. DISCOUNT RATE

Cost of equity = rf* 16.63% + beta 1.4687 × ERP 13.94% = **37.10%**; weights equity
0.9213 / debt 0.0787 → rating-basis WACC **35.74%**; CDS basis 32.34%. The beta is tier 1
and conforming: R² 0.325, standard error 0.183, 256 weekly observations against the
published EGX30.

**THE STUDY DISCOUNTS ON A GLIDE, NOT ON THAT NUMBER.** The rating schedule opens at
**31.56%** and falls to **18.88%** at the terminal. This matters here more than on almost
any other name in the book, because the order book converts out to year nineteen, and a
flat crisis-level rate values a pound in year nineteen at a quarter of what the schedule
values it at.

**THE CASH IS CHARGED FOR ONCE.** Free cash flow is EBIT×(1−t) + D&A − capex − working
capital, with **no finance income inside it**; the 2026 finance income of EGP 15,154.7mn
appears in the projected income statement and the earnings per share only. The bridge adds
net cash of EGP 59,280.4mn once, at face. The equity weight of 0.9213 is below one, so
the net-cash pathology that can push an operating rate above the cost of equity cannot
arise here.

## 5. TERMINAL — AND IT IS NOT WHERE THIS ANSWER COMES FROM

**No perpetuity is taken on the development leg.** The order book is FINITE and is
converted over fourteen years as an annuity: residual book EGP 5,265,316.3mn, annual cash
EGP 30,123.3mn, present value EGP 20,701.7mn — **7.6 per cent of enterprise value.**

A growing perpetuity is taken ONLY on the recurring legs: free cash flow of EGP
30,714.4mn at 7% → present value EGP 39,398.9mn, **14.4 per cent of enterprise value.**

**The explicit ten years carry 78.0 per cent of enterprise value.** On a developer, that
is the opposite of the usual complaint.

## 6. BALANCE SHEET

The bridge stands on the reviewed sheet of 30 June 2026. Non-operating assets added at
face: net cash 59,280.4 + investment property 25,228.0 + associates 1,496.6 + FVOCI
1,802.8 = **EGP 87,807.8mn, which is 24.4 per cent of pre-minority equity.** A quarter of
this answer is not the development business at all, and that is the single largest thing
a reader should interrogate.

**THE MINORITY IS THE OTHER ONE.** It takes **45.21 per cent of book equity** at 30 June
2026 and earns **20.98 per cent of FY2025 profit as filed** (25.88% FY2024, 29.32% as
restated). The bridge removes it at BOOK — the conservative of the two — and the
sensitivity below prices the alternative.

## 9. WHAT THE PRICE IMPLIES

To reach EGP 96.60 the model needs a discount rate of **32.32 per cent** on the capacity
mode. The study's own year-one rate is **31.56 per cent**. **The market discounts 76 basis
points HARDER than we do** — a narrow disagreement about the price of time, and not the
source of the gap.

On the recovery mode the price implies 17.58 per cent, **below Egypt's own 23.00 per cent
sovereign yield** — an equity return under the government bond, which is where that mode
stops being a reading of this market and starts being a reading of a different company.

## VERDICT

**No error found in the company's favour.** The lever that moved this edition is a house
macro rate this study should never have owned, and it moved the answer away from the
price rather than toward it.

**The two things most capable of making this study the optimistic one are named and
neither is a forecast:** the 24.4 per cent of pre-minority equity that is cash and
property added at face rather than earned, and the minority removed at its 45.2 per cent
book share while it earns 21.0 per cent of profit. Both are priced in section 5's
sensitivity. Removing the minority proportionally at book takes the capacity case to EGP
95.84 — **within one per cent of the traded price.**

**AND THE LARGEST DRIVER RUNS THE OTHER WAY.** Contracted sales are forecast 38.6 per
cent below the walk-forward and below every filed year. Fixing that would widen this gap.
A study that is wrong here is wrong by being too pessimistic about the order book and too
generous about the balance sheet at the same time.
