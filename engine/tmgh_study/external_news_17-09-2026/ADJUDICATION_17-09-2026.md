## 1. THE LAND BANK — WHICH IS WHY THIS NAME'S PROMPT ASKED FOR IT

The standing asset-base rule requires a developer's operating asset base to be no older than
the information set the study says it read. **This study carries `landbank_msqm = 20.0` from
the 1H 2026 earnings release and NO `asset_base_record`**, so it is on that gate's ratchet
with the reason recorded: the figure is current, and *nothing states its vintage in a form a
gate can read*. The gate reports no new breach.

**Both returns describe a group land bank of roughly 115m sq m rising to about 128m sq m**
after an Iraqi allocation, with Banan in Riyadh, Jood and Yamal in Oman beside it.

**These are almost certainly not the same quantity**, and saying so is the point rather than
a hedge: 20.0m sq m cannot be this group's total holding — Madinaty alone is of that order —
so the committed figure is a *remaining or undeveloped* measure and the 115–128m sq m is a
*total group* measure, probably including the international projects. **Nothing in the study
states which**, and that is exactly the vintage-and-scope gap the missing record leaves open.

**The question this pair raises, and it is the real one: does this valuation reach the Saudi,
Omani and Iraqi land at all?** The standing lens for a developer is a cash-flow lens with an
RNAV beside it, and a land bank that is printed rather than consumed cannot carry an RNAV.
**This study's land bank is not wired into its arithmetic** — it is bound out of the KPIs and
used as a format argument inside a sentence. A land bank that moves by 13m sq m changes
nothing in a model that never reads it.

**Both returns agree there was NO closed land acquisition after 30 June 2026.** The Iraq
licence is dated May 2026 — *inside* the information set, not after it — and the PIF
memorandum is June 2026 and non-binding on the secondary account. **So the correct reading is
not that the asset base is stale against later events; it is that the record cannot say.**
