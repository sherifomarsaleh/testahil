## 1. The same defect, two more costumes

Declaration 6 named one defect wearing four costumes: **a rate or a level observed once and
carried forward as though the world stopped.** It closed the fourth and applied the second
and third where they were found. Two places were not reached, and both are that same
defect.

### 7A — THE FOREIGN LEG OF THE PURCHASING-POWER RELATION

This lens carries a long-run foreign inflation figure, `US_INFLATION_LT`, and every
currency path it builds runs on it. **One run carries its own foreign leg instead: the last
published foreign print at the origin, held flat for five years.**

At one origin that print is **8.00%** — a one-off spike — so the currency depreciates at
the domestic rate divided by 1.08 compounding, and the run's dollar-priced revenue grows at
**5.5% a year while its domestic costs compound at 13.9%**. An 8.4-point-a-year wedge
opens between a price and a cost that are the same event, which is the incoherence
[R-MACRO-01] exists to refuse: **a company cannot be valued in an economy the study beside
it does not recognise**, and here it is not even the study beside it — it is the same lens.

**The correction uses a constant already in the file and chooses nothing.** The foreign leg
of the relation is the house long-run figure everywhere, on every name.

**IT IS TWO-SIDED BY CONSTRUCTION AND THAT IS MEASURED RATHER THAN ASSERTED.** Across the
twenty origins this run holds, the last published foreign print ranges from **−0.36% to
8.00%** and straddles the house figure — **nine origins below it and eleven above.** Where
the print sat below, this correction makes the currency depreciate more slowly and LOWERS
that origin's projected revenue. A lever that could only ever help is a lever that is being
fitted; this one is measured to run both ways before it is applied.

### 7B — THE DOMESTIC LEG, ON THE SAME RUN

Declaration 6 put inflation on the origin's own published forward ladder for two names and
**exempted the third on a reason that covered only its currency** — "its currency already
runs on relative purchasing-power parity". True, and it says nothing about the ladder: that
run still holds its domestic rate flat at the last published print for five years, which is
declaration 6's own defect number three, unclosed on the one name it was excused from.

**THE DIRECTION IS DECLARED IN ADVANCE AND IT IS THE UNHELPFUL ONE.** Revenue grows at the
domestic rate divided by the foreign one and cost grows at the domestic rate, so a HIGHER
domestic ladder widens the absolute wedge between them and **makes that run's margin decline
worse, not better.** It is declared and applied anyway, in this order, because a correction
taken only when it helps is not a correction.

The domestic ladder is mapped onto that run's own June fiscal year by `fiscal_june` — half
of each of the two calendar years the fiscal year spans — which is one of the four closed
mappings [R-MACRO-01 AMENDED] admits, borrowed rather than minted.

### 7C — THE SOVEREIGN FLOOR BINDS A BOOK THAT IS ACTUALLY LOCAL

[R-COC-01] refuses a cost of debt below its own sovereign **on an all-local-currency book**,
and separately requires foreign-currency debt to be carried at **local-equivalent cost**
(foreign coupon plus expected local depreciation). Both halves have been written since the
method's adoption. **Nothing anywhere established which kind of book it was**, so the floor
was applied unconditionally — the qualifier was doing load-bearing work in a sentence
nobody was made to evaluate.

**MEASURED ON THE WHOLE BOOK BEFORE THIS WAS WRITTEN: the floor binds on 16 of 16 cells.**
Not one name's independently computed effective rate reaches the sovereign at any origin;
the observed rates run 3.70% to 11.88% against floors of 14.30% to 22.35%, at debt weights
from 18% to 88%. This is systematic, not one name's quirk, and it runs one way — it raises
every cost of capital and therefore lowers every fair value.

**THE FLOOR IS NOT DELETED AND IS NOT WIDENED.** Where a run commits a currency composition
read from the company's own filings, the debt is split and the two tranches are priced as
[R-COC-01] already directs:

    kd = w_local x max(r_local, sovereign)  +  w_foreign x (r_foreign + expected depreciation)

- **the local tranche keeps the full floor**, because on a local tranche the floor's premise
  holds;
- **the foreign tranche is carried at local-equivalent cost**, the expected depreciation
  taken from this lens's OWN purchasing-power relation as corrected by 7A — the same
  relation, not a second one;
- **`r_foreign` and `r_local` are the company's own disclosed facility rates**, and where a
  foreign currency carries no disclosed rate its balance is treated as LOCAL, which never
  understates the cost.

**WHERE THE COMPOSITION IS NOT COMMITTED, NOTHING CHANGES AND THE FLOOR STANDS.** A weight
that is not disclosed is not estimated — the cell keeps the rate it has and the gap is named,
which is SIGCM clause 8 and is deliberately the conservative direction, since the origins
this cannot reach are among those the correction would most help.

## 2. Order, fixed here, before any figure exists

**7A, then 7B, then 7C**, each measured on the stack beneath it and each reported whichever
way it moves the answer. [R-VCAL-01]'s promotion guard binds: one lever at a time, halted the
moment the stacked pooled bias would cross zero by more than the bootstrap half-width.

## 3. What this declaration does not do

It moves no fair value toward any price, sets no threshold, and adds no free parameter: 7A
substitutes a constant already in the module for a one-off print, 7B substitutes a published
ladder for a print held flat, and 7C splits a book at weights the company itself discloses
and prices each half by a rule already written. **Every one of the three would be right
whichever way it moved the answer**, which is the only test that separates a correction from
a fit.
