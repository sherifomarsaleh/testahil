# The cells that call a company expensive — audited

**The bar, set by the principal 08-09-2026:** *"I am not worried if we say something is
cheap. Because some markets and some companies are genuinely cheap. I am concerned if we
say a company is expensive by more than 10%."* — and, ruling on which test governs: *"The
bar is the one you stated — no company called expensive by more than 10% without an audit
behind it."*

**Why this and not the pooled bias.** Criterion 3's clause A demands the pooled bias cover
zero — that the method be neither cheap nor expensive on average. This house's stated
purpose is finding companies that are cheap, and most cells read cheap. **A symmetric
zero-bias test penalises the method for doing the thing it is for.** Whether the cheapness
is INFORMATION rather than merely a lean is clause D and E's question — gap closure against
subsequent returns — and those are Phase 2b's subject by the adoption rule, not Phase 1's.

**The population: 22 cells, 5 names, 8 origins, EGX only.** Six call a company expensive by
more than ten per cent. Each is below with what is actually wrong, measured.

|---|---|---|---|---|

| PHDC 2019 | 0.858 | 1.748 | −50.9% | not a defect — a developer mid-build |
| SWDY 2018 | 5.874 | 17.970 | −67.3% | **macro, and unfixable by construction** |

## 1. EGCH 2023 — the defect was real, was named, and is now fixed

**Was 2.417 against 10.710, −77.4%. Now 9.494 against 10.710, −11.4%.** The largest single
correction in this book.

**What it was.** The run's projected operating margin ran
**27.6% → 22.2% → 16.4% → 10.1% → 3.3%** across the explicit window — an **88 per cent
relative collapse** against a house trigger of five. [R-ANCHOR-01 CLAUSE TWO] governs
exactly this and was adopted on this very company.

**Where it came from, and it is not where the first audit looked.** The run builds its
currency by relative purchasing-power parity — correctly — and takes the **foreign** leg of
that relation from **the last published foreign inflation print at the origin, held flat for
five years.** At this origin that print is **8.00%**, a one-off spike. So the pound
depreciated at the domestic rate divided by 1.08 compounding, dollar-priced revenue grew at
**5.5% a year, and domestic costs compounded at 13.9%.** An 8.4-point-a-year wedge opened
between a price and a cost that are the same event — which is [L-048] and the incoherence
[R-MACRO-01] exists to refuse, arriving not between two studies but inside one lens, which
carries its own long-run foreign figure and uses it on every other name.

**The correction chooses nothing**: the foreign leg is the constant already in the module.
It is **two-sided by measurement rather than assertion** — across the twenty origins this
run holds, the print it supersedes ranges from −0.36% to 8.00% and straddles the house
figure, **nine origins below and eleven above**, so at nine of them this same correction
LOWERS projected revenue.

**What remains at −11.4%.** The margin still declines 18.5% in relative terms, and that
decline is DECLARED: this company's delivered study carries `input_cost_outpacing_price`
with its measurement from the company's own audited accounts — cost per unit of revenue ran
54.059% to 61.613% over three years, a rise of 7.55 points. The residual decline is inside
what the company's own filings support.

**One correction was declared in advance and NOT applied, and the reason is point-in-time,
not its effect.** The same run holds its DOMESTIC rate flat too, which is the identical
defect; putting it on the published ladder needs the macro archive, whose vintage for this
market is the IMF edition published each **October**, while this run's fiscal year ends
**30 June**. At origin FY2023 that ladder was four months from publication and the origin
cannot have seen it. **Point-in-time discipline is absolute and outranks the correction.**
Its direction is recorded so the hold cannot be read as selection: applied, it makes the
margin decline **worse** at both scored origins (−47.7% and −64.1%, against −18.5% and
−23.1% under the correction that was applied). A lever held because it hurt would be the
offence; this one is held because the origin could not have known it, and it hurt.

## 2. PHDC 2019 — not a defect

Year-one free cash flow is **−4,368mn** because land and construction are paid for during
the window, and the terminal carries 142% of enterprise value because the value arrives
afterwards. The margin is flat at ~39% and nothing in the construction has broken. **A
developer mid-build being worth less than its quoted price is a conclusion this method is
entitled to reach.**

This cell was refused earlier on exactly that terminal share, and the refusal was withdrawn
on the principal's ruling that a high terminal proportion is normal.

## 3. SWDY 2018, 2019, 2020, 2022 — four hypotheses, three refuted, one measured

**Every hypothesis is recorded with its measurement, including the ones that were wrong,
because a wrong diagnosis left in a record is worse than none.**

**Refuted — the beta.** The first draft said these four were one input, a beta held flat at
1.00 on a large liquid name that must move less than the market. Measured on this name's own
point-in-time regressions at those exact origins: **1.0031 / 1.0712 / 1.0605 / 1.3407** — at
or ABOVE 1.00, never below. Correcting it would push these cells FURTHER below the price.

**Refuted — the margin.** The projection carries ~11% at every horizon against a filed
operating margin running **9.4 / 22.2 / 17.4 / 12.5 / 11.3 / 9.3 / 8.5 / 10.1** per cent
across 2015-2022. Eleven per cent is mid-range. The projection is not pessimistic about the
business.

**Measured and corrected, but small — the cost of debt.** [R-COC-01] refuses a rate below
its own sovereign **on an all-local-currency book** and separately carries FX debt at
**local-equivalent cost**; nothing established which kind of book this was, so the floor ran
unconditionally. **It binds on 16 of 16 cells in the whole book** — no name's independently
computed effective rate reaches the sovereign at any origin, the observed rates running
3.70% to 11.88% against floors of 14.30% to 22.35%. On this name the company's own foreign
currency risk note discloses the split: **63.2% of borrowings foreign at FY2020, 52.0% at
FY2021**, with its own average rates per currency. Corrected, the cost of debt falls
14.45% → 11.93% at 2020 and 14.85% → 12.00% at 2021, moving that cell from −53.1% to
−48.8%. **Real, right, and not the answer.** At FY2022 the disclosure does not foot in
extraction and was REFUSED rather than repaired, so the floor stands there with the gap
named; at FY2018 and FY2019 the filings disclose the three currencies and not their weights,
so no weight is estimated.

### What it actually is, measured through the house's own instrument

[R-FCAL-01] prescribes splitting a miss macro versus company by re-running every origin on
the knowable inflation path and on perfect foresight. **That instrument was reporting
nothing on this name, and the reason was a defect in it rather than a null result** — the
view feeding it filled BOTH fields from the as-known error, so it asserted for every driver
that perfect foresight changes the error by exactly nothing. Fixed at source, on the two
foresight runs the diagnostic already performs:

| driver | MAE as known | MAE with perfect foresight of inflation | removed |
|---|---|---|---|
| cable price per tonne | 0.4335 | **0.1141** | **74%** |
| cable cost per tonne | 0.4101 | **0.1086** | **74%** |
| revenue | 0.4944 | **0.2192** | **56%** |
| cost of revenue | 0.4886 | **0.2001** | **59%** |
| contracting revenue | 0.3902 | 0.2945 | 25% |
| **gross profit** | 0.5555 | 0.4967 | **11%** |

**The reading is sharp and it is coherent with everything else this house has measured.
Knowing the inflation path removes three quarters of the error in the price and cost LEVELS
and almost none of the error in the SPREAD between them.** The model gets the margin
structure roughly right and the macro scale badly wrong — which is [R-TERM-01]'s pooled
diagnosis on the whole book arriving on one name: *revenue comes in 45% above forecast and
cost 39% above it, so the margin is roughly right and the whole business is understated.*

**AND IT CANNOT BE FIXED, BY CONSTRUCTION.** Point-in-time discipline is absolute: each
origin sees only what was published by that date. At origin 2018 the published path
compounded to ×1.58 by 2023 against a realised path far above it, because Egypt devalued
twice afterwards and no forecast published in 2018 said so. **A method that under-forecasts
because the world moved outside every published forecast is not a defective method**, and
correcting it would mean feeding these origins information they did not have — which is the
one thing this whole exercise exists to refuse.

**The method has earned its precision here, which is the test that separates a surprise from
a failure.** [R-FCAL-01]: *a method that cannot beat "no change" has not earned the precision
it displays.* Against both pre-registered naive benchmarks on this name:

- **gross profit beats BOTH at every horizon** — skill +3.0% to +40.1% against freeze,
  +6.0% to +33% against trend;
- **revenue beats freeze at every horizon** — +15.7% to +46.0% — and ties trend at one year,
  losing 3 to 13 points at two to five.

**The residual, which is a real and nameable defect and is NOT fixable from what this run
commits.** Two of this company's three segments — contracting and other — are projected on
**GDP × domestic inflation**, a top-down macro path, which [R-SIGCM-02] calls *the floor of
last resort, never the default*. Only cables is on its own driver. Contracting went from
**EGP 1.8bn in 2013 to EGP 87.1bn in 2025**, and perfect foresight of inflation removes only
19% of its error — so the rest is the driver, not the currency. **Its own driver is
backlog**, which this company discloses in its earnings releases and which **the run does not
commit**. That is [R-FCAL-01 AMENDED]'s own lesson for the third time: what a process commits
decides what can ever be asked of it later.

**It is named rather than estimated, and the direction is why.** Building contracting on a
proxy would RAISE these values and move them toward the price — which is exactly the
circumstance in which a house must not approximate. The figure is extracted or the cell
stands as it is.

**SWDY 2022 carries a second undiagnosed thing:** its cash-flow path runs
16,425 → 1,310 → 2,632 → 3,897 → 5,271. A first year twelve times the second is a
discontinuity, not a forecast, and it is recorded open.

## What this audit concludes

**Six cells, six audits, and the bar is met.** One was our defect and is fixed. One is an
honest disagreement. Four are one name whose miss is measured, mostly macro, and unfixable
without giving a past origin information it did not have.

**Two things were found by auditing cell by cell that no pooled number would ever have
shown**: a lens carrying two different foreign inflation legs, and a macro/company split
that was one number printed twice. Both had passed every gate in this repository, and both
were visible to anyone who looked at the arithmetic rather than at the result.

**WHAT THIS AUDIT DOES NOT DO.** It moves no fair value toward any price. Every verdict above
is about a CONSTRUCTION, and every fix applied would have been right whichever way it moved
the answer — which is the only test that separates an audit from a fit. Both applied
corrections moved the pooled bias AWAY from zero, and neither was reconsidered for it.
