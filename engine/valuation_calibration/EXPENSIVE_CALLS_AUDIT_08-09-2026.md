# The six cells that call a company expensive — audited

**The bar, set by the principal 08-09-2026:** *"I am not worried if we say something is
cheap. Because some markets and some companies are genuinely cheap. I am concerned if we
say a company is expensive by more than 10%."* — and, ruling on which test governs: *"The
bar is the one you stated — no company called expensive by more than 10% without an audit
behind it."*

**Why this and not the pooled bias.** Criterion 3's clause A demands the pooled bias cover
zero — that the method be neither cheap nor expensive on average. This house's stated
purpose is finding companies that are cheap, and 13 of 22 cells read cheap, which is why
the bias sits at +0.2879. **A symmetric zero-bias test penalises the method for doing the
thing it is for.** Whether the cheapness is INFORMATION rather than merely a lean is
clause D and E's question — gap closure against subsequent returns — and those are Phase
2b's subject by the adoption rule, not Phase 1's.

**The population: 22 cells, 5 names, 9 origins, EGX only.** Six call a company expensive
by more than ten per cent. Each is named below with what is actually wrong.

---

## 1. EGCH 2023 — fair value 2.417 against 10.71, **−77.4%**

**OUR DEFECT, AND IT IS A NAMED ONE.** The projected operating margin runs
**27.6% → 22.2% → 16.4% → 10.1% → 3.3%** across the explicit window. That is an
**88 per cent relative collapse**, against a house trigger of five per cent.

[R-ANCHOR-01 CLAUSE TWO] governs exactly this and was adopted on this very company: *a
rate that declines materially from its own opening year is the same claim as one opening
below the filed record, named, sourced and measured on the same terms.* The delivered
study was corrected for it — it declares `input_cost_outpacing_price` and carries the
measurement. **The walk-forward projection was not.** The correction reached the study and
not the run behind it.

This is the largest single error in the book and it is fixable: the same declaration the
study already carries, applied to the run.

## 2. PHDC 2019 — 0.858 against 1.748, **−50.9%**

**NOT A DEFECT — THE ORDINARY SHAPE OF A DEVELOPER.** Year-one free cash flow is
**−4,368mn** because land and construction are paid for during the window, and the
terminal carries 142% of enterprise value because the value arrives afterwards. The
margin is flat at ~39% and nothing in the construction has broken.

This was refused earlier today on exactly that terminal share, and the refusal was
withdrawn on the principal's ruling that a high terminal proportion is normal. **It stands
as a real disagreement**, and a developer mid-build being worth less than its quoted price
is a conclusion this method is entitled to reach.

## 3–6. SWDY 2018, 2019, 2020, 2022 — −67.3%, −29.0%, −53.1%, −37.9%

**FOUR OF THE SIX ARE ONE NAME, AND THAT IS THE FINDING RATHER THAN FOUR FINDINGS.**
Every one of them projects a flat ~11% operating margin, positive and growing cash flow
after year one, and nothing structurally broken. What they share is a **cost of capital
running 16.8% to 23.3%**, on a name that is large, liquid and among the most heavily
traded on its exchange.

**The suspect is the beta.** This lens carries **beta = 1.00 for every name at every
origin** — declaration 2's choice, and the full-shrinkage limit of a Vasicek estimator.
Measured today on this name's own point-in-time regressions, its beta is not 1.00. A
company whose shares move less than the market is being charged as though they move with
it, at every origin, and a discount rate too high by a few hundred basis points is worth
double digits of value on a terminal carrying 39% to 86% of it.

**Lever 5 already built the correction and the promotion rule rejected it** — because it
moved the pooled bias away from zero. Under the bar the principal has now set, that
rejection was answering a different question: the point-in-time beta is the *correct*
input regardless of what it does to a symmetric bias test, and these four cells are where
the flat 1.00 costs the most.

**SWDY 2022 carries a second thing worth naming:** its cash-flow path runs
16,425 → 1,310 → 2,632 → 3,897 → 5,271. A first year twelve times the second is a
discontinuity, not a forecast, and it has not been diagnosed here.

## What this audit concludes

| cell | verdict |
|---|---|
| EGCH 2023 | **our defect** — a named, governed margin collapse the run never received |
| PHDC 2019 | **not a defect** — a developer mid-build, real disagreement |
| SWDY 2018/19/20/22 | **one suspected defect, four times** — a flat beta of 1.00 on a low-beta name |
| SWDY 2022 | **plus one undiagnosed** — a year-one cash-flow discontinuity |

**Two named defects and one honest disagreement, not six separate problems.** Both defects
have a known fix already built: the anchor declaration on EGCH's run, and the
point-in-time beta on SWDY's cost of capital.

**WHAT THIS AUDIT DOES NOT DO.** It does not move any fair value toward any price. Every
verdict above is about a CONSTRUCTION, and the two fixes named are corrections that would
be right whichever way they moved the answer — which is the only test that separates an
audit from a fit.
