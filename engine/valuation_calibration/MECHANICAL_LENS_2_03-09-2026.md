# The mechanical fair value — second declaration, superseding the first

**3 September 2026.** This supersedes `MECHANICAL_LENS_03-09-2026.md`, which is not
edited and stays in the repository as it was sealed. Its first run is committed in
`FIRST_RUN_SEALED_LENS_03-09-2026.json`, unaltered, so that this replacement can be
audited against what triggered it.

## Why the first is withdrawn — and the reason is a rule, not a number

The first declaration discounted projected **net profit** and capitalised it into a
perpetuity. Applied to a real-estate developer that is a **standing error in this
repository**, and it was written down before the lens was declared:

> **[R-LENS-03]:** *normalised earnings is not a developer lens at all, deliberately
> absent from both developer rows, because a developer recognising revenue on
> handover reports earnings that are an accident of which project completed in
> which year.*

A perpetuity on those earnings is worse still: it capitalises profit the company
must reinvest in land and construction to sustain, so the terminal alone can exceed
the market capitalisation. That is a specification error, and the class rule
forbidding it was available before a single figure was computed. **I missed it.**

**The trigger is the rule, not the size of the number.** That distinction is the
whole difference between a correction and a fit, which is why the first run was
committed before this file was written rather than after.

## The bar this declaration sets on itself

**There is no third declaration.** If this construction also fails, the finding is
that a mechanical developer valuation is not constructible from what the
walk-forward commits — which is a real and reportable result — and the calibration
waits for the projection to carry the missing items rather than trying a third
shape. A construction adjusted until it produces a comfortable number is a fit, and
the third attempt is where that becomes indistinguishable from one.

## The construction

[R-LENS-03] gives developers a **cash-flow lens with a present-value RNAV beside
it**. A full FCFF is not constructible here — the walk-forward projects revenue,
cost, gross profit, overheads, finance cost and profit, but **no capital
expenditure and no working capital**, and inventing either would be the fabrication
this project refuses everywhere else. What the projection does carry is the
company's own **contracted backlog**, which is the developer's order book: revenue
already sold and not yet delivered.

At origin *t*:

1. **Backlog at *t***, as the walk-forward projects it, seen only from that origin.
2. **Gross margin**, the median of the three fiscal years to *t* as reported —
   the company's own realised margin, never a forecast one.
3. **Delivery period**, fixed at **4 years** for every origin and every name: the
   contracted book converts to profit evenly over four years. Fixed, not fitted,
   and stated here.
4. **Tax** at the statutory 22.5%, fixed.
5. Discount those four years at the point-in-time cost of equity from the archive
   (`rf* + 1.00 × ERP`, beta fixed as before).
6. **No terminal value at all.** The order book is a finite claim; a perpetuity on
   it would reintroduce exactly the error being withdrawn.
7. Add nothing for land, options or recurring assets — every one would be a
   judgement, and their absence is a **stated downward bias** rather than a
   silence. This lens is a FLOOR on a developer, and the report says so.

## Fixed here, never fitted

- beta = 1.00 · delivery period = 4 years · tax = 22.5%
- gross margin = the median of the three reported years to the origin
- no terminal value, no land, no recurring assets
- an origin missing any input is **dropped**, never filled

## What would make this the wrong instrument

- If it does not resemble the as-delivered series once that record is long enough
  (the pre-registration's own falsifier, unchanged).
- If its level is read as a house fair value. It is a floor on one lens of one
  class and is not comparable to a delivered central.
- If it fails as the first did. Then the answer is **not** a third construction —
  it is that the walk-forward does not commit what a mechanical valuation needs,
  and that is the finding.
