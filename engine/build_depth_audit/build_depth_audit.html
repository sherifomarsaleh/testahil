Which valuations were built from the bottom up

Every one of the 90 stocks carrying a published fundamental fair value, read against SIGCM clause 2: revenue as volume × price, cost as cost-per-unit, to the finest sourced level, with the gap flagged wherever the disclosure stops. The verdict for each name comes out of its own delivered study — and, for the 21 studies that carry a code-built engine/<name>_study/ directory, out of its compute.py as well.

ADisclosed units15

Revenue = a disclosed physical unit x a price or rate; cost per unit; margins fall out as outputs.

A-Major legs5

The unit build carries the major legs; the rest sits at segment level because nothing finer is disclosed, and the study says so.

BDerived units7

Real unit economics, but the units are the preparer’s estimate, an index, or back-solved from disclosed totals.

CSegment level6

Each disclosed segment on its own driver. No unit economics; the gap is flagged.

DTop-down25

A group or segment revenue-growth path plus a margin assumption or glide.

EAsset / NAV marks20

Value comes from marking assets, stakes or segment earnings at multiples. No revenue build at all.

FBank driver build12

Balances x margin — a NIM, cost-of-risk and cost-to-income bridge.

What the pattern shows

Build depth tracks the study's vintage, almost perfectly

Bottom-up construction is not spread across the book. It arrived with the current protocol and has been applied to whatever has been rebuilt since — the August-2026 cohort runs 17 bottom-up against 2, the July-2026 cohort 5 against 53.

The code-built studies are where the unit builds live

Of the 21 stocks whose study carries an engine/<name>_study/ directory, 18 are bottom-up and 3 are not — MODON and SWDY stop at segment level on disclosure grounds, STC is top-down by an explicit gate decision. Of the 69 studies with no build directory, only 9 are bottom-up: the five Egyptian developers, plus SALIK, LULU, CLHO and DSCW.

Most of the non-bottom-up studies say so, and say why

This is the flag-the-gap rule working, not failing. STC, ADNOCGAS, AGTHIA, RMDA, SABIC, SWDY, MODON and CLHO each name the missing disclosure in the delivered document before falling back to a coarser driver. No study in the book manufactures a volume/price split its filings cannot support.

Tier B should not be read as tier A

Seven studies have the full shape of a unit build on units that are not disclosed. ELEC back-solves tonnage from LME copper and the exchange rate; RIYADHCABLE runs a tonnage index (FY2025 = 100) because the company publishes no tonnage; and the five Egyptian developers price every project off a unit mix per square metre that four of the five state outright is "the preparer's estimates … illustrative, not authoritative", calibrated so the model reproduces disclosed totals. ARCC is the cautionary precedent inside the house: three earlier editions back-solved cement tonnes from an assumed price and presented the resulting utilisation as corroboration — an accounting identity that reproduces audited revenue for any price. They were withdrawn once the disclosed volumes were read.

One live inconsistency, in the bank class

ADCB — the house bank reference — states that "all eight of ADCB's drivers are top-down, because the bank reports blended results … rather than the deposit-repricing betas, fee volumes or product unit-economics a bottom-up build would need". ADIB and DIB repeat that wording. But Al Rajhi, on a structurally identical NIM / cost-to-income / cost-of-risk bridge, calls the same construction "a legitimate bottom-up build rather than a manufactured one", and SNB says it forecasts net special commission income as "NIM × average earning assets rather than a top-down growth rate". The substance is the same; only the label differs. This audit applies the ADCB reading and places all 12 banks in tier F — worth reconciling in the protocol so the term means one thing across the book.
