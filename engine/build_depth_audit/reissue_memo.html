Re-issue the book, or wait?

You are right that the cost of capital is not sound across the book. Of 90 covered stocks, 7 carry a beta whose provenance the gate can actually attest, and the same 7 are the only ones that are also built bottom-up. The question is not whether to fix it. It is what to fix first, and what a re-issue has to be worth.

the recommendation

Don't re-issue all 90 now, and don't wait either. Neither is the real choice. The gating event in this protocol was never a date — it is re-publication. Your own rule already says no study may be re-issued or rolled forward on a composite beta; it simply isn't enforced in code for the 71 studies that carry no beta artefact at all.

So: close the gate this week so nothing new ships on a known-bad input, measure before you rebuild — the beta re-derivation is cheap and doesn't need the study rebuilt — then re-issue in materiality order, folding the cost-of-capital fix and the bottom-up rebuild into one pass per name. 63 of the 83 names that need a cost-of-capital pass also need a driver rebuild. Doing those as two waves wastes the expensive half twice.

A duplicate index file is quietly breaking provenance

ADXGENERAL.csv is byte-identical to FADGI.csv — the same series under a filename the resolver does not register. ADNOCDIST and ADNOCDRILL regressed against it, so their numbers are right and their provenance will not resolve. Delete the duplicate, repoint both records.

74 of 90 published fair values carry no date

fairAsof is set on 16 ticker entries. Every other published fair value sits on the live site with no statement of when the fundamental work was done or which method vintage produced it. Stamping all 90 is a data edit, not a rebuild, and it is the cheapest honest thing available while the queue runs.

The sequence, and the dates

Three dates, not one — because the three jobs have different costs and only the first is urgent.

now · this week · hours, not days

Close the gate and stamp the pages

Register DFMGI, delete the ADXGENERAL duplicate, extend assert_beta_provenance() to the roll-forward path so a study with no beta artefact cannot be re-published, and stamp fairAsof plus a method-vintage note on all 90 entries. Nothing here touches a delivered document.

by the next metronome · 1 Sep 2026

Measure the damage before rebuilding anything

own_stock_beta() is cheap and needs no study rebuild. Run it for all 90 against their registered index, recompute Ke and WACC under v2, and re-run only the discounting step to get each study's implied move. That yields a materiality-ranked queue instead of an assumption about one. Publish it the way the beta tracker was published.

before the wave starts

Freeze the standard you are re-issuing to

This is the step your own question implies. If the method is still improving, a name re-issued in September gets re-issued again in November, and you will have paid twice for one study. Write the target down as a versioned standard — v2 WACC, own_stock_beta() against the registered index, ground-up to the finest sourced level, terminal growth reconciled — and re-issue to it, so a finished study is finished.

1 Oct – 31 Dec 2026

Re-issue in materiality order, one pass per name

Every name whose centre moves more than 5% — the threshold the pipeline already uses for the cone — re-issued by year end. Everything below that folds into its own next scheduled roll-forward rather than earning a special pass. And each name gets one rebuild covering both the cost of capital and the driver build, never two.

Why not simply do it all now

71 of the 90 studies have no build directory. Re-issuing them is not an update — it is reconstructing the model first, then re-issuing. That is rewriting the book, and compressing it into a single wave is precisely how the composite-beta defect propagated in the first place: each study copied the last, including the mistake, and the CLHO precedent that established the procedure carried it for every name after it. A queue that runs slowly and is enforced beats a wave that runs fast and is copied.

Why not wait, either

Because a beta correction is not cosmetic, and you already have the proof inside the house. FERTIGLB, re-derived on 10 Aug 2026:

One thing to decide that this memo cannot

ARCC and SCEM lose their tier-1 beta outright against the real EGX30 — R² of 0.047 and 0.025, both below the 5% floor, and the Dimson correction rescues neither. Under the hierarchy they fall to a same-country peer beta or to β = 1.0 with the failed diagnostics shown. Which tier they land on changes their published range, and it is a judgement call about the EGX peer set, not an arithmetic one. Worth settling before the wave rather than inside it, because whatever you decide there becomes the precedent for every thin EGX name behind them.
