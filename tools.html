Tools

Every figure on this page is read off the published studies — the same 50,000-path simulation each study froze on its run date; nothing is re-simulated here. "Odds" are the share of those paths on each side of a level. The four buckets split the paths at ±10% of today’s price — a "big win" is a finish more than 10% up, a "big loss" more than 10% down, the two "small" buckets sit between — and always add to 100%. The screener asks one question per name: can the study’s central fair value be reached inside the 3-month cone? None of this is advice — it’s the same math, arranged for a decision you’ve already framed. Full method →

Rank the whole coverage by reachability — can the study’s central fair value be reached in 3 months, given how much the name actually moves? Big-win odds is the share of the simulated 3-month paths that finish more than 10% above the price the cone was struck at at the check date.

Two prices, two jobs. The gap to fair value is measured against the latest price we hold, and every row prints the date of that price — they are entered by hand, so some are days old and some are weeks. The odds columns stay on the price each cone was struck at, because that is the anchor the simulation was built on. Where the two differ, the date beside the price is the one to read.

A big gap to fair value is not a recommendation — it can mean opportunity or a valuation that needs its next study cycle. Reachability only says whether the cone’s own 3-month spread covers the study’s central value ("in reach" = inside the middle half of paths, "stretch" = inside the 90% band, "out of reach" = beyond it). "Odds up" is the share of paths finishing above today’s price; "Big-win odds" is the share finishing more than 10% above it — the same ≥ +10% bucket the Odds tab shows. Rankings refresh whenever the studies do, and whenever a fresher price is entered — each row states the date of the price it was ranked on.

How the funnel works

Seven conditions applied to every covered name, ordered so the ones with a track record are applied before the ones without. Exposure is considered first; individual names last. The prose is the procedure and does not move; every figure marked LIVE is computed from the published data each time this page loads — never typed.

What this is not. A name meeting every condition is a description of what the published figures say about it against a stated rule — not a recommendation, not a rating, and not a suggestion to buy or sell anything. The conditions are filters over published data; they carry no view on whether any name is a good investment, and the largest of them has no track record at all. Zone 1 — set the exposure

GATE 0

Exposure is considered before any individual name

proven

The procedure settles total equity exposure first, against the book's own measured worst case, before any name is looked at. On this record, being in the market at a size that survives its own drawdowns accounted for more than every selection condition below put together.

RULE Exposure judged against the median published 3-month p5, not against a target return.

Zone 2 — narrow the universe

GATE 1

Keep only what can be read at all

prerequisite

Drop a name only where an input does not exist: no fair value, no computable momentum, no band record. Nothing is dropped for being old or for being work in progress. Prices are entered by hand on a monthly rhythm and a study rebuild takes days, so both are printed on the row and neither ends the name — a tool that dead-ends everything it has touched answers nothing on the day you ask it.

RULE Stop only on a missing input. Age and rebuild-in-progress are flags.

GATE 2

Cap each name from its own band record

proven

Set the maximum before forming a view, so the risk budget cannot be argued backwards from conviction. A name whose own 90% band has historically under-covered gets a smaller cap than one whose bands held.

RULE A name flagged "bands ran narrow" sizes down. Caps, cuts nothing.

GATE 3

Order by the gap — and read the two sides differently

ungraded

A fair value well above the price is the ordinary shape of a name the fundamental lens reads as cheap, and the condition records that. A fair value well below the price is a high-prior-of-defect region — historically our own model missing something rather than a short — so those go to an investigation queue, never to a position.

RULE Condition met at a gap above +10% against the latest price held. Below −10% the study is audited rather than acted on.

FLAG A name whose study is being rebuilt is evaluated on the published fair value — the only number a reader can act on — and its row says so.

GATE 4

Use momentum as a veto, not a ranking

thin

The lean is right about 53 times in 100 against a base rate near 51 — enough to refuse a name, not enough to order a list. It is not used to rank: the cross-sectional version is refuted, and in a tape where most names print up, the sign is nearly free.

RULE A strong negative call fails the condition. It only ever removes; it never supports a position.

GATE 5

All three lenses must point the same way

ungraded

The fundamental, momentum and technical reads are built independently, so agreement is evidence rather than an echo — but tested as a stock picker it earned nothing. Its job is timing rather than selection: two of three is recorded as disagreement, not as a weaker version of agreement.

RULE Condition met only where all three lenses point the same way. It speaks to when, never to which.

Zone 3 — act, then loop

GATE 6

Enter and exit off the touch ladder

proven

The bands are calibrated, so a published level is a probability rather than a chart line. The procedure reads entries and exits off that ladder and controls risk by size rather than by stops — on this record a stop tighter than the typical drawdown fires on ordinary movement.

RULE Nothing above the name's own p95. Use size, not stops.

GATE 7

Rebalance quarterly and grade every decision

proven

Monthly rebalancing measured net-negative: the round trip is larger than the signal. Each quarter the gap and direction call are recorded against what actually happened — that record is the only thing that can move the gap condition from hypothesis to evidence.

RULE Quarterly or not at all. Re-run Gates 1–5 on the current state each quarter.

Tested, and it did not work

  • Monthly rebalancing. Every one-month construction is net-negative after the round trip. The commission is larger than the signal.
  • Sizing by cone width. Inverse-volatility weighting did not beat equal weight; the names co-move too hard for it to bite.
  • Trading the centre of the cone. The centre is the carry — the same for every name. Reading it as a target is reading the risk-free rate as a forecast.
  • Momentum as a ranking. Sign-unstable across samples. It survives as a veto and nothing more.
  • Lens agreement as a stock picker. Zero excess over the panel. It is a timing gate, not a selector.

What actually earns the money

Gates 0, 2, 6 and 7 do most of the work and all of them are proven — exposure at a survivable size, a cap from each name's own record, execution off the ladder, and a quarterly clock. None of them selects a name. Gate 4 is a small measured filter. Gates 3 and 5 are where the upside lives and neither has a graded record, so they narrow and time, and never support a size that Gates 0 and 2 had not already allowed.
