In plain terms: Agthia trades about 25% below our weighted central fair value — cheap on every fundamental lens, though the tape is soft and sits below all four moving averages. It is Abu Dhabi’s food-security champion: Al Ain water, Grand Mills flour and Agrivita feed, an Egypt-centred snacking portfolio (Abu Auf, BMB, Al Foah) and a protein arm (Nabil, Atyab) ramping a new Saudi plant, with ADQ as the majority owner. Reported FY2025 looked dreadful — EPS 0.103, EBITDA −32% — but AED 143mn of that is ring-fenced provisions, not lost operating power: underlying EBITDA held a 12.5% margin and Q1 2026 already turned (revenue +3.3%, profit +12.5%) while paying a 6.3% dividend yield. On the lenses the consolidated DCF reads AED 4.60, the segment sum-of-the-parts 4.24, conservative multiples 3.83 and normalized earnings 4.51 — a weighted central near 4.37. The bull case is the Snacking margin rebuilding (green coffee and the Egyptian pound are the levers) and the KSA plant filling; the cautious case (~3.05) is a third year of provisions or an Egypt shock.
