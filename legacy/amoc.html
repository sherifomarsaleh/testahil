Trend, momentum and key levels — what is the price doing now?

What the chart says

In plain terms: at today’s 21.5% cost of capital the discounted cash flows are worth about EGP 3.8bn while the net debt against them is about EGP 9.8bn — so on the base case the equity is worth nothing, and is floored at zero only because a shareholder cannot owe more than the stake. What EGP 2.19 buys is an option on three things going right together: the receivables collecting, conversion margins recovering from the 1Q26 trough, and the CBE resuming its easing cycle. That’s the bet.

50,000 simulated futures — near-term price paths, independent of the fundamental value.

The full 3-month path, from the same distribution as the odds above. Static — nothing here is adjustable, unlike the levers under Fundamental.

Our weighted fundamental range is EGP 4.09–8.52 and the widest single lens reaches 9.88 — so the whole simulated price cone sits ABOVE the weighted fair-value range, not around it. Read that gap for what it is: the simulation prices where the tape can travel over three months, the study prices what the business is worth, and the two answer different questions. Nothing in the drift comes from the valuation. The point at which we would rebuild the study is a sustained fundamental move above EGP 9.88 — above the top of every lens — which on the study’s own grids means a permanent gross margin near 12.2%, against a best-ever filed quarter of 10.19%.

What drives the odds

One Monte Carlo model, 50,000 paths, driven by the outside forces relevant to a hard-currency industrial exporter listed in a soft-currency market: the LME copper price, the EGP/USD path, the CBE policy corridor and the pace of easing, Egyptian inflation, Gulf and North African grid and infrastructure spending, the order book and its conversion rate, interconnector and data-centre demand for cable and transformers, energy and freight costs, convertibility and repatriation risk on the foreign earnings, and the free float — 11.6% of the shares, with 68% held by the family and 20% by Electra, which is what makes the tape thin. How to read the odds bar

It's a picture of where the price could be — not a single guess. Here's each part:

The thick teal band is where the price lands about half the time. The two numbers above it are the edges of that "middle half."
The thin pale band is almost the whole range — about 9 times out of 10 the price stays inside it. The far numbers are the rare low and rare high.
The upright line is the middle outcome — half the simulations land above it, half below.
The gold dot is the latest price. If most of the bar sits to its right, the odds lean up; to its left, they lean down.

The sentence above the bar says the same thing in words. These are model estimates from 50,000 simulations — useful odds, never guarantees.
