What the chart says

In plain terms: Modon has already sold AED 65.4 billion of property it has not yet built or handed over — the contracted backlog, 95% of it development sales, converting into revenue on a schedule this study models year by year. Capitalise that conversion plus a continuing sales machine and the cash-flow lens says AED 3.54 a share; assume the company never sells another dirham after the current book and the wind-down says AED 2.64. The earnings lenses sit lower still (AED 1.46–2.20). The weighted central is AED 2.50, about 12% BELOW the market price of AED 2.83. That is a reversal, and it is worth saying why. Two earlier editions read this stock as roughly 19% cheap. The difference is not the business — not one revenue line, margin or backlog figure has changed. It is that those editions could not obtain the exchange's official index and measured how much the share moves with the market against a basket built from our own coverage, flagged both times as a stand-in. The official index has since been obtained, and measured properly — including the correction a stock with 84.75% of its shares in one holder requires — Modon is markedly more sensitive to the market than the stand-in implied. The required return rises from 9.1% to 12.6%, and a higher required return makes the same future cash worth less today. The stand-in had been flattering the company. The bet is still conversion: receivables at 440 days of revenue have to actually collect, and the H1-2026 release — AED 26 billion of sales in six months — is the evidence the machine is running.

Lens 3 of 3Monte Carlo — where could the price go?

50,000 simulated futures — near-term price paths, independent of the fundamental value.

The full 3-month path, from the same distribution as the odds above. Static — nothing here is adjustable, unlike the levers under Fundamental.

Our weighted fair value of AED 2.50 sits BELOW the middle of the simulated three-month cone (AED 2.86), between its 5th and 25th percentiles. Read that gap for what it is: the simulation prices where the price can drift on its own volatility over three months, the study prices what the business is worth as the backlog converts. The two are different objects on different clocks and are never blended — and note the cone is unchanged from the previous edition, because the forecast that produced it did not change. Only the fair value moved. A sustained beta reading near the bottom of its range (1.09), or a backlog and collection surprise in the next results release, is where we’d rebuild the study.

What drives the odds

One Monte Carlo model, 50,000 paths, driven by the outside forces relevant to an Abu Dhabi master developer: off-plan sales pace and the mortgage-affordability effect of the EIBOR path, the backlog conversion and handover schedule, oil price and the UAE fiscal impulse behind it, population and visa-reform inflows into Abu Dhabi housing, construction-cost inflation on the unrecognised backlog, the pipeline flowing from the IHC ecosystem it sits inside, the Ras El Hekma exposure in Egypt with its own currency risk, hospitality seasonality, and a free float thin enough to move on modest volume. How to read the odds bar

It's a picture of where the price could be — not a single guess. Here's each part:

The thick teal band is where the price lands about half the time. The two numbers above it are the edges of that "middle half."
The thin pale band is almost the whole range — about 9 times out of 10 the price stays inside it. The far numbers are the rare low and rare high.
The upright line is the middle outcome — half the simulations land above it, half below.
The gold dot is the latest price. If most of the bar sits to its right, the odds lean up; to its left, they lean down.

The sentence above the bar says the same thing in words. These are model estimates from 50,000 simulations — useful odds, never guarantees.
