The selection funnel

Seven gates for putting money into an Egyptian name, ordered so the decisions that have a track record run before the ones that do not. Size is set first; names are picked last.

Every gate below carries the strength of the evidence behind it, and the numbers on this page are computed from the published data each time it loads — never typed. Run it over the whole book →

What the coloured rail means

ProvenMeasured on our own resolved record. Does most of the work.
ThinReal but small. Sized as a filter, never as a thesis.
UngradedLargest claimed edge, no track record yet. Selects candidates only.
PrerequisiteNot an edge. Without it the gates downstream read noise.

Zone 1 — set the exposure

GATE 0

Size the whole position before naming anything

proven

Choose total equity weight so the book's own worst case is one you can hold through. If it is not, cut the weight — never the discipline below. Being in the market at a survivable size has been worth more than every selection rule combined.

RULE Weight such that the median published 3-month p5 does not force a sale.

Zone 2 — narrow the universe

GATE 1

Keep only what can be read at all

prerequisite

Drop a name only where an input does not exist: no fair value, no computable momentum, no band record. Nothing is dropped for being old or for being work in progress. Prices are entered by hand on a monthly rhythm and a study rebuild takes days, so both are printed on the row and neither ends the name — a tool that dead-ends everything it has touched answers nothing on the day you ask it.

RULE Stop only on a missing input. Age and rebuild-in-progress are flags.

GATE 2

Cap each name from its own band record

proven

Set the maximum before forming a view, so the risk budget cannot be argued backwards from conviction. A name whose own 90% band has historically under-covered gets a smaller cap than one whose bands held.

RULE A name flagged "bands ran narrow" sizes down. Caps, cuts nothing.

GATE 3

Rank on the gap — and read the two sides differently

ungraded

A fair value well above the price is the ordinary shape of finding something cheap: that is a candidate. A fair value well below the price is a high-prior-of-defect region — historically our own model missing something rather than a short — so those go to an investigation queue, never to a position.

RULE Candidate = gap above +10% against the latest price held. Below −10% → audit, do not trade.

FLAG A name whose study is being rebuilt is evaluated on the published fair value — the only number a reader can act on — and its row says so.

GATE 4

Use momentum as a veto, not a ranking

thin

The lean is right about 53 times in 100 against a base rate near 51 — enough to refuse a name, not enough to order a list. It is not used to rank: the cross-sectional version is refuted, and in a tape where most names print up, the sign is nearly free.

RULE Reject a strong negative call. It vetoes; it never justifies a size.

GATE 5

Require all three lenses before deploying

ungraded

The fundamental, momentum and technical reads are built independently, so agreement is evidence rather than an echo — but tested as a stock picker it earned nothing. Its job is timing: it stops you buying early. Two of three is a watch-list entry, not a smaller position.

RULE All three constructive → deploy. Otherwise wait. Decides when, never which.

Zone 3 — act, then loop

GATE 6

Enter and exit off the touch ladder

proven

The bands are calibrated, so a level is a probability rather than a chart line. Stagger the entry below the price, stage the exits above it, and control risk with size — a tight stop on an EGX name is noise dressed as prudence.

RULE Nothing above the name's own p95. Use size, not stops.

GATE 7

Rebalance quarterly and grade every decision

proven

Monthly rebalancing is net-negative: the round trip is larger than the signal. Record the gap and the direction call acted on, and what happened — that record is the only thing that can move Gate 3 from hypothesis to edge.

RULE Quarterly or not at all. Re-run Gates 1–5 on the current state each quarter.

Tested, and it did not work

  • Monthly rebalancing. Every one-month construction is net-negative after the round trip. The commission is larger than the signal.
  • Sizing by cone width. Inverse-volatility weighting did not beat equal weight; the names co-move too hard for it to bite.
  • Trading the centre of the cone. The centre is the carry — the same for every name. Reading it as a target is reading the risk-free rate as a forecast.
  • Momentum as a ranking. Sign-unstable across samples. It survives as a veto and nothing more.
  • Lens agreement as a stock picker. Zero excess over the panel. It is a timing gate, not a selector.

What actually earns the money

Gates 0, 2, 6 and 7 do most of the work and all of them are proven — exposure at a survivable size, a cap from each name's own record, execution off the ladder, and a quarterly clock. None of them picks a stock. Gate 4 is a small measured filter. Gates 3 and 5 are where the upside lives and neither has a graded record, so they select candidates and set timing, and never justify a size that Gates 0 and 2 had not already allowed.

Nothing here is a rating or a price target. The procedure produces fair-value ranges, distributions, and a decision about size.
