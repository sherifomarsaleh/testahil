In plain terms: the business is excellent and the disagreement is about the price of Egyptian money. Discounted at a 26.6% pound cost of capital the shares are worth about EGP 70 against a 105 price; discount the hard-currency half of the cash flows at a dollar rate and the same model prints 86. What 105.20 buys is the market applying something even more generous than the dollar view — a bet that convertibility never binds and Egyptian easing arrives on schedule. That’s the gap.

What drives the odds

One Monte Carlo model, 50,000 paths, driven by the outside forces relevant to a hard-currency industrial exporter listed in a soft-currency market: the LME copper price, the EGP/USD path, the CBE policy corridor and the pace of easing, Egyptian inflation, Gulf and North African grid and infrastructure spending, the order book and its conversion rate, interconnector and data-centre demand for cable and transformers, energy and freight costs, convertibility and repatriation risk on the foreign earnings, and the free float — 11.6% of the shares, with 68% held by the family and 20% by Electra, which is what makes the tape thin.
