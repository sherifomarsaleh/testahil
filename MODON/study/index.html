Lens 2 · Technical — what is the chart doing?

Bear case:

In plain terms: Modon has already sold AED 65.4 billion of property it has not yet built or handed over — the contracted backlog, 95% of it development sales, converting into revenue on a schedule this study models year by year. Capitalise that conversion plus a continuing sales machine and the cash-flow lens says AED 3.54 a share; assume the company never sells another dirham after the current book and the wind-down says AED 2.64. The earnings lenses sit lower still (AED 1.46–2.20). The weighted central is AED 2.50, about 12% BELOW the market price of AED 2.83. That is a reversal, and it is worth saying why. Two earlier editions read this stock as roughly 19% cheap. The difference is not the business — not one revenue line, margin or backlog figure has changed. It is that those editions could not obtain the exchange's official index and measured how much the share moves with the market against a basket built from our own coverage, flagged both times as a stand-in. The official index has since been obtained, and measured properly — including the correction a stock with 84.75% of its shares in one holder requires — Modon is markedly more sensitive to the market than the stand-in implied. The required return rises from 9.1% to 12.6%, and a higher required return makes the same future cash worth less today. The stand-in had been flattering the company. The bet is still conversion: receivables at 440 days of revenue have to actually collect, and the H1-2026 release — AED 26 billion of sales in six months — is the evidence the machine is running.
